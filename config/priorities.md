# Editorial Priorities and Significance Ranking

## Core question

A story belongs in the brief when it changes the audience's understanding of international power, conflict, diplomacy, economic coercion, alliance behavior, or political stability.

Publication recency alone is not significance.

## Candidate scoring

Score each serious candidate:

| Dimension | Range | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic consequence | 0-5 | Potential to alter policy, conflict, alliances, economic security, or state behavior |
| Escalation / de-escalation potential | 0-5 | Meaningful change in probability or severity of confrontation |
| US foreign-policy relevance | 0-4 | Direct implications for US policy, forces, alliances, trade, or domestic debate |
| Novelty | 0-3 | New information rather than repetition of known conditions |
| Audience / discussion value | 0-3 | Useful substantive question for a political livestream |
| Uncertainty / contested facts | 0-3 | Important unresolved facts or competing interpretations |

Use this rough priority score as a sorting aid:

```text
3 * strategic_consequence
+ 2 * escalation_or_deescalation
+ 2 * us_relevance
+ novelty
+ audience_value
```

Do not subtract for uncertainty. High uncertainty can increase editorial importance when the uncertainty itself matters.

The numerical score is a triage aid, not an excuse to stop thinking.

## High-priority categories

- Major military escalation or credible de-escalation
- Territorial or battlefield changes with strategic consequences
- New military commitments, deployments, or alliance guarantees
- Changes in nuclear posture, proliferation, or arms control
- Significant sanctions, export controls, tariffs, or economic coercion
- Major diplomatic negotiations, breakdowns, treaties, or recognition decisions
- Elections or leadership changes that affect foreign policy
- Coups, state instability, or regime-threatening crises
- Strategic energy developments
- Significant US foreign-policy changes
- Major China-Taiwan, Russia-Ukraine, NATO, Middle East, Korean Peninsula, India-Pakistan, or other interstate-security developments
- International legal decisions with real geopolitical consequences

## Usually low priority

- Routine official statements that do not change policy
- Recycled threats without new capability or intent evidence
- Daily casualty or strike counts without strategic meaning
- Minor bilateral visits
- Purely domestic political drama with no material foreign-policy consequence
- Viral claims whose only significance is that they went viral

## Editorial anti-bias rules

- No regional quotas.
- No ideological quota.
- No obligation to include a story because major US media led with it.
- No obligation to exclude a story because it is under-covered.
- Separate audience interest from strategic importance.
- Do not turn uncertainty into false equivalence.
