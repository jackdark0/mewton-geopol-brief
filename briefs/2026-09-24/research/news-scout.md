## Candidate scan

### 1. U.S.-Iran diplomacy continued, but the parties publicly remained far apart

- **What changed:** The first indirect talks of the current round took place on September 22. By September 23, a senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran was reviewing Washington's response but that the sides remained far apart. Iran prioritized relief from the U.S. blockade of its ports and reopening the Strait of Hormuz; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the U.S. priority remained preventing an Iranian nuclear weapon and keeping shipping lanes open.
- **Why it may matter:** The channel survived its first contact, but the disagreement is now clearer. The bargaining sequence still joins nuclear limits, port access, shipping and military pressure.
- **Caveat:** Iran's detailed terms come from one unnamed official. Rubio's claim that Iran fired on ships that morning was not independently established in the inspected sources. There is no ceasefire, shipping agreement or nuclear framework.
- **Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Iran%2C%2BUS%2Bstill%2Bfar%2Bapart%2Bin%2Bpeace%2Btalks%2C%2BIranian%2Bofficial%2Bsays%3B%2BPezeshkian%2Bvows%2Bno%2Bsurrender/27093045.html); [Rubio press availability](https://pubt.io/view/F4387A41B4220FB296F1F65B59627CEE7BE0B141); [UN summary of Pezeshkian's address](https://www.un.org/fr/le-pr%C3%A9sident-iranien-refuse-de-%C2%AB%C2%A0courber-l%C3%A9chine%C2%A0%C2%BB-face-%C3%A0-trump-mais-reste-ouvert-au-dialogue)

### 2. Fighting in northern Ethiopia escalated into an offensive and airport seizures

- **What changed:** Reuters reported fighting across Tigray, Afar and Amhara and a major offensive by Tigrayan forces. The African Union said Tigrayan forces had seized and occupied the Mekelle, Axum and Shire airports, detained federal personnel and disrupted civilian aviation.
- **Why it may matter:** The fighting threatens the 2022 Pretoria peace framework and could reopen a multi-front war involving federal forces, neighboring regions and possibly Eritrea.
- **Caveat:** The scale, initiator and territorial picture remain contested. The federal government and Tigrayan authorities blame each other. Claims of Eritrean support are denied and not independently established here.
- **Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/09/23/tigrayan-leaders-say-new-war-with-ethiopian-government-has-begun-as-fighting-erupts); [African Union](https://www.au.int/en/pressreleases/20260923/chairperson-calls-restraint-and-immediate-de-escalation-northern-ethiopia); [Reuters explainer](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/why-new-war-in-ethiopia-s-tigray-threatens-to-trigger-wider-conflict-ce785ad9d181f520)

### 3. The Board of Peace released a $2.45 billion Gaza recovery blueprint

- **What changed:** The U.S.-backed Board of Peace presented a six-month plan with 66 projects covering border access, water, electricity, solar kits, rubble and tunnel work. The proposed first-phase cost is $2.45 billion.
- **Why it may matter:** The plan is the most concrete near-term recovery menu yet attached to the wider Gaza governance and security sequence.
- **Caveat:** The money is not fully secured, implementation has no fixed calendar, and Israel and Hamas still dispute core conditions. A proposal is not reconstruction on the ground.
- **Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-s-board-of-peace-to-unveil-2-45-billion-gaza-recovery-plan-axios-reports-ce785ad9dd88f224); [AP](https://abcnews.com/US/wireStory/trumps-board-peace-proposes-245-billion-blueprint-begin-136685552); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/23/trump-board-of-peace-gaza-reconstruction-plan)

### 4. Kyiv offered a Black Sea truce while Moscow rejected a temporary pause

- **What changed:** President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine was ready for a maritime truce based on proposals from Egypt, India and Turkey. He also kept open a reciprocal pause in attacks on energy infrastructure. Russia's Foreign Ministry reiterated its commitment to its war objectives, while Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov rejected a temporary pause at the UN Security Council.
- **Why it may matter:** Kyiv has put two narrow, testable de-escalation options on the table before winter, but Moscow's public answer points away from a pause.
- **Caveat:** No text, start date, monitoring system or Russian assent exists. This is a diplomatic offer, not a ceasefire.
- **Source:** [Reuters](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/09/23/zelenskiy-says-ukraine-ready-for-maritime-truce-and-puts-onus-on-russia-to-respond)

### 5. Morocco's provisional election result produced a fragmented parliament

- **What changed:** Provisional results put the Authenticity and Modernity Party first with 97 of 395 seats, followed by the National Rally of Independents with 66, Istiqlal with 65 and the Justice and Development Party with 54. Official turnout was roughly 38 percent.
- **Why it may matter:** No party is close to a majority, so coalition bargaining will determine the next government under a monarchy that retains strategic authority.
- **Caveat:** These are provisional results. Two official pages reported turnout as 38.08 and 38.02 percent, so the safe formulation is “roughly 38 percent.”
- **Sources:** [Moroccan government](https://maroc.ma/fr/actualites/legislatives-2026-le-pam-en-tete-avec-97-sieges-selon-les-resultats-provisoires); [Reuters](https://www.fidelity.com/news/article/default/202609232356RTRSNEWSCOMBINED_KBN3VA0A4-OUSWD_1); [AP election context](https://apnews.com/article/046460d3e478cce506e77be052dbaa36)

### 6. Ukraine and Australia signed a long-term support framework

- **What changed:** The two governments signed a political agreement covering security assistance, training, intelligence, sanctions, cyber cooperation, energy and possible joint production of air-defense systems, missiles and drones.
- **Why it may matter:** The framework broadens Ukraine's long-term defense-industrial relationships beyond Europe and the United States.
- **Caveat:** The document is a political commitment, explicitly subject to domestic law and available resources, and is not a defense guarantee or treaty registered under Article 102 of the UN Charter.
- **Source:** [Ukrainian presidency and agreement text](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/ugoda-pro-dovgostrokovu-pidtrimku-j-spivrobitnictvo-mizh-ukr-106529)

### 7. The ICC convicted a former Séléka commander from the Central African Republic

- **What changed:** The International Criminal Court found Mahamat Said Abdel Kani guilty beyond reasonable doubt of four crimes against humanity: imprisonment or severe deprivation of liberty, torture, persecution and other inhumane acts.
- **Why it may matter:** It is the court's first conviction of a former Séléka leader and preserves individual accountability for a conflict whose political settlement remains incomplete.
- **Caveat:** Several war-crime charges were dismissed because the chamber found the relevant armed conflict had ended at the time of those acts. Sentencing will come later.
- **Sources:** [ICC](https://icccpi-web.icccpi-prod.uniccloud.org/fr/news/mahamat-said-abdel-kani-declare-coupable-de-crimes-contre-lhumanite-commis-en-republique); [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/International%2BCriminal%2BCourt%2Bconvicts%2BCentral%2BAfrican%2Bmilitia%2Bleader/27092970.html)

### 8. Senate Democrats introduced sanctions legislation over Israel's E1 settlements

- **What changed:** More than 10 Democratic senators backed a bill to sanction people facilitating the E1 settlement project in the occupied West Bank.
- **Why it may matter:** The proposal puts a concrete penalty behind congressional opposition to a project critics say would divide Palestinian territory.
- **Caveat:** It is proposed legislation, not policy. The House was out of session and Reuters assessed passage before the midterms as unlikely.
- **Source:** [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/US%2BSenate%2BDemocrats%2Bseek%2Bsanctions%2Bover%2BIsraeli%2Bsettlements%2C%2Bbill%2Bsays/27092938.html)

### 9. Germany prepared a military encryption upgrade worth up to €1.7 billion

- **What changed:** A parliamentary budget committee was set to consider a framework for encrypted military data equipment worth up to €1.7 billion, according to documents seen by Reuters.
- **Why it may matter:** Secure communications are a practical constraint on German rearmament and NATO interoperability.
- **Caveat:** The report described an expected committee approval, not a completed contract award. The first order was projected at about €100 million next year.
- **Source:** [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Germany%2Bto%2Bupgrade%2Bmilitary%2Bdata%2Bencryption%2Bfor%2Bup%2Bto%2B%E2%82%AC1.7%2Bbillion/27093019.html)

- **Source:** [African Union](https://au.int/en/pressreleases/20260923/auc-chairperson-co-chaired-us-meeting-quad-and-quintet-sudan)
