## Claim-by-claim stress test

### 1. Britain expands support for Saudi Arabia

**Strongest defensible claim:** Britain announced Typhoons for defence of Saudi territory and infrastructure, plus A400M transport, advisers and a 90-day refuelling extension that can support Saudi defensive operations including attacks on Houthi targets in Yemen.

**Challenges**

- “Britain expands” describes a policy announcement, not proven arrival or sorties. Use future or announced tense until operational confirmation.
- Do not apply the Typhoon “defensive only” limitation to the whole package. Refuelling has an explicitly wider functional scope.
- “Support for strikes” is entailed; “participation in strikes,” “war entry,” or “combat mission in Yemen” is not.
- The UK government's description is authoritative for its own orders but not independent evidence that every operation will remain within those limits.
- Strategic effect depends on sortie demand, basing, tanker availability and Saudi integration, none of which is public.

**Required edit discipline:** place the two mission boundaries in the same paragraph; label implementation as a watch item.

**Residual confidence:** high on policy, medium on likely enabling value, low on realized battlefield effect.

### 2. Ukraine warning restoration, Starshield restrictions and PURL delays

**Strongest defensible claim:** Zelensky said warning capability was restored after saying Starlink/Starshield needed restoration; anonymous-source reporting linked a Musk decision to restrictions; Rubio acknowledged possible interceptor non-delivery while disputing political intent.

**Challenges**

- “Restored” is a presidential account, not a published uptime test, coverage map or technical audit.
- The CBS report is a source-mediated account. It does not reveal an order, implementation chain, duration or full scope.
- Do not use a post-cutoff Wall Street Journal publication as pre-cutoff confirmation. CBS's pre-cutoff update is admissible; its reference to earlier reporting does not import later evidence.
- PURL interceptor delivery, missile-warning sharing and Starshield access are separate systems. Similar timing does not prove a common decision.
- Rubio's technical explanation and denial of punishment are claims by an interested official, not a completed investigation.
- “Musk switched it off” is too categorical. The evidence supports involvement in a decision that reportedly led to restrictions, with implementation unresolved.

**Alternative explanation:** technical routing, contractual limits, separate agency decisions or mixed technical-policy causes could explain some of the observed gaps.

**Required edit discipline:** split verified events into separate sentences; say the command chain is unresolved; never assert a retaliation order.

**Residual confidence:** high on reported disruption and reported restoration; medium on Musk-linked decision reporting; low on a unified punitive policy.

### 5. Abyei mass-casualty strike

**Strongest defensible claim:** the UN verified at least 28 civilian deaths while local authorities reported 153; an anonymous Sudanese officer acknowledged an army strike but disputed the target and victim account.

**Challenges**

- The UN number is a verified floor, not a competing final estimate. The local number is a preliminary authority claim, not a verified total.
- Never average 28 and 153 or present a range as though both endpoints use the same method.
- The anonymous officer's admission supports army involvement but remains source-mediated and may refer to the same or part of the event sequence.
- “Market strike” and “fuel logistics strike” may describe the same physical location from different perspectives. Intent remains unproved.
- Civilian identity, RSF presence, missing persons and multiple strike locations could all affect the final composition.

**Required edit discipline:** put provenance beside each number; say “at least 28 verified by the UN” and “153 reported by local authorities”; leave target and final toll open.

**Residual confidence:** high on mass civilian harm; medium on Sudanese strike involvement; low on final toll and intended target.
