## Claim: C-01 — Elon Musk initiated the satellite-service restriction affecting Ukrainian military operations last week

- **Origin / principal promoters:** WSJ reported the initiating decision on October 20 using unnamed sources. Attribution confidence: high for the reported allegation, lower for its factual truth. Presidential authorization, foreknowledge and particular deaths are separate propositions.
- **Why it matters:** Responsibility determines whether this was a corporate decision, a government direction, a contract dispute, or some combination. Those mechanisms imply different remedies for Ukraine's dependence on the service.
- **Evidence offered for it:** WSJ attributes initiation to Musk. S-02 supplies another newsroom's sourcing. The Ukrainian presidency's October 20 call readout says the Starlink/Starshield situation required restoration; that supports a continuing service problem, not who ordered it.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No authenticated order, contract instruction, engineering change record, or named decision witness was inspected. Musk's earlier public position assigned limits to US authorities. Claims of prior presidential consultation and a unilateral corporate decision are distinct; neither automatically proves the other false. Restrictions on selected military connectivity do not establish a complete civilian Starlink shutdown. Neither a restriction nor anger over refinery strikes independently establishes motive or a death count.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial. Two newsrooms report sourcing, but undisclosed witnesses could overlap; neither published the underlying order.
- **Current assessment:** plausible as an attributed reporting finding; unclear as a conclusively documented command chain. Do not publish “proved Musk acted alone” or “proved Trump ordered it.”
- **Confidence:** medium. The reporting is specific; decision and implementation records remain unavailable.
- **Sources:**

Headline, subheading, bylines and two opening paragraphs inspected; remainder inaccessible. S-02's dated version establishes pre-cutoff existence of the core attribution; do not import unseen details.
  - **S-02 — Separate anonymous-source reporting:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/): attributes restriction to Musk; execution unclear. Updated October 20, 21:17 EDT, before cutoff. Full text inspected.

Retain the dated call readout, not the surrounding news feed.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the complete WSJ article and original Musk statement. Separate who proposed, authorized, implemented and knew about the restriction. Establish which terminals/service and speed metric were affected. Check whether CBS and WSJ evidence streams are independent. Preserve reported attribution if no order is public.

## Claim: C-02 — The delayed PURL interceptor delivery resulted from a deliberate political instruction to pressure Ukraine

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Zelensky's complaint about political slowing of support, and reporting about US pressure over Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure. The specific motive inference is stronger than the delivery-delay proposition. Attribution confidence: high for the public complaint and Rubio's denial, not for a hidden directive.
- **Why it matters:** A missing authorization can reflect competing allocations or deliberate coercion. Treating every delay as punishment could distort interpretation of US policy; treating every delay as routine could conceal actual pressure.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters' October 20 report says Hegseth had not yet authorized the latest allied-purchased package and refers to earlier reporting that Washington threatened the PURL mechanism over refinery strikes. Rubio acknowledged that the described non-delivery could be the case.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Rubio attributed allocation pressure to global interceptor demand and denied political intent. No package schedule, signed hold, allocation comparison, or decision record links this particular delay to refinery pressure. A reported earlier threat does not establish implementation against this shipment.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for non-delivery/authorization problems; no inspected independent documentary corroboration of motive.
- **Current assessment:** unclear on political causation. Supported narrower wording: Rubio acknowledged possible interceptor non-delivery while denying a deliberate political directive.
- **Confidence:** medium on that narrower statement; low on motive.
- **Sources:**

## Claim: C-06 — On most recent nights, oil passing through Hormuz reached at least 70–80% of its pre-conflict level

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Rubio's October 20 Mumbai remarks, S-05. This is a quantitative claim about oil flow on unspecified nights, not about all-vessel counts. Attribution confidence: high for the mirrored speech; measurement confidence low.
- **Why it matters:** An apparent recovery can coexist with scarce tanker capacity and expensive freight. Mismatched denominators can manufacture either a recovery narrative or a contradiction.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The assertion itself; Bloomberg's October 20 report describes resilient regional oil exports based on preliminary Kpler figures. Neither supplies the exact nightly Hormuz series underlying Rubio's threshold.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** JMIC's October 18 report gives 254 total vessel transits for the week ending October 14 and says that count is approximately 70% below pre-conflict levels. That is a different unit, period and population. It cannot be subtracted from, averaged with, or used to falsify an oil-volume percentage. The underlying nightly volume series, baseline, coverage of dark/AIS-off movements, and cargo definitions are missing.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for continuing oil flows; no for the exact most-nights threshold.
- **Current assessment:** unclear pending matched measurement. Do not write “Hormuz is 80% open” from this claim.
- **Confidence:** low on the stated percentage; high that the cited vessel-count series is not a direct comparator.
- **Sources:**
  - **S-05**, above, energy discussion following the question about Ukrainian strikes. No independent data appendix accompanies the mirrored remarks.
  - **S-09**, above, p. 2 section 2B, provides a dated vessel-count comparator and exposes the denominator mismatch.
  - **S-13 — Bloomberg reporting, syndicated:** [gCaptain, engine-room attacks](https://gcaptain.com/iran-strikes-more-ship-engine-rooms-in-ramp-up-of-hormuz-attacks/), dated October 20; body inspected. Preliminary Kpler evidence concerns regional exports, not necessarily nightly Hormuz cargo volume. Exact revision history not exposed: retain as a discovery lead pending a version/underlying-data check, not as proof of the percentage.

Specify crude versus products, exports versus actual strait crossings, volume versus vessels, direction, and observation dates. Check whether shuttle and ship-to-ship movements are counted repeatedly.
