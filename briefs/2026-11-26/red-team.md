# Red-team review - 2026-11-26

## Finding: USTR ceilings are not expected effective rates

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “USTR turns 14 Section 301 cases into a negotiating calendar,” especially the supply-chain and bargaining discussion.
- **Problem:** The analysis correctly calls the tariffs proposals, but still risks making the headline ceilings feel like the likely outcome. Exclusions, offsets against other duties and possible negotiated rates could make the final burden materially smaller or differently distributed.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** USTR's own release describes ceilings, netting rules, annexes and negotiated adjustments that vary across economies.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The package may be a common legal framework for highly differentiated settlements, not a single broad tariff wall.
- **Required editorial action:** Put “proposed,” “up to” and the product/netting caveat in the first paragraph; do not aggregate the rates into one implied shock.

## Finding: Federal orders are being analyzed ahead of implementation evidence

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “Baghdad tests whether federal authority can become physical control.”
- **Problem:** The strategic mechanism is plausible, but every downstream effect depends on implementation that the ledger does not establish. Even the phrase “tests whether” can imply an active field operation rather than an order.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The inspected evidence documents directives and Kurdish rejection, not a federal handover, deployment or change in export control at a named site.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The order may be a bargaining and anti-smuggling signal designed to pressure Erbil without immediate physical enforcement.
- **Required editorial action:** Lead with the implementation gap, avoid “seize” or “takeover” except in attributed source language, and make the first watch item verified changes at named sites.

## Finding: Shared leak provenance limits apparent corroboration

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “The Rosstat leak changes the casualty floor.”
- **Problem:** Methodological convergence across newsrooms can be mistaken for independent confirmation of the leak. The analysis notes the shared corpus, but a compressed final brief could lose that caveat and overstate authenticity.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** BBC Russian and Mediazona collaborate on the named list; iStories received the same hacker-supplied files. Rosstat did not authenticate the dataset.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Several competent teams may be reproducing a similar result from a corrupted or selectively supplied common dataset. Their cross-checks reduce that risk but do not eliminate it.
- **Required editorial action:** State that the roughly 490,000 figure is a supported registered-record floor from a disputed leak; separate it typographically and verbally from Mediazona's roughly 700,000 model.

## Finding: “Locks in” can overstate Ukraine fund availability

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “EU locks in its existing 2027 Ukraine tranche while debating another loan.”
- **Problem:** “Locks in” sounds like unconditional cash on hand. The decision makes funds accessible under an existing framework; instalments, modalities, procurement rules and conditions still matter.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The implementing decision approves the strategy and amounts but refers to memoranda, instalments and conditional mechanisms.
- **Best competing interpretation:** This is a necessary legal planning step with high signaling value, not the same as disbursement or delivery.
- **Required editorial action:** Use “makes accessible” or “adopts the allocation,” and state that disbursement remains conditional.

## Finding: Oil-price causation is underdetermined

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “Ras Tanura demonstrates risk repricing before target effects are known.”
- **Problem:** The title and mechanism can be read as causal proof that the Ras Tanura claim moved Brent by 3.5%. The verified source also cited wider regional supply risks.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** A same-day market move and attack claim establish temporal association, not the share of the move attributable to the claim.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The claim was one input in a move already driven by broader regional risk or other supply expectations.
- **Required editorial action:** Say Brent rose “amid” the claim and wider supply concerns. Do not assign the full move to Ras Tanura.

## Finding: The Chinese pause does not establish supply relief

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “China's mineral-control pause preserves supply and leverage.”
- **Problem:** The legal pause preserves a channel for exports; it does not prove supply volumes will be sufficient. “Preserves supply” is too strong without licences or shipment data.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Only one US-specific clause is suspended; general dual-use controls remain, and the record contains no post-extension volume data.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Beijing has preserved discretion while offering political reassurance, with little immediate commercial change.
- **Required editorial action:** Use “preserves a supply channel” and make licences and shipment volumes the test.

## Finding: The rapid-fire airbase claim may be too granular

- **Severity:** minor
- **Targets:** Savasleyka rapid-fire recommendation.
- **Problem:** A single belligerent claim about five damaged aircraft can create more heat than signal unless later imagery corroborates it.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The only inspected chain traces to Ukraine's General Staff, which said its assessment was preliminary.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The strike occurred but the aircraft count and damage severity are inflated or temporary.
- **Required editorial action:** Attribute every element, say independent confirmation was absent at cutoff, or omit if space is tight.

## Finding: Golden Dragon scale does not resolve the Ream basing dispute

- **Severity:** minor
- **Targets:** Golden Dragon rapid-fire recommendation.
- **Problem:** “Largest edition” and location at Ream are operationally relevant but do not distinguish normal access from exclusive basing.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Cambodia denies exclusivity, and the inspected record includes other foreign naval access.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The exercise demonstrates a close defence relationship and familiarity with Ream, not a permanent Chinese base.
- **Required editorial action:** Preserve the distinction and use post-exercise presence and third-country access as indicators.

# Analysis that survived challenge

- The Section 301 action is a proposal and negotiating calendar, not an enacted tariff shock; USTR's primary materials establish that distinction.
- Baghdad's order matters because it links oil, borders and export authority, but physical implementation is the decisive next fact.
- The Russian registered-record floor and the modeled total answer different questions and must not be merged.
- The EU's adopted EUR45 billion 2027 allocation and the Commission's additional EUR45 billion proposal are separate instruments.
- Pre-cutoff evidence did not establish damage or reduced throughput at Ras Tanura.
- China's extension suspends one US-specific clause rather than all controls on the covered materials.

# Highest-risk unresolved issue

The authenticity and completeness of the leaked Rosstat-linked dataset remain the day's highest-impact evidentiary risk. Cross-checks and independent filtering support a very large registered-death floor, but all published estimates reviewed here ultimately depend on a common hacker-supplied corpus that Rosstat disputes.
