## C-01 — Claim: Ukraine's satellite service was fully restored by October 23

- **Origin / principal promoters:** President Trump said restoration had occurred; Reuters reported his October 23 offer to address any continuing problem. Attribution confidence: high for the reported statements, distinct from confidence in technical restoration.
- **Type / scope:** Empirical technical status; Ukraine's affected Starlink/Starshield functions as of October 23. Full restoration means removal of the disputed limits, not merely a speed improvement for some users.
- **Why it matters:** A declared restoration would change Ukraine's immediate operational outlook and the diplomatic dispute. Partial recovery would not establish dependable access across functions or locations.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Trump's public assurance; earlier reporting of improvements. This is support for an assertion of restoration, not a public technical test. [Reuters via Investing.com, October 23](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/trump-says-he-will-fix-any-starlink-access-problem-in-ukraine-4970057) records his conditional promise to intervene if a problem remains, rather than a demonstrated service audit.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Zelenskyy's October 23 official address says: “First, the speed limits on Starlink and Starshield are still there.” The following sentence says speed had not been restored. No common technical definition, complete terminal sample, or company/government diagnostic record was retrieved. The two systems must not be treated as technically identical. [Ukrainian presidency, October 23, 21:51 displayed local time](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/nam-potribni-tristoronni-peregovori-mizh-ukrayinoyu-amerikoy-107013) establishes Ukraine's contrary official account; it is not independent telemetry.
- **Independent corroboration:** Partial for continuing reported restrictions; no independent system-wide measurement. Reuters and the presidency are independent publication channels, but the Ukrainian service-state evidence can share Ukrainian officials as its origin.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear**; EFCS **Unsupported** as a categorical full-restoration proposition, with a possible true core of partial improvement. Do not call restoration disproven across every terminal.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** that full restoration is not established; **low** on the complete service state. The claimant and user government contradict one another, and neither supplies public diagnostics.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Different units or functions may have recovered at different times, making both accounts partly accurate. That supports qualification rather than choosing one blanket account.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Trump said the service had been restored, but Ukraine continued to report speed restrictions on October 23; the public record does not establish full recovery.
- **Sources / provenance:** Reuters body, lines 39–45; presidency address, paragraphs beginning with the two adverse developments. The presidential address is the primary statement; no audio-based quotation claim is made. Do not infer a particular failed interception or casualty from these service reports.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate company/Pentagon technical clarification, distinguish products and affected functions, and check whether a unit-level improvement was generalized into full restoration.

## C-02 — Claim: No one in the Trump administration requested the disputed satellite-service changes

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Secretary of State Marco Rubio's October 21 denial, carried into the present dispute. Attribution confidence: high in the department's published transcript carried by Public Technologies; direct State.gov text was sought but not retrieved.
- **Type / scope:** Attribution of a government instruction concerning the current restriction episode; not a claim about every historical U.S.–SpaceX interaction.
- **Why it matters:** This determines accountability and how allies should understand access to strategically important private infrastructure.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Rubio denied a government request in the [State Department transcript distributed by Public Technologies](https://pubt.io/view/3FFEBABE5F497D9D86CF9EE63DA24E382C2EE1EC), particularly the paragraphs beginning “What I can tell you with certainty” and the questions about foreknowledge. **Background: October 21.** His surrounding answers distinguish commercial Starlink from Starshield and acknowledge that a military contract is a different case. Do not strip that concession from the denial.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** New [Reuters reporting, inspected via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/exclusiveus-has-for-years-asked-musk-to-alter-starlink-service-in-ukraine-middle-east-sources-say-4970529) repeats its reporting that the order originated inside the White House, notes the administration had not acknowledged issuing it, and records Musk's contrary account of Starshield control. It also reports earlier requests to change service in several countries, citing six former senior officials. Those historical requests narrow the broad private-company narrative but do not independently prove who ordered this restriction. No order, contract annex, decision log, or named firsthand witness was retrieved.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial / unresolved.** Rubio's denial and Musk's reported explanation are interested primary accounts. Reuters' current-order reporting and historical interviews are not the same proposition; the latter must not launder the former into proof. Multiple Reuters mirrors remain one lineage.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear / HOLD** on who requested the current changes. Neither “Musk alone” nor “Trump personally ordered it” is established by the inspected public record.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the public accounts conflict; **low-to-moderate** on responsibility. The decision record is absent and key reporting uses anonymous sources.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Government contract authority, informal influence, operational implementation, and a presidential order are separate links. Evidence for one need not establish all four.
- **Publication-safe correction:** U.S. officials, Musk, and reporting give conflicting accounts of the decision chain; the government–company boundary remains unresolved.
- **Sources / timing:** The new Reuters story displays October 23, 20:43 publication and 21:00 update without an explicit timezone on Investing.com. A [Yahoo Reuters listing](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/exclusive-us-years-asked-musk-004340637.html) independently displays October 24, 00:51 UTC, establishing pre-cutoff circulation; its body could not be opened (429). It is used only for timing, not factual evidence. The inspected Investing.com body is the evidence. The Public Technologies transcript identifies State as author and says it was distributed unaltered; it is an official-origin mirror, not independent reporting.
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve the original October 15/current-order Reuters investigation, the relevant military contract, the State original, and Musk's original post. Do not infer current command from historical collaboration.

## C-03 — Claim: The Oleshky ceasefire begins on October 29

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The White House's October 23 statement. Attribution confidence: high; exact primary statement inspected.
- **Type / scope:** Negotiation terms and chronology, confined to the humanitarian operation around Oleshky. This is an announced arrangement, not evidence that a pause is already in effect or a nationwide peace settlement.
- **Why it matters:** The difference between the military pause, demining, aid movement, and evacuation changes both expectations and what compliance should be monitored.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The [White House release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/10/first-lady-melania-trump-secures-ceasefire-between-russia-and-ukraine-localized-to-oleshky/) explicitly gives October 29. An earlier [Ukrainska Pravda report, October 23, 19:13 displayed local time](https://www.pravda.com.ua/news/2026/10/23/8059463/) reports Lubinets confirming the humanitarian operation and ceasefire on October 29.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** A later [Ukrainska Pravda report, October 23, 22:24 displayed local time](https://www.pravda.com.ua/news/2026/10/23/8059482/) quotes Ukraine's General Staff specifying a pause from 03:00 Kyiv time October 27 and reports Russia's General Staff specifying 04:00 Moscow time October 27. [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/russia-ukraine-agree-on-ceasefire-in-embattled-town-to-hold-peace-talks-next-month-ce785cdfdc8ff627) also reports October 27. Its latest displayed update is October 24, 01:14 BST, or 00:14 UTC, before cutoff.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Yes for announcement of a local humanitarian arrangement; partial for exact phases.** The White House, Ukrainian General Staff and Russian General Staff are distinct official origins. Reporting can independently obtain statements without independently proving future compliance. The Ukrainian Facebook post was attempted but inaccessible; the Russian Telegram page returned only an embed, not its text.
- **Current assessment:** **Qualified / materially misleading** if October 29 is presented as the sole military-pause start. The strongest reconciliation is an October 27 preparatory military pause before an October 29 mission, rather than evidence that no agreement exists. The [AP report](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-ceasefire-oleshky-melania-trump-60167627316e0e2f62fc359cf78f317d) attributes that sequence directly to Lubinets, but the mutable page's final update time was not exposed; use its phase explanation cautiously and confirm against the timestamped reports above.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate-high** on the need to distinguish phases; **moderate** on final scope and implementation details. No signed operational protocol was retrieved.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Officials may be describing different geographic areas or operation phases. A headline calling the dates contradictory without qualification would obscure this possibility.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Both sides announced an October 27 local pause; the White House gave October 29 for the Oleshky arrangement, with reporting indicating the humanitarian mission follows the preparatory pause. Implementation remains ahead.
- **Sources / provenance caution:** Do not describe the White House as establishing exclusive causal credit. The [Vatican's October 19 primary bulletin](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/it/bollettino/pubblico/2026/10/19/0822/01529.html), **background**, records the pope's earlier ceasefire request; [LB.ua's October 23 report](https://en.lb.ua/news/2026/10/23/46362_ukraine_prepares_operation_lift.html) attributes thanks for a multi-party effort to Arakhamia. Neither proves that Melania Trump's involvement was unnecessary, or that another participant alone caused the agreement.
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve original General Staff notices; retain exact named timezones; distinguish October 27 pause, October 29 aid mission, evacuation duration, and geographic coverage. Check the signed terms if available.

## C-04 — Claim: Four vessels transferring oil in the northern Persian Gulf were hit simultaneously on October 21

- **Origin / principal promoters:** IRGC Navy, through Iranian state media. Attribution confidence: high for the statement, not the incident.
- **Type / scope:** Physical attack and damage; exactly four vessels in the northern Gulf. Separate from the three alleged mine incidents in C-05.
- **Why it matters:** A confirmed attack on active oil transfers would bear on the resilience of Gulf exports and the exposure of workaround shipping.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [IRNA](https://irna.com/en/news/86300121/) and [Press TV](https://www.presstv.co.uk/Detail/2026/10/22/778916/IRGC-Navy-four-vessels-hit-Persian-Gulf) carry the specific IRGC account. [Reuters via StreetInsider, October 22, 00:39 EDT](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Irans%2BIRGC%2BNavy%2Bsays%2Bseven%2Bvessels%2Bhit,%2Bwarns%2BUS%2Bof%2Bwider%2Bresponse/27217663.html) confirms it was publicly made. **Background:** the original statement predates this research window; it remains a continuity dispute, not seven fresh attacks on October 23.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No vessel names, IMO numbers, owners' confirmation, authenticated incident imagery, damage record, or independent vessel-by-vessel match was retrieved. [gCaptain's October 22 account](https://gcaptain.com/iran-claims-more-ships-struck-near-hormuz-as-trump-accuses-tehran-of-faking-hits/) describes the claims as unconfirmed. Its illustrative photo is not proof of these incidents.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for the four attacks. IRNA, Press TV, Reuters and gCaptain repeat the same IRGC origin on the incident claim.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear / HOLD.** Unsupported as an established physical-event count; not shown false.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the inspected packet lacks independent confirmation; **low** on what occurred.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Operators may conceal incidents or disable location reporting; a public evidence gap is compatible with real attacks. That is a reason to retain uncertainty, not promote the claim to fact.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Iran claimed four oil-transfer vessels were hit; the specific incidents remain independently unverified in this packet.
- **Sources / entailment:** Iranian reports establish wording and alleged location; Reuters establishes attribution, not damage. The [UKMTO October 16 overview, page 3](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/20261016-ukmto_vra_overview_u.pdf?rev=dad3b12b1ccf4e859f22e0ef07709827), **background**, documents serious underreporting. It cannot confirm later incidents.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek named vessels, insurer/owner statements and imagery; do not add the four to an independent attack total without de-duplication.

## C-05 — Claim: Three additional vessels struck mines west of Hormuz on the night of October 21–22

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Same IRGC Navy statement as C-04. Attribution confidence: high.
- **Type / scope:** Mine causation and incident count; three vessels, west of the strait, in the named night. No inference about where the mines originated is included.
- **Why it matters:** Verified new mining would be a different maritime threat from missiles, drones, or harassment and could require different protection and clearance measures.
- **Evidence offered for it:** IRNA and Press TV carry the allegation of three vessels, fires, and immobilization. The Reuters brief carries the collision claim. These are the same lineage, not three confirmations. See the C-04 links.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No wreckage, mines, operator report, vessel identity or forensic causal evidence was retrieved. [JMIC's October 18 advisory, page 2](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/update-106-jmic-advisory-note-18-october.pdf?rev=86f67da1821f4dcdb4e27868b6679729) says earlier October 17 claims of two tankers striking mines lacked substantiation. **Background:** that finding concerns different alleged incidents and cannot refute the three claimed here.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for these three mine collisions.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear / HOLD.** An accident, another weapon, a false report, or an actual mine strike remain unresolved possibilities; no one explanation is selected from plausibility alone.
- **Confidence:** **High** in the evidence gap; **low** in incident count and mine attribution.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** A continuing mine threat and incomplete reporting make occurrence possible. But a general threat assessment cannot verify a named night's incident count.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Iran alleged three mine collisions; the public evidence retrieved does not establish their occurrence or cause.
- **Sources / quantity discipline:** C-04's four vessels and C-05's three are mutually described subsets of the IRGC's seven-vessel allegation. The arithmetic 4 + 3 = 7 is correct; the underlying vessel identities and uniqueness are unverified. Do not call seven a verified incident denominator or imply seven independent attacks.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain incident-level positions and times, mine evidence, and a match to UKMTO/JMIC notices. Keep projectile attacks and mine collisions distinct.

- **Current assessment:** **Weak / Unsupported**, not false merely because unproved. The same standard applies to Tehran's incident claim and Washington's fabrication claim.
- **Confidence:** **High** that absence of UKMTO warnings alone cannot establish fabrication; **low** on whether any or all of these incidents actually happened.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Authorities may possess non-public surveillance disproving the account. No such record was available to this scout, so that possibility cannot settle the public evidence judgment.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Iran's seven-vessel account remains unverified; Trump's assertion that it was invented is also not established by the evidence inspected.

## C-07 — Claim: Iran has detailed plans to attack Gulf undersea communications cables if the U.S. and Israel attack again

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Two unnamed senior Iranian security officials interviewed by Reuters in a new October 23 report. Attribution confidence: high to Reuters' reported interviews, unknown independent identity verification.
- **Type / scope:** Contingency planning; conditional on renewed attack. This is separate from having demonstrated an ability to sever every cable, preventing repairs, causing a global outage, or deciding to execute the plan.
- **Why it matters:** The claim signals a possible expansion of coercion from shipping and oil into digital infrastructure and may affect Gulf states' calculations.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [Reuters via Investing.com Canada](https://ca.investing.com/news/world-news/iran-has-detailed-plans-to-hit-gulf-undersea-cables-if-war-resumes-officials-say-4900566) directly reports the interviews and earlier named Iranian warnings. Officials asserting private preparations is the strongest retrieved support, but no plan document is public in this packet.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The interviewees provide no exact attack locations or method; no operational document, exercise, damage evidence, or independent intelligence confirmation was retrieved. Declared capability may also function as deterrent signaling. No primary cable-operator assessment was retrieved to quantify achievable disruption.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for existence and maturity of the plans. [The Times of Israel's dated Reuters republication](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/iranian-officials-tehran-has-detailed-plans-to-hit-gulf-undersea-cables-if-war-resumes/) is the same interview lineage, not corroboration.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible but unverified / HOLD** on operational planning; **Supported** only as a reported Iranian threat. Do not replace the conditional threat with a forecast of execution.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** on the threat's existence; **low** on readiness, ease of execution, and likely damage.
- **Strongest challenge to this assessment:** Security plans are normally secret; absence of a public document is unsurprising. Conversely, unverifiable detail can amplify deterrent signaling. Neither consideration independently establishes the plans' operational maturity.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Two Iranian officials told Reuters Iran had prepared cable-attack plans for renewed war; the plans and capability were not independently demonstrated.
- **Sources / timing / denominator caution:** The Reuters mirror displays October 23, 12:58 publication and 13:32 update, without explicit timezone; the dated Times of Israel entry provides an additional pre-cutoff carrier. This is one Reuters origin. The share of worldwide traffic carried by all undersea cables is not the share exposed in Hormuz; no global-outage percentage is supported here. Broader impact also depends on rerouting, spare capacity and repair access, which need operator evidence.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek original named warnings, any independently inspectable plan or capability evidence, and cable-operator/TeleGeography assessments. Preserve the distinction between global traffic, international bandwidth, regional exposure, and inter-regional demand.
