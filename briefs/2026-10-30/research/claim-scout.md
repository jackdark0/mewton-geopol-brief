## Claim: C-01 — Qatari funds illegally financed Arab voter-mobilization efforts in Israel's October 27 election

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Likud's reported suspicions and Israeli television reporting, especially Channel 12, relayed in a dated Times of Israel entry. This is a finance/foreign-interference allegation; it does not itself assert altered ballots or a changed seat result. Attribution to the reported allegation is high; attribution to the still-unpublished underlying intelligence is unresolved.
- **Why it matters:** A genuine foreign funding operation would affect election security and regional diplomacy. Treating an unproven allegation as established can also delegitimize Arab citizens' political participation and influence coalition bargaining.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The October 29, 10:03 a.m. ToI entry reports intelligence presented to Central Elections Committee chair Noam Sohlberg and an instruction to investigate suspected foreign funding and electoral bribery. This supports the existence of a serious investigative lead, not its truth. A later dated entry describes a focused inquiry into money's source, route, amount, and possible recipient knowledge.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Qatar denied interference and funding intended to influence the result in the October 29, 2:54 p.m. entry. Its denial is an interested party's account, not exculpatory proof. No bank records, transfer amounts, recipients, authenticated communications, intelligence dossier, charge, or merits finding were inspected. The October 29, 2:32 p.m. entry reports that the committee's acting director said the intelligence was not yet verified. A turnout increase alone would not establish illegal funding.
- **Independent corroboration:** **no** for the underlying Qatari funding allegation. Multiple reports largely trace to the intelligence inquiry and common broadcaster sourcing. The denial is a separate statement lineage, not independent resolution of the facts.
- **Current assessment:** **unclear.** An investigation is supported; the tested funding proposition remains unestablished. This is not a finding that Qatar was cleared or that no prohibited financing occurred.
- **Confidence:** **medium.** The public record's limits are clear, but inaccessible intelligence and financial records could materially change the assessment.

## Claim: C-02 — Arab parties stole Israel's election outcome through systematic fraud

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Netanyahu's Likud and allied political figures, as documented in the dated October 29 ToI entries. This is stronger than C-01: proving foreign campaign-finance misconduct would not by itself prove systematic ballot fraud or a changed election outcome.
- **Why it matters:** The allegation can shape the legitimacy of coalition formation and treatment of Arab-led parties. It also risks converting an intelligence inquiry into a claim that the vote count should be disregarded.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Promoters point to alleged foreign-funded mobilization and the newly public investigation. Neither supplies a public count of invalid votes or a demonstrated seat effect in the inspected record.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The October 29, 2:32 p.m. entry reports Livne's statement that the election was conducted lawfully and that the committee knew of no basis for systematic integrity violations. The 9:11 p.m. entry reports sources saying the focused inquiry would not affect results and certification remained scheduled for November 4. These are strong narrowing statements, but the anonymous part should remain attributed. No audit, invalid-ballot tally, systematic polling-station evidence, or causal calculation translating alleged misconduct into seats was inspected.
- **Independent corroboration:** **no** for the stolen-outcome proposition. Committee and security accounts are different institutional voices, relayed through overlapping media; they are not two completed independent forensic audits.
- **Current assessment:** **weak.** Available evidence supports an investigation into narrower suspected conduct, while the relevant election authority rejects the systematic-fraud narrative. Absence of public proof is not proof that every individual irregularity was absent.
- **Confidence:** **medium.** Strong authority-specific rebuttal and an unfilled evidentiary bridge; unpublished case material and incomplete primary-document access limit a categorical falsity verdict.
- **Sources:**
  - [ToI, October 29, 2:32 p.m.: Livne rejects widespread-fraud allegation](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/elections-panel-director-says-claims-of-widespread-fraud-appear-to-be-baseless-vote-was-conducted-lawfully/) — body paragraphs reproducing committee letter and describing Likud's allegation; competent for the committee's stated position, not proof of universal absence of wrongdoing.
  - [ToI, October 29, 9:11 p.m.: inquiry described as focused](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/certified-election-results-will-be-presented-to-the-president-as-scheduled-with-nothing-in-foreign-influence-probe-that-could-affect-them-shin-bet-sources-say/) — anonymous Channel 12/Shin Bet lineage; retain attribution for certification and outcome implications.
- **Questions for auditor:** Define what stolen means before rating it: illegal financing, coercion, invalid ballots, changed count, or changed seats? Obtain the original committee statements and any pre-cutoff evidence quantifying affected votes. Do not infer collective wrongdoing from Arab turnout. Do not treat a pending inquiry as a legal finding.

## Claim: C-03 — Three oil tankers struck naval mines in the southern Strait of Hormuz during the 24 hours covered by Iran's October 29 announcement

- **Origin / principal promoters:** IRGC Navy's public-relations account, carried by Tasnim and state media. Iran's regular army subsequently alleged another tanker incident east of the strait. These are distinct interested military claims, not four independent confirmations.
- **Why it matters:** Mine-strike confirmation would materially affect shipping risk, the credibility of safe-transit assurances, and the US-Iran confrontation. Incorrectly identifying another burning vessel as a tanker would inflate the apparent evidence.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Tasnim's dated October 29, 14:57 account relays the IRGC's assertion of three tankers, fire, abandonment, and navigation equipment being disabled. AFP's updated report carries the additional regular-army allegation. The inspected accounts do not publicly identify the alleged tankers or establish their flags or incident times.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** UKMTO Warning 185-26 Update 001 independently records a reported fire nine nautical miles north of Khasab; regional authorities identify that vessel as a **dhow**, with crew evacuated and the dhow sinking. The record supplies no mine attribution. It therefore does not corroborate three tanker mine strikes and must not be added to Iran's total. AFP reports CENTCOM's safe-route assertion, but that assertion is also interested and concerns primary transit routes, which may not be the same geographic claim as Iran's southern route. Missing: named ships, owners/flag-state confirmations, incident coordinates and times, damage inspections, mine identification, or geolocated imagery tied to the alleged tankers.
- **Independent corroboration:** **partial** only for a separate maritime fire/sinking; **no** for the precise three-tanker mine-strike proposition.
- **Current assessment:** **unclear.** Iran's announcement is established as an announcement. Tanker count and mine causation remain unverified. A separate dhow incident neither proves nor disproves that other tanker incidents occurred.
- **Confidence:** **medium.** Primary maritime reporting sharply limits one proposed corroboration; tanker-specific evidence remains absent from the inspected sources.
- **Sources:**

## Claim: C-04 — Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia leaked classified Iran- and Ukraine-war information to reporters

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Unnamed Pentagon officials cited by reporters. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell publicly described an investigation, personnel action, and Justice Department referral without naming the individual in the statement reproduced by news sources. That is distinct from a published finding establishing Sylvia's guilt.
- **Why it matters:** An accusation against the senior military planner affects confidence in defense decision-making and civil-military relations. The reason for removal is now a substantive disputed story, while the alleged disclosure itself still needs evidence.
- **Evidence offered for it:** NPR's October 29 report attributes the Iran/Ukraine disclosure allegation to officials and describes Parnell's investigation/referral statement. The Washington Examiner separately reports the same public statement. These reports substantiate a new stated rationale and referral; the public evidence does not expose the allegedly leaked documents, communications, investigator findings, or charging record.

Parnell's statement does not name Sylvia. A personnel action and referral do not establish that a crime occurred, that prosecutors charged him, or that a court determined guilt. Alternative accounts of an internal personnel dispute cannot themselves exonerate him without evidence.
- **Independent corroboration:** **partial** for removal, the accusation, and public referral statement; **no** for the underlying leak proposition. Multiple reports of Parnell's statement share that origin. Anonymous-source overlap between outlets is unknown.
- **Current assessment:** **unclear.** Report the allegation and process with attribution. Do not state that Sylvia leaked classified information as a settled fact.
- **Confidence:** **medium.** Convergent reporting supports the accusation's existence; conflicting anonymous accounts and withheld case material prevent a finding on misconduct.
