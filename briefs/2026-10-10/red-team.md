# Red Team - 2026-10-10

**Scope:** Adversarial review of `verified.md` and `analysis.md` against the fixed cutoff of 2026-10-10 02:00:00 America/New_York. This review does not admit later reporting.

## Executive challenge

The evidence supports a strong six-story brief, but only if the editor keeps three boundaries visible:

1. A legal authorization is not proof that Russian diesel has moved or that prices will fall.
2. Official or media claims about military action are not independent confirmation of the actor, weapon, distance, or intent.
3. Reported planning options are not a presidential decision or an imminent operation.

The most likely editorial failure is to turn a well-sourced change in policy, posture, or planning into a stronger claim about outcomes. The final must distinguish what happened from what might follow.

## Story challenges

### 1. Russian diesel authorization and Miami diplomacy

**Strongest surviving fact:** OFAC General License 135 creates a broad but temporary authorization through April 7, 2027, for transactions ordinarily incident and necessary to the sale, delivery, offloading, or importation of Russian-origin diesel. The exclusions in the legal text remain material.

**Failure modes:**

- Calling this a completed sanctions rollback or a durable normalization. It is a time-limited license with important exclusions.
- Treating promised Russian supply, cargo movement, or US consumer relief as established. No cargo, buyer, terminal, or retail-price effect was verified by the cutoff.
- Turning simultaneity into causation. The license and Miami talks overlapped, but the record does not prove that one was consideration for the other.
- Repeating the unnamed-source claim that Washington wants Ukraine to stop refinery attacks as a settled US demand.

**Required edit:** Lead with the legal text, then separate the Reuters market report and unnamed diplomatic reporting. Say that timing affects leverage; do not say there was a quid pro quo.

**Confidence:** High on the authorization; medium on the diplomatic context; low on commercial and bargaining outcomes.

### 2. US designation of the ICC

**Strongest surviving fact:** OFAC designated the International Criminal Court itself. General Licenses 13, 14, and 15 preserve specified categories of ordinary operations, communications and software services, and pension payments.

**Failure modes:**

- Saying the court was immediately cut off from all finance or technology. The licenses directly contradict that formulation.
- Treating the licenses as proof that the institution will function normally. Their practical sufficiency is untested, and some relief is time-limited.
- Presenting the Nobel Peace Prize announcement as causally connected to the designation. The juxtaposition is editorially striking but not evidence of coordination.
- Collapsing legal and political judgments. The US action is verified; the competing claims about ICC legitimacy remain argument.

**Required edit:** Describe the designation and carve-outs precisely. Frame operational damage as the next empirical test, not an accomplished fact.

**Confidence:** High on legal status; medium-low on near-term operational consequences.

### 3. Saudi airspace risk and Pakistan's role

**Strongest surviving fact:** Saudi authorities reported three deaths from attacks affecting airport or aircraft infrastructure; the Houthis claimed responsibility. EASA defined two prohibited areas for covered operators and advised caution elsewhere. Pakistan publicly limited its role to Saudi defense and said its ground forces would not enter Yemen.

**Failure modes:**

- Merging the Saudi casualty report and the Houthi claim into independent confirmation that a specific Houthi weapon killed the victims.
- Calling Saudi airspace closed. EASA prohibited two defined areas for operators within its remit and recommended caution elsewhere.
- Treating Pakistan's declaration as proof of all field activity. Its ground-force statement does not settle air, intelligence, logistics, or future-role questions.
- Using phrases such as “Pakistan enters the war” without distinguishing an acknowledged defensive deployment from offensive operations.

**Required edit:** Attribute the casualty report, attack claim, and Pakistani assurances separately. Make the aviation-rule change the verified strategic signal.

**Confidence:** High on the bulletin and statements; medium on the incident attribution; low on unobserved Pakistani activity.

### 4. EU-China trade framework

**Strongest surviving fact:** EU and Chinese officials announced a framework involving minimum-price undertakings and work on rare-earth export-license facilitation.

**Failure modes:**

- Calling the framework a completed deal. Political approval and implementation details remained unresolved.
- Saying Chinese EV exports will be cut in half. The commissioner's “half” formulation referred to a four-year counterfactual, not a verified reduction from current volume.
- Saying China removed rare-earth controls. A green channel or faster licensing is not abolition.
- Claiming the measure will rebalance EU industry without evidence on price levels, volumes, enforcement, or substitution.

**Required edit:** Use “framework,” define the counterfactual, and make approval and implementation the watch points.

**Confidence:** High on the announced framework; medium-low on economic effects.

### 5. Eritrean forces in northern Ethiopia

**Strongest surviving fact:** Multiple reporting streams support an Eritrean military presence in northern Ethiopia and elevated mobilization. The exact scale, entry date, command chain, and depth remain uncertain.

**Failure modes:**

- Stating the “60 kilometres” figure or “all-out invasion” as verified fact. Those are Ethiopian government claims.
- Treating the CTP geolocation as proof of when troops moved or who commanded them. CTP explicitly could not establish date or actor.
- Counting official claims, local media, and downstream reports as fully independent when they may share one source chain.
- Predicting renewed full-scale war without a clear indicator ladder.

**Required edit:** Say the presence is corroborated, but label disputed scale and intent. Focus watch points on verified force movements, clashes, evacuation orders, and mediation.

**Confidence:** Medium-high on presence; low-to-medium on scale, timing, and intent.

### 6. Reported US options for strikes on Iran

**Strongest surviving fact:** The New York Times reported that senior US officials presented a three-day strike option, while the same report said President Trump had rejected five earlier operations and pledged no pre-election attack.

**Failure modes:**

- Presenting the option as approved, scheduled, or imminent.
- Treating one anonymous-source lineage as independent confirmation.
- Omitting the contradictory restraint evidence in the same report.
- Using the phrase “renewed presidential direction” as fact when the public evidence establishes renewed planning, not a final order.

**Required edit:** Keep the story only as a tightly framed planning signal. Pair the option with the rejected-operation history and the public pledge. Confidence should not exceed medium on the reported briefing and must be low on execution.

**Confidence:** Medium on the reported presentation; low on whether it will be used.

## Cross-cutting tests

### Retrieval grounding

The core legal and regulatory stories are anchored in direct documents. Military incident reporting is necessarily less direct and should retain attribution in every consequential sentence. No search snippet should appear as evidence in the final.

### Atomicity and entailment

Avoid sentences that combine a verified act with an unverified effect. In particular:

- “Washington authorized Russian diesel, lowering prices” fails; only the first clause is established.
- “Houthi attacks killed three people” is too compressed; Saudi authorities reported deaths and the Houthis claimed the attacks.
- “The EU halved Chinese EV imports” is false to the source; the figure is a future counterfactual estimate.
- “Trump ordered a three-day strike” is unsupported; officials reportedly presented an option.

### Provenance and independence

The Ethiopia and Iran stories have the greatest risk of apparent source abundance masking a narrow underlying chain. The final should not use citation count as a proxy for independent confirmation.

### Denominator discipline

The EU-China “half” claim needs its denominator every time: projected cumulative Chinese EV sales over four years under an unchanged-policy counterfactual. The Chinese fuel-export figure covers gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel together, not diesel alone. The Saudi airspace restriction applies to two areas and covered operators, not the entire country or all airlines.

### Time discipline

The edition must remain frozen at 02:00 America/New_York. Any October 10 development published after 06:00 UTC is outside the evidence set even if it changes the story before the finishing run.

## Editorial instructions

1. Use six major stories; do not pad.
2. Put confidence labels on each story and make confidence attach to the evidence, not the importance.
3. Preserve verdict/confidence separation in contested claims.
4. Keep Rapid Fire to confirmed changes and label proposals or company forecasts.
5. Use only the verified official dates in the upcoming-events calendar.
6. Make all three show questions genuine dilemmas rather than disguised conclusions.
