## Candidate: U.S. aircraft disables a merchant ship leaving Iran

- **Event time/date:** 2026-11-26; disclosed 2026-11-27.
- **Region / actors:** Gulf of Oman; U.S. Central Command, Iran, Palau-flagged M/V *Pacific Archer*.
- **What changed:** CENTCOM said a U.S. aircraft fired precision munitions at the ship's stern and disabled its propulsion after it left Bandar Abbas and did not comply with the U.S. blockade. AP reported that tracking sites listed Singapore as the destination and that the civilian crew was unharmed.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - It is a direct U.S. use of force against a civilian merchant vessel during a diplomatic effort to prevent renewed U.S.-Iran escalation.
  - CENTCOM calls it at least the fifteenth disclosed vessel disabled since the blockade began; the operation is coercive state practice, not a one-off interception.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high that the U.S. struck and disabled the ship; medium on the alleged blockade violation and warning sequence.
- **Source chain:** CENTCOM is the originating claimant; AP independently reports the statement and checks public tracking, but does not independently establish the ship's intent.
- **Sources:** [AP dedicated report](https://apnews.com/article/iran-naval-blockade-us-military-disabled-ship-800620518a351282f098faa9381b3375) — strike details, tracking discrepancy, blockade history; [AP regional report](https://apnews.com/article/saudi-iran-yemen-israel-november-27-2026-dbb2c950f5cd991a52f3a065b6325535) — contemporaneous regional context.
- **Questions for auditor:** What evidence establishes the ship's intended Iranian call or refusal to comply? Is there imagery, crew or flag-state reporting? What legal basis has Washington published for disabling merchant ships in international waters?

## Candidate: Reported breach of the U.S. Air Force's Cloud One platform

- **Event time/date:** Discovered within roughly the last month; reported 2026-11-27.
- **Region / actors:** United States; Air Force, U.S. Cyber Command, Cloud One contractors, unidentified hackers.
- **What changed:** Reuters reported, citing five unnamed people, that hackers reached Cloud One and that the discovery paused migrations, blocked some updates and led to revoked administrator rights. Federal News Network separately said a source familiar with the incident confirmed a breach and some disruption. The Air Force declined to confirm operational details and did not say whether data were taken.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - Cloud One hosts personnel, logistics and maintenance applications and supports systems tied to air-defense feeds and global munitions tracking.
  - A control-plane compromise could create operational and counterintelligence risk even if no classified data were taken.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** medium-high that a breach occurred; low on actor, duration, data access and operational effect.
- **Source chain:** Reuters and Federal News Network cite different unnamed sources, although the latter explicitly followed Reuters and confirmed only some details. Air Force language is nonconfirming.
- **Sources:** [Reuters copy](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Hackers%2Bbreach%2BUS%2BAir%2BForce%2Bflagship%2BCloud%2BOne%2Bcomputing%2Bplatform/27405284.html) — multi-source report and explicit unknowns; [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/air-force/2026/11/hackers-breach-air-forces-cloud-one-computing-environment/) — separate source confirmation and system context.
- **Questions for auditor:** Did the breach reach only an administrative plane or hosted applications? Was data exfiltrated? Is Cyber Command investigating? Are Reuters and FNN sources independent?
