## Candidate: Cloud One breach may reach the Air Force's administrative control plane

- **Underlying development:** Reuters reported that intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach Cloud One's control plane; the discovery paused migrations, blocked some application updates and prompted revoked administrator privileges. Federal News Network separately confirmed a breach and some administrative disruption with its own source.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The immediate public record contains no casualty, kinetic event or named foreign actor, but Cloud One supports 750,000 users and hosts Air Force and Space Force personnel, logistics, maintenance and mission applications.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Access to a cloud control plane can expose system configuration and operational metadata, impede patching and create pathways across hosted applications. Even without confirmed data theft, remediation can reduce availability and slow logistics or software deployment.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Two specialist reports with at least partly separate sourcing; Air Force nonconfirmation coupled with acknowledgement of continuing protective measures; named applications and documented Cloud One scope.
- **What remains inference:** The attacker's identity, data exfiltration, persistence, classified-system exposure, operational effect and whether the FNN and Reuters sources are fully independent.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [Reuters copy](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Hackers%2Bbreach%2BUS%2BAir%2BForce%2Bflagship%2BCloud%2BOne%2Bcomputing%2Bplatform/27405284.html) — incident, disruptions and explicit unknowns; [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/air-force/2026/11/hackers-breach-air-forces-cloud-one-computing-environment/) — separate source confirmation and platform scope.
- **Questions for auditor:** Which source details are independently corroborated? Did Cyber Command or CISA issue an incident record? Was the affected plane unclassified, and did the compromise cross tenant boundaries?

## Candidate: The U.S. blockade is becoming a repeatable ship-disablement regime

- **Underlying development:** The U.S. disabled *Pacific Archer* after it left Bandar Abbas. CENTCOM says 15 disclosed vessels have been disabled since spring and 158 redirected since the blockade resumed in July.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Each incident can look tactical, but the accumulated practice is a continuing maritime coercion regime with an asserted right to strike merchant ships.
- **Strategic mechanism:** The blockade raises insurance and transit risk, constrains Iran's trade and creates recurrent escalation opportunities with Iran, flag states, crews and shipping firms. It also tests international acceptance of U.S. enforcement in international waters.
- **What evidence is observable now:** CENTCOM's acknowledgement of a precision strike; public ship-tracking destination listed as Singapore; AP's tally and the prior fatal Settebello incident.
- **What remains inference:** Whether the ship planned a prohibited Iranian port call or cargo movement, whether warnings were effective, and the legal validity of the blockade or each use of force.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP dedicated report](https://apnews.com/article/iran-naval-blockade-us-military-disabled-ship-800620518a351282f098faa9381b3375) — official account, tracking and history.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek the full CENTCOM release, AIS history, flag-state response, crew account and published legal rationale.

## Candidate: EU political support for Ukraine financing has not become deliverable money

- **Underlying development:** Twenty-four EU members supported the Ukraine-financing proposal, but leaders failed to agree on the 2028-2034 budget and deferred the financing question to December.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** A headline about broad 24-state support can obscure the operational fact that no adopted financing instrument emerged.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Ukraine's 2027 budget and defense planning depend on predictable disbursement. Delay weakens planning and bargaining leverage even when most member governments support the policy in principle.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Official European Council outcome language; Costa's statement that one supporting state, Bulgaria, will join only the non-military component; AP reporting that the loan was caught in wider budget negotiations.
- **What remains inference:** The final amount, whether nonparticipating states can be bypassed through enhanced cooperation, repayment conditions and whether a December agreement is likely.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [European Council](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/meetings/european-council/2026/11/26-27/) — exact outcome; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/europe-budget-ukraine-2028-summit-costa-fd025046850b824b2e62def18408c6b6) — negotiation mechanics.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate the Commission proposal and identify the three states outside the 24; distinguish the new proposal from the existing 2026-2027 loan.

## Candidate: Mecca pact advances institutionally but not yet visibly in force posture

- **Underlying development:** Erdogan's approval completed Turkey's domestic ratification, while the pact's strategic committee convened with the three states' senior diplomatic, defense and military leadership and reaffirmed support for Saudi security.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The meaningful change is institutional: domestic approval, a staffed secretariat and recurring committee. Public rhetoric can exaggerate that into automatic war entry or an existing deployment.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A standing decision structure and rapid-deployment planning can improve deterrence and burden-sharing for Saudi defense. It could also tie Turkey and nuclear-armed Pakistan more closely to Gulf escalation choices.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Turkish ministry meeting notice; AP report of presidential approval, a ten-year term, a Saudi-based secretariat and prior rapid-deployment language.
- **What remains inference:** Entry into force, force packages, basing, command arrangements, funding and whether any member would automatically fight the Houthis or Iran.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [Turkish Foreign Ministry](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/no_-231_-ortak-savunma-icin-mekke-anlasmasi-kapsaminda-tesis-edilen-stratejik-siyasi-ve-savunma-komitesi-nin-ikinci-toplantisi-hk.en.mfa); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/pakistan-saudi-turkey-meeting-trilateral-alliance-6c17e4827e5aef3e69f78cdb01d597dc).
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain Gazette text and joint communique; separate ratification, entry into force and operational implementation.

## Candidate: Australia escalates a foreign-interference attribution into named sanctions

- **Underlying development:** Australia named two people and imposed counterterrorism-financing sanctions, asserting they organized two 2024 antisemitic arson attacks through proxies for the IRGC.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The legal designations are narrow, but the underlying attribution is a serious state-directed violence claim and follows embassy suspension and diplomatic expulsions.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Named designations can expose networks, deter intermediaries and enable asset freezes. They also harden Australia-Iran relations and create reputational and legal stakes if the public evidence is thin.
- **What evidence is observable now:** A formal ministerial release, an updated consolidated list and a reference to ASIO's June threat assessment.
- **What remains inference:** The operational chain, evidence tying the two men to Iran and to the attacks, their location and assets, and whether prosecutors could meet a criminal burden.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high.
- **Sources:** [Australian foreign minister](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/further-action-against-irans-irgc-attacks-australia); [DFAT Consolidated List](https://www.dfat.gov.au/international-relations/security/sanctions/consolidated-list).
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate the June ASIO assessment and designation instrument; do not convert an executive assessment into an adjudicated fact.

## Candidate: A limited Black Sea and energy ceasefire track emerges

- **Underlying development:** Sybiha said Ukraine offered a Black Sea ceasefire for food exports and reported a Russian demand that Ukraine halt oil-sector strikes. Kyiv would consider the latter only with reciprocal protection for Ukrainian energy infrastructure.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** It is narrower than a general ceasefire, but limited agreements can be more negotiable and produce observable compliance tests.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A Black Sea arrangement could reduce food-shipping risk, while a reciprocal energy deal would trade Ukraine's deep-strike pressure on Russian refining for protection of Ukraine's grid. Each side would give up a different leverage tool.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Reuters-based reporting of Sybiha's statement and older public Ukrainian support for a reciprocal energy ceasefire.
- **What remains inference:** Russia's actual offer, scope, enforcement, duration, inclusion of ports and naval operations, and whether either side is willing to accept verification.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high.
- **Sources:** [Gulf Times PDF](https://www.gulf-times.com/gulftimes/uploads/pdf/2026/11/28/main-20261128-1-1795817076.pdf?ts=1795817076); [BGNES](https://www.bgnes.com/conflicts/ukraine-proposes-black-sea-ceasefire-as-russia-demands-halt-to-oil-attacks); [OSCE record of Ukraine's prior reciprocal position](https://resources.osce.org/sites/default/files/documents/official_documents/2026/10/pcdel1022%20ukraine%201581pc.pdf).
- **Questions for auditor:** Find the original statement and Russian response; establish whether this is a new proposal or a restatement of September terms.
