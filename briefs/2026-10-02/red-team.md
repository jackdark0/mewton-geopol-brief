# Red-team review - 2026-10-02

## Finding: Do not convert a diplomatic rupture into proof of interstate entry

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Ethiopia-Eritrea analysis; proxy-escalation assessment.
- **Problem:** The formal rupture is verified, but sponsorship, command, and direct Eritrean participation are not. The analysis could lead an editor to present the bilateral break as confirmation that the northern Ethiopian war has become interstate.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Both governments are interested sources for the other's alleged support to armed groups. AFP explicitly says access and communications conditions make verification difficult. The Addis blasts also have no established cause.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The governments may be formalizing a relationship that had already collapsed while continuing primarily indirect pressure; severance need not produce a new operational commitment.
- **Required editorial action:** Lead with the diplomatic act. Describe direct war or proxy sponsorship only as a risk or allegation. Keep the Addis blasts out of causal framing.

## Finding: The combined U.S. force movement rests on one unnamed official

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** U.S. naval movement story and editorial ranking.
- **Problem:** AP's combined 9,000-person movement, Makin Island orders, and projected carrier overlap share one unnamed-official source. USNI independently supports Roosevelt's departure and relief expectation, not the whole package.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The source-provenance gap is material because “third carrier” suggests additive escalation while USNI says Roosevelt is expected to relieve George Washington.
- **Best competing interpretation:** This is mostly a planned rotation plus an amphibious deployment, not a newly ordered three-carrier surge.
- **Required editorial action:** Use “AP reports” for the combined movement. State that three-carrier overlap is possible and temporary, not present. Make relief timing a watch item.

## Finding: Trump's interview cannot characterize the later paper exchange

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** U.S.-Iran chronology and any “latest offer” wording.
- **Problem:** The interview was conducted September 28 and published October 1; Iran announced receipt of a U.S. response on September 30. Collapsing these into one negotiation would create temporal contamination.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The transcript establishes Trump's view of an earlier offer and his willingness to consider bombing. It does not identify the later U.S. response, common text, or acceptance status.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Trump rejected one package while officials continued a separate or revised exchange through mediators.
- **Required editorial action:** Call it an earlier offer. Say publication clarified Trump's position without proving talks ended or defining the later response.

- **Severity:** major

## Finding: Pakistan and UNAMA counts are not competing estimates of one denominator

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Pakistan-Afghanistan story.
- **Problem:** “Pakistan says 22 militants; UN says ten civilians” can sound like two totals describing the same mutually exclusive population. Neither source published a complete named list or site-by-site reconciliation.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** UNAMA presents a verified civilian minimum; Pakistan presents an initial militant claim and a zero-collateral assertion. The categories may concern different people or include disputed identities.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Pakistan may have hit both militants and civilians, or may have misidentified some victims; the record cannot choose.
- **Required editorial action:** Present each atom separately, label UNAMA's number a minimum, and explicitly say the accounts are unreconciled. Do not subtract or add them.

## Finding: The sanctions story combines two legal tracks with different status

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** U.S. Iran industrial/A7 story.
- **Problem:** Combining sector determinations, entity designations, an OFAC network designation, a FinCEN alert, and a proposed rule can blur what changed immediately.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Named OFAC designations have blocking consequences now. A sector determination expands designation authority. The FinCEN transfer prohibition remained proposed and was pending Federal Register publication at the cutoff.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The main immediate effect is risk signaling and designation; the broadest payment-channel restriction may never take effect in the proposed form.
- **Required editorial action:** List immediate actions separately from the proposal. Avoid saying Washington has already cut A7 sub-agents out of payment channels.

## Finding: A7 numbers are activity metrics, not impact or Iranian revenue

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** sanctions analysis and any numeric headline.
- **Problem:** The $17 billion and $179.1 billion figures use different populations and could be mistaken for illicit proceeds stopped by the October 1 action.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** One is FinCEN's globally aggregated sub-agent transaction assessment; the other is gross A7A5 token transfer activity and may include repeated/internal transfers. Neither is the amount tied specifically to Iran or blocked by the new action.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Large gross flows may coexist with a much smaller sanctions-evasion or Iran-linked subset.
- **Required editorial action:** Prefer omitting both figures from the final brief unless the denominator caveat is useful to the story. If included, place the caveat in the same sentence.
