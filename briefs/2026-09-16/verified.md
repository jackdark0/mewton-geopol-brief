## Story: House rebukes the Iran war as CBO documents its readiness cost

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- **[CONFIRMED]** At 22:32 ET on September 15, the House agreed to H. Con. Res. 93 by 220-204; seven Republicans and 213 Democrats voted yes. [House Clerk](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2026307?Page=1)
- **[SUPPORTED]** This was the third House vote against the Iran war. Earlier congressional measures had not stopped U.S. operations, and the resolution had not become a law presented to the president. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-powers-resolution-8519dae8197214b4edf2192e174c82f4)
- **[CONFIRMED]** The administration opposed the resolution and argued that a concurrent resolution cannot bind the executive. That document establishes the administration's legal position, not a final judicial answer. [White House statement of administration policy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/SAP-HCR93.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTED]** CBO estimated roughly $38 billion in Defense Department costs through August 1. It estimated another month at about $2 billion under May-June operating intensity or $3 billion under July intensity, potentially more if operations escalated. [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62756)
- **[SUPPORTED]** CBO identified missile-defense interceptor use as the war's largest military opportunity cost and said lower inventories could persist for several years, limiting capacity for another missile-heavy contingency. CBO also said its estimates were subject to considerable uncertainty because the Defense Department did not answer its information requests. [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62756)
- **[SUPPORTED]** A separate Lead Inspector General report estimated $33.4 billion through June 29 using a different time boundary and accounting frame; it also reported munitions and industrial-base constraints. The two totals are corroborating context, not additive figures. [Lead Inspector General report](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Sep/09/2003993626/-1/-1/1/OEF_Q3_JUN2026_FINAL_508%20SECURE.PDF)

### Material claims not established

- The House vote immediately ended, or legally compelled an end to, U.S. operations. The resolution's legal effect is disputed; no evidence showed operations ceased after the vote.
- The war's definitive all-in cost is $38 billion. CBO's figure is an estimate of Defense Department costs through August 1 and excludes or separately treats other effects.
- A precise fraction of U.S. interceptor stocks has been consumed. The inspected public documents support depletion and multiyear readiness effects, not a usable public denominator.

### Source provenance

- The House Clerk independently establishes the vote. The White House independently establishes only the executive branch's position.
- CBO is the primary cost-estimate source. AP explains the political context but does not independently recreate CBO's estimate.
- The inspector general report is a separate official evidence stream with a different period and method.

### Open questions

- Will the Senate take up the House resolution, and will either chamber pursue binding appropriations limits?
- Will the Defense Department release data that materially revises CBO's estimate or clarifies interceptor replenishment schedules?

### Recommendation

- Include as the lead major development. Keep the political vote, legal effect, budget estimate, and readiness assessment distinct.
