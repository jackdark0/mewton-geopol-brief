# Red-team review — 2026-10-29

## Finding: Iran story still invites countdown reading

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “The warning may be coercive diplomacy rather than a countdown” and the lead ranking in `analysis.md`.
- **Problem:** The strongest evidence is one anonymous diplomatic source chain reported by AP. A reader may treat the existence of preparations, force buildup, and post-election timing as cumulative proof of a decision even though each can be part of contingency planning.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The same report says no final decision was conveyed and the White House declined comment. The Wall Street Journal report may overlap with the same recipients rather than independently confirm presidential intent.
- **Best competing interpretation:** US planners are asking partners to prepare for multiple scenarios while using the possibility of force as leverage; no preferred course has been selected.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Put “no final decision is known” in the headline or first two sentences. Never use “imminent,” “countdown,” or “will strike.”

## Finding: North Korea capability inference exceeds the available geometry

- **Severity:** minor
- **Targets:** The analysis that a roughly 10-missile salvo demonstrates an ability to generate several threats together and increases interceptor burden.
- **Problem:** “About 10” launches do not establish simultaneity, spacing, accuracy, or a saturation profile.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** South Korea supplied the aggregate number and range, but the ledger has no launch interval or trajectory set.
- **Best competing interpretation:** This may have been a sequential training event with little new operational information beyond launch volume.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Say the salvo is relevant to defensive workload if closely coordinated; do not describe a demonstrated saturation attack.

## Finding: Brazil route details are less secure than the legal facts

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “reached Miami after traveling through Argentina and Ecuador” and the possible bilateral-crisis framing.
- **Problem:** The route comes through reporting, while the strongest primary evidence establishes only no recorded Brazilian exit, canceled passports, failure to deliver other passports, and an investigation. There is no warrant or cooperation request.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Federal Police explicitly says the restrictions alone do not make Bolsonaro a fugitive or authorize arrest.
- **Best competing interpretation:** A high-profile political figure may have breached precautionary measures, but the legal system is still determining what occurred; US involvement may remain limited to ordinary visa discretion.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Center the police notice, attribute the route to AP, and state that no formal extradition dispute is established.

## Finding: Ethiopia's “confirmed delivery” rests on a transaction party

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “at least two fighters are acknowledged” and any headline implying independently verified physical delivery.
- **Problem:** Rosoboronexport is the seller, and Ethiopian messaging is the buyer's public narrative. Agreement between transaction parties is not independent verification.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** No imagery, serial-number record, customs document, or independent on-site observation appears in the ledger.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Two aircraft were presented or transferred in some legal sense, while location, readiness, and current inventory remain less certain than the announcement suggests.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Use “Russia's state exporter says it delivered two” or “the parties acknowledge a first two-aircraft batch,” not “independently confirmed.” Make the missing S-400 evidence explicit.

## Finding: Rwanda allegations risk laundering an official case into fact

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** The detailed claims about troops, missiles, jamming, payments, gold transport, and tens of millions of dollars.
- **Problem:** Treasury is authoritative for the sanctions but is also the claimant for the underlying conduct. A legally sufficient designation is not public independent proof of every factual allegation.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The release provides conclusions and sanctions mechanics, not the underlying intelligence, account records, or transaction exhibits.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The United States has high confidence based on nonpublic evidence, but outside readers cannot test the detailed case; Rwanda disputes unilateral blame.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Attribute each underlying conduct claim to Treasury. State that the designation is confirmed and the detailed evidentiary basis is not public.

## Finding: Israel's leverage analysis is arithmetically sound but politically incomplete

- **Severity:** minor
- **Targets:** “Arab-led parties potential leverage” and the suggestion that bargaining may constrain policy.
- **Problem:** Pivotal seats create mathematical leverage only if parties can credibly cooperate. Strong ideological vetoes may leave those seats outside the effective coalition set.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The 53/48/19 split is a projection at 88.6% counted, and no cooperation agreement is verified.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The result produces deadlock rather than leverage if both major Jewish blocs reject dependence on Arab-led parties.
- **Required editorial action:** **add context.** Describe leverage as potential and list deadlock as an equally defensible result.

## Finding: Japan production effects are future tense

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Any phrasing that the agreement “expands production,” “adds capacity,” or “reduces bottlenecks.”
- **Problem:** The primary release establishes technical-information sharing and a planned FACO. It gives no contract, site, target rate, budget, or completion date.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Agreement is not throughput. A final-assembly/check-out line may still depend on imported components and constrained upstream supply.
- **Best competing interpretation:** This is alliance-industrial signaling and preparatory work whose material capacity could remain small or delayed.
- **Required editorial action:** **correct.** Say the allies agreed on steps intended to enable increased production and local assembly. Reserve claims of added capacity for observable output.

## Finding: Cuba realignment language outruns one vote

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “Washington demonstrated that it could change the coalition” and any claim of diplomatic realignment.
- **Problem:** The vote denominator changed, but the evidence does not distinguish durable policy shifts from one-off pressure, tactical abstentions, or issue-specific signaling.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The resolution remained nonbinding and passed 130-17. No follow-on bilateral policy change appears in the ledger.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Washington won a narrower symbolic margin on a recurring vote without changing the embargo's international legitimacy or any government's substantive Cuba policy.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Call it diplomatic erosion in this vote, not a durable realignment.

## Finding: Rapid-fire casualty claims need four-way separation

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Nyala and Yemen items.
- **Problem:** Toll, civilian share, weapon, and perpetrator are separate propositions. Combining them in a compact bullet can make an observed treatment count appear to prove civilian identity and attribution.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** MSF directly counted 36 bodies and more than 150 wounded but did not publish a complete victim list or forensic attribution in the inspected material. The Yemen figure comes from a Houthi authority with no independent corroboration.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Serious attacks occurred, but several strategically important components remain unknown.
- **Required editorial action:** **correct.** For Nyala, state the MSF-observed minimum and separately mark higher toll, victim mix, weapon, and attribution disputed. For Yemen, label both sides' claims and say neither was independently verified.

## Finding: The calendar can silently import post-cutoff information

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** The future `UPCOMING EVENTS` section.
- **Problem:** Checking the calendar at the 07:00 finishing run could introduce announcements made after 02:00, violating the fixed edition cutoff.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Several events, including Israel's count and China's plenum, were still developing around the cutoff.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Use pre-cutoff official schedules only, even if a later update is more current when editing.
- **Required editorial action:** **correct.** Verify event dates from official pages available by the cutoff and do not import post-cutoff outcomes or schedule changes.
