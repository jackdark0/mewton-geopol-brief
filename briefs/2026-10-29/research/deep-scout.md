## Candidate: Japan shifts missile cooperation toward domestic production

- **Underlying development:** Japan and the United States agreed to share technical data to expand SM-3 Block IIA production and to create an AMRAAM final-assembly and checkout capability in Japan.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The meeting's broader alliance language can obscure the industrial change.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Local assembly and production can distribute supply-chain risk, expand surge options, and shorten sustainment routes. It also ties Japan more deeply to the alliance's munition base.
- **What evidence is observable now:** A direct Japanese defense ministry readout names both production initiatives and related flexible-deployment measures.
- **What remains inference:** Actual added output, timing, funding, sites, and whether component bottlenecks are solved.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [Japan Ministry of Defense](https://www.mod.go.jp/en/article/2026/10/8dbc5c2ef47ef3843a13f3bb48dc6b00fff7ba03.html) supports the agreed steps.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does the agreement include funded contracts or only technical preparation? What is the baseline and target annual capacity?

## Candidate: Ethiopia's air-power deal is real but its delivered quantity is not settled

- **Underlying development:** Ethiopia announced a 12-aircraft Su-35 package and four S-400 batteries; Rosoboronexport told Reuters that two Su-35s constituted the first delivery.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines collapse an announced package, a contract, and physical delivery into the same claim.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Initial fighters improve air-superiority potential; a completed S-400 layer would raise the cost of external air operations and alter regional planning. Training, weapons stocks, maintenance, and crew readiness determine usable capability.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Matching Ethiopian and Russian acknowledgment of a first Su-35 delivery, with the Russian exporter specifying two.
- **What remains inference:** That all 12 are in country; any S-400 arrival; operational readiness; delivery schedule; contract value.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [Reuters via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/ethiopia-says-taken-delivery-12-122823000.html) records both official accounts; [Addis Media Network](https://www.amn.gov.et/en/ethiopia-gets-twelve-su-35-fighter-jets-sets-to-receive-air-defense-system-prime-minister/) records the Ethiopian framing.
- **Questions for auditor:** Is there imagery or serial-number evidence? Does the state-media wording describe delivery or the total planned fleet?

## Candidate: Rwanda sanctions target the institutional money-and-command chain

- **Underlying development:** OFAC designated Rwanda's defense ministry and Zigama, a military-linked savings and credit institution, rather than only field commanders.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The headline is another sanctions round; the target selection reaches military finance and the state institution itself.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Blocking transactions can raise the cost of paying, supplying, and shielding forces linked to M23. Effectiveness depends on dollar exposure, partner enforcement, and whether alternate payment channels are available.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Legal designations and Treasury's detailed allegations; Rwanda's public rejection of unilateral blame.
- **What remains inference:** The scale of blocked assets, enforcement outside the US, and whether the action changes Rwandan behavior.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0668/) supports targets and legal rationale; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/congo-rwanda-m23-us-1297c38fde8b5595229fb9700419e098) supports Rwanda's response.
- **Questions for auditor:** Which allegations have public documentary corroboration? What is Zigama's external financial exposure?

## Candidate: Brazil's post-election dispute moves from rhetoric to cross-border legal friction

- **Underlying development:** A defeated presidential candidate reached the United States while Brazilian police say he had not surrendered passports and had no recorded exit through Brazilian controls.

## Candidate: Cuba vote shows US leverage without legal change

- **Why it may be under-covered:** The familiar annual passage can conceal a large movement in the denominator and country alignment.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A weaker margin may reduce Havana's diplomatic insulation and signal that Washington can impose reputational costs. The vote itself does not lift or tighten sanctions.

- **What remains inference:** Whether governments changed substantive Cuba policy or made a one-off vote under US pressure.

## Candidate: Iran contingency warnings create risk before any strike decision

- **Underlying development:** Regional governments were reportedly told to prepare for possible US action after the midterms and Iranian retaliation, while no final presidential decision was conveyed.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Discussion tends to ask only whether a strike is decided; preparations themselves can move forces, markets, and defensive postures.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Advance warning can reduce allied vulnerability and improve deterrence, but it can also compress Iranian decision time and encourage preemption or dispersal.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Two diplomatic accounts of briefings, a substantial US regional buildup, and public partner calls for diplomacy.
- **What remains inference:** Whether the warning reflects a preferred plan, a contingency, or pressure tactics; exact targets and timing.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-iran-strikes-midterm-elections-c7df622f1e87de6035173be8fb8c4add) supports the briefings and caveats.
- **Questions for auditor:** Separate observable deployments from inferred intent and refuse countdown language without a decision source.
