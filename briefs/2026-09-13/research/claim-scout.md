## Claim: The Houthi capture of Perim/Mayun means they now control — or have closed — the Bab el-Mandeb Strait

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The strongest version appears in headline shorthand and commentary describing a Houthi "takeover" of the strait. It rests on local/Yemeni-government accounts of their arrival on Perim/Mayun, plus their prior capture of coastal ground. The Houthis’ own spokesman simultaneously said navigation was safe for all companies except Saudi vessels.
- **Why it matters:** If true as a control claim, it would turn the Red Sea outlet into a second effective oil and trade chokepoint while Hormuz remains disrupted. If overstated, it can incorrectly convert a serious increase in threat and leverage into a completed blockade.
- **Evidence offered for it:** A Reuters report, published September 11, says four Yemeni-government sources reported that the Houthis reached Perim; two government sources said government forces withdrew, and Reuters described the move as tightening their grip on the route. Reuters also records the Houthi spokesman’s Saudi-vessels exception. AP independently reported that two officials said the Houthis captured Mayun after taking Mokha, extending their reach *near* the commercial lane.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Neither inspected report establishes continuous control of both shipping lanes, a legal or physical closure, a maritime-exclusion order effective against all traffic, verified interdictions after the capture, or a traffic denominator showing transit stopped. Reuters’ wording is conditional: *if* the Houthis gain control of the strait. The Houthi reassurance is not independent proof of safety, but it conflicts with the claim of a universal closure.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Reuters’ four Yemeni-government sources and AP’s two anonymous officials are separate reporting efforts, but both substantially rely on participant-side sources; neither is public physical or maritime-traffic confirmation of actual passage control.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible as increased ability to threaten and selectively disrupt traffic; weak as a settled claim that the Houthis control or have closed the strait.** Preserve the territorial-gain core and reject the stronger operational conclusion for now.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** The underlying territorial advance has multiple-source reporting, but the strategic effect is an inference not yet demonstrated by observed shipping outcomes.
- **Sources:**

  - [AP, published Sep. 11, 2026, 17:01 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-houthis-iran-strait-mokha-shipping-ab2e69eb2959979bae44eb4d45ae637e) — **independent reporting; two anonymous officials.** Supports reported captures of Mokha and Mayun and the risk to shipping, while describing forces as near the lane rather than documenting effective control.
  - [AP assessment, published Sep. 12, 2026, 05:13 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-houthis-war-saudi-oil-iran-us-9c2b68921a16bb7bf9a90d31fb7473d9) — **independent analysis/reporting.** Shows the narrative’s strategic salience before cutoff; it is not evidence that a blockade has occurred.
- **Questions for auditor:** Can vessel-tracking or UKMTO/JMIC notices before 06:00 UTC distinguish threat from actual interruption? Are the Reuters and AP territorial accounts independently sourced or ultimately from the same Yemeni-government command? Did the group control both navigable channels or merely deploy on one island/coastline?

## Claim: Houthi assurances make Red Sea navigation safe for all non-Saudi shipping

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree, reported by Reuters on September 11, said navigation remained safe for all shipping except Saudi vessels under a prior ban. The claim is a participant assurance, not a neutral safety finding.
- **Why it matters:** Shipowners, insurers, and governments may treat a claimed exemption as a practical safe-passage guarantee. The distinction matters for diversion, insurance, and escalation calculations at Bab el-Mandeb.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters attributes the statement directly to Saree. The same report says the announced restriction was directed at Saudi vessels, which supports the narrow proposition that the Houthis declared a differentiated policy.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** There is no inspected independent authority or shipping-safety body confirming that non-Saudi vessels were safe, that the Houthis can reliably identify ownership/affiliation, or that attacks, mines, or misidentification risks ceased. The Reuters report describes active fighting and a potential strategic threat; AP says the advance renewed shipping risk. A declared policy does not establish implementation or safety.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for actual safety. The direct speech is evidence only that the spokesman made the assertion; AP’s reporting independently describes risk but does not validate the assurance.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported only as an announced Houthi position; unclear and unverified as a factual safety guarantee.**
- **Confidence:** **High** on the statement; **Low** on real-world safety.
- **Sources:**
  - [Reuters via Investing.com, published Sep. 11, 2026, 14:03](https://ca.investing.com/news/commodities-news/yemens-houthis-reach-strategic-island-at-mouth-of-vital-shipping-lane-4836948) — **participant statement reported independently.** Supports exact policy claim and records the surrounding conflict; no verification of outcome.
  - [AP, published Sep. 11, 2026, 17:01 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-houthis-iran-strait-mokha-shipping-ab2e69eb2959979bae44eb4d45ae637e) — **independent reporting.** Supports continuing concern and notes the attempted reassurance; does not certify navigation safety.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate pre-cutoff UKMTO/JMIC advisories, incident reports, and actual traffic/insurance evidence. Was "Saudi" defined by flag, cargo, beneficial owner, charterer, or port call? Were any non-Saudi ships attacked after the declaration?

## Claim: The September 10 East–West pipeline attack proves Iranian-directed Iraqi-militia responsibility

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The attack has been folded into a wider narrative of coordinated Iranian and allied-group pressure on Saudi oil infrastructure. It extends a confirmed attack into separate claims of Iraqi-militia authorship and Iranian direction.
- **Why it matters:** Attribution could justify Saudi retaliation, draw Baghdad into the regional war, and reshape the security of Saudi Arabia’s remaining oil-export bypass. Conflating launch location with command risks escalating on evidence that does not yet establish the actor.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Saudi Arabia’s state news agency said the Energy Ministry’s official source reported multiple September 10 attacks in the Riyadh and Madinah regions, injuries, and a precautionary pipeline shutdown. Reuters reported satellite imagery it verified showing smoke in the pipeline vicinity on September 10. Those facts make the attack itself real and strategically significant; they do not identify its perpetrator.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The SPA Energy Ministry release does not identify the drones’ origin, an armed group, Iran, or a command chain. Reuters reported no Saudi confirmation of the incident when it first saw the imagery. Publicly inspectable pre-cutoff material does not supply launch-site imagery, debris/forensics, a claimed responsibility statement, flight-track data, or an Iranian order. Evidence that an attack occurred is not evidence for the full attribution ladder.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial** for the attack and shutdown (Saudi primary record plus Reuters-verified imagery); **no independent corroboration located before cutoff** for perpetrator identity or Iranian direction.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported that the pipeline was attacked and shut as a precaution; weak/unsupported that this proves Iraqi-militia or Iranian responsibility.**
- **Confidence:** **High** on attack/shutdown; **Low** on perpetrator and command attribution.
- **Sources:**
  - [Saudi Press Agency / Energy Ministry, Sep. 11, 2026](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2674017) — **primary official record.** Supports multiple attacks, injuries, and precautionary shutdown; it does not attribute responsibility or origin.
  - [Reuters via Investing.com, published Sep. 11, 2026, 14:03](https://ca.investing.com/news/commodities-news/yemens-houthis-reach-strategic-island-at-mouth-of-vital-shipping-lane-4836948) — **independent reporting; verified satellite imagery.** Supports smoke near the pipeline and contemporaneous operational concern; it reported no Saudi confirmation at that time and provides no perpetrator attribution.

- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the Saudi Foreign Ministry’s original statement and any Iraqi primary investigation statement with a timestamp before cutoff. What physical evidence, if any, identifies a launch point? Is there evidence separating a place of launch from the responsible group, supplier, or commander?

## Claim: Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps directly guided the Houthi offensive to create a second shipping front

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters, citing Yemeni-government, Iranian, and regional sources, reported that the advance came with direct IRGC guidance; two Iranian sources said Tehran told the Houthis to escalate against Saudi Arabia and promised additional funding, weapons, and senior officers. The proposition has been amplified as an explanation for the Houthi advance.
- **Why it matters:** Direct tactical guidance would be stronger evidence of interstate command in a widening war than the better-established, but distinct, proposition that Iran supports the Houthis. It bears on deterrence, target selection, and responsibility for attacks beyond Yemen.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Reuters account includes multiple source categories, describes alleged guidance, and reports a claimed offer of more support. The geographic timing of the advance and its apparent benefit to Iranian pressure strategy make the allegation strategically coherent, but coherence is not verification.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Reuters also records Iran’s public denial that it gives military direction and says Tehran describes the Houthis as an ally rather than proxy. No public order, intercept, adviser identity, travel record, weapons-transfer document, captured material, or independently verified command-and-control evidence was inspected. The Houthi capacity to act in parallel with Iranian interests does not alone demonstrate Iranian command.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No / unknown.** The cited source categories sit inside one Reuters reporting chain; no separate primary or independent evidentiary stream was recovered before cutoff.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible but unclear.** Iranian support is a different, less demanding claim; the direct-guidance allegation should not be converted into a settled fact.
- **Confidence:** **Low-to-medium.** Reuters is a high-quality outlet, but this high-stakes attribution rests on anonymous sources with no public corroborating record.
- **Sources:**
  - [Reuters via Investing.com, published Sep. 11, 2026, 14:03](https://ca.investing.com/news/commodities-news/yemens-houthis-reach-strategic-island-at-mouth-of-vital-shipping-lane-4836948) — **investigative reporting; anonymous Yemeni-government, Iranian, and regional sources in one lineage.** Supports attribution that sources made the allegation and reports Iran’s public denial; it does not independently prove command.
  - [AP, published Sep. 11, 2026, 17:01 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-houthis-iran-strait-mokha-shipping-ab2e69eb2959979bae44eb4d45ae637e) — **independent reporting.** Supports Houthi territorial moves and strategic risk, but supplies no proof of Iranian command.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek a second, genuinely independent account, source documents, imagery or communications evidence. Does any source distinguish strategic encouragement, materiel support, operational advice, and direct command? What counterevidence could establish autonomous Houthi decision-making?

## Claim: The BRICS New Delhi declaration is a united endorsement of Iran and a direct condemnation of U.S.–Israeli policy

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Commentary around the September 12 summit frames the joint statement as proof that the expanded bloc has lined up behind Iran against the West. AP reported that the statement expressed concern over attacks on civilian infrastructure and safeguarded peaceful nuclear facilities, and opposed unilateral sanctions not authorized by the UN Security Council.
- **Why it matters:** The claim affects how much diplomatic isolation or coalition-building should be inferred from the statement. A unanimous communiqué can be politically meaningful without committing governments to military support, sanctions evasion, or a specific attribution of blame.
- **Evidence offered for it:** AP, published at 04:18:50 UTC — inside the cutoff — reports the leaders’ statement, including its concern over attacks on civilian infrastructure and peaceful safeguarded nuclear facilities and its condemnation of unilateral sanctions lacking UNSC authorization. Iran’s president attended; the language aligns with Iranian interests on those issues.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The AP account says the declaration did not name countries; the article characterizes its criticism of U.S.–Israeli strikes as indirect. It establishes neither a formal endorsement of Iran’s conduct nor operational commitments by BRICS states. The official full declaration available after the cutoff was not used, consistent with the frozen-source rule; this leaves exact text, signatory reservations, and implementation commitments for the audit stage.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** The political act and its reported wording come from one inspected AP report. No pre-cutoff primary text was recovered and inspected for this scout file.
- **Current assessment:** **Qualified / materially overstated when rendered as unanimous backing for Iran or direct condemnation of named states.** The defensible core is a consensus statement that criticizes categories of conduct and sanctions in language congenial to Iran and Russia.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** The AP account is timely and specific, but the primary text was unavailable within the frozen-source constraint.
- **Sources:**
  - [AP, published Sep. 12, 2026, 04:18:50 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/india-brics-modi-russia-china-iran-wars-5393274f87a16461b195d13f324e747a) — **independent reporting based on the summit statement.** Supports the reported language, the absence of named countries, and the indirect-characterization limitation.
- **Questions for auditor:** Can a pre-cutoff official copy or recording of the adopted text be recovered? Which states formally endorsed each paragraph, and are there reservations? Does the text create any mechanism, funding, or policy commitment beyond declaratory language?
