## Candidate: D01 — OFAC keeps specific Lukoil operations legally open into April 2027

- **Underlying development:** FACT / legal act: On 15 October OFAC issued replacement licenses 128D and 130B. D01a: 128D authorizes specified transactions connected with Lukoil International retail stations outside Russia through 24 April 2027, 00:01 EDT. D01b: 130B authorizes specified transactions involving four named Bulgarian Lukoil entities and their qualifying subsidiaries to the same deadline. These are separate permissions with different exceptions.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The broader diesel and shadow-fleet disputes can obscure the immediate legal treatment of a European refinery, aviation supply, bunkering, and retail operations. This is a published authorization rather than an anonymous policy possibility.
- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: A longer authorization horizon can reduce banks' and suppliers' immediate withdrawal incentives and preserve non-Russian fuel logistics. The countervailing mechanism is reduced urgency to dispose of sanctioned ownership interests. Neither effect is measured here.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Both dated license texts are public. The Bulgaria text names Neftohim Burgas, Lukoil Bulgaria, Lukoil Aviation Bulgaria, and Lukoil Bulgaria Bunker, plus entities they own at least 50% individually or collectively. 128D excludes transfers to Russian persons/accounts; 130B does not authorize otherwise prohibited dealings with other blocked persons. Support and narrowing therefore come from the operative paragraphs, not the action-page headline.
- **What remains inference:** Commercial response, refinery utilization, actual payment flows, and Washington's motive. Do not call this delisting, blanket permission for Russian oil, or proof that money may freely return to Russia. The prior licenses are superseded, but their entire old wording was not compared; avoid asserting the exact incremental duration or all changes until the auditor retrieves them.
- **Initial priority:** high. Triage dimensions strategic/escalation/US/novelty/discussion/uncertainty = 4/2/4/3/3/2; weighted priority 30.
- **Sources:**
  - [OFAC action notice](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261015) — primary legal-action index, dated 15 October 2026; links both licenses. One OFAC lineage, not independent corroboration of effects.
  - [General License 128D](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937236/download?inline=) — primary two-page instrument, dated/effective 15 October; page 1 paragraphs (a)-(c) establish retail scope, expiry and exclusions; page 2 supersedes 128C.
  - [General License 130B](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937241/download?inline=) — primary one-page instrument, dated/effective 15 October; paragraphs (a)-(c) establish Bulgarian scope, expiry and exclusions.
- **Questions for auditor:** Compare 128C/130A to replacements; check FAQ 1225 and interaction with other licenses. Seek Bulgarian regulator/company/bank response and independently observed operations. How much supply is actually at risk? Verify exact license time wording before quoting it.
- **Provisional disposition:** D01a/b SUPPORTED, High confidence for published authorization; operational and bargaining effects remain ASSESSMENT, Moderate confidence in mechanism, Low on measured magnitude. Narrowing search for `Lukoil 130B October 15` located derivative and AI-labelled summaries; these were excluded as independent evidence.

## Candidate: D02 — Washington targets an Iraqi bank and Iran's foreign-currency conduits

- **Underlying development:** FACT: Treasury announced 15 October designations including Iraq-based International Development Bank for Investment and Finance (IDB), plus Iranian offshore banks. D02a is the designation itself. D02b is Treasury's separate allegation that IDB supplied Iran with dollar access and had militia-related ties. D02c: OFAC's GL FF permits limited wind-down through 30 October 2026, 00:01 EDT, requiring payments to blocked persons to enter blocked accounts.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Tankers draw attention, but Iran's ability to turn sales into usable foreign currency is a different constraint. An Iraqi banking channel also places pressure on Baghdad and correspondent banks.
- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: Blocking intermediaries can raise conversion and settlement costs even without changing physical oil production. Banks may pre-emptively withdraw from adjacent relationships. Substitution into other networks could blunt the effect; Treasury's enforcement action does not establish successful financial isolation.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Public designation record and wind-down text establish legal status. Treasury describes IDB and two offshore banks as financial conduits. Shafaq independently published an article at 18:59:11 UTC on 15 October, establishing that the announcement was public before cutoff; its account derives from Treasury and supplies no independent banking evidence.
- **What remains inference:** Truth of alleged underlying transactions, size of continuing flows, bank solvency, deposit access, Iraqi government response, and proxy financing effects. Do not repeat Treasury's claim of severed revenue as an observed result.
- **Initial priority:** high. Dimensions 4/3/4/3/3/3; weighted priority 32.
- **Sources:**
  - [Treasury announcement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0660) — primary interested-government source, 15 October; IDB designation and Treasury's allegations, especially the Iraqi-bank section. Capable of establishing U.S. action and stated rationale, not independent proof of covert flows.
  - [GL FF](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937246/download?inline=) — primary one-page legal permission, dated 15 October, paragraphs (a)-(b); wind-down conditions and deadline. Same U.S. government lineage.
  - [Shafaq report](https://www.shafaq.com/en/World/US-sanctions-Iraqi-bank-in-Iran-linked-network) — 15 October 18:59:11 UTC; contemporaneous publication and attribution check, explicitly derivative of Treasury on transaction allegations.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain Central Bank of Iraq and IDB responses and any correspondent-bank notices. Which accounts and currency settlement routes actually close? Can any disputed Treasury allegation be checked independently? Keep designation, conduct allegation, and effectiveness as three separate claims.
- **Provisional disposition:** D02a/c SUPPORTED, High confidence in legal records. D02b remains an attributed CLAIM, Moderate confidence that Treasury made it; underlying conduct HOLD pending independent evidence. Two-sided search included bank response and denial variants; no inspected independent response resolved the allegations.

## Candidate: D03 — France and the Netherlands institutionalize a nuclear-deterrence dialogue

D03a: a formal meeting took place. D03b: the Dutch government says contemplated cooperation supplements NATO collective defence and deterrence.

- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: Regular consultation could build shared concepts, identify conventional support requirements, and make European deterrence cooperation easier to operationalize. A small first meeting could also remain diplomatic signalling with little practical result.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The French presidency describes officials from both leaders' offices and defence/foreign ministries. The Dutch article frames the dialogue as strengthening nuclear and conventional deterrence while retaining NATO's role. The French record says a way forward was agreed but gives no public operational detail.

- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: Selective permissions can preserve leverage over industrial inputs while rewarding European engagement. ## Candidate: D06 — Britain commits repair funding for Chornobyl's damaged confinement structure

- **Underlying development:** FACT / announced contribution: Britain announced £17 million on 15 October for Chornobyl repairs in an EBRD-coordinated programme with Ukraine and G7 donors. D06a is the pledge. D06b, restored confinement/ventilation, is the work's intended outcome, not an achieved condition.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Nuclear safety here concerns maintaining a protective structure rather than electricity generation or a newly operating reactor. Slow deterioration can create liabilities long after immediate attack headlines fade.
- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: Funding can sustain engineering work and reduce the probability of deferred-maintenance failure. Continued donor coordination matters because repair financing, access and execution are separate bottlenecks. This does not demonstrate an imminent radioactive release.
- **What evidence is observable now:** UK announcement specifies the contribution and intended confinement/ventilation work. Its technical rationale is attributed to the donor; no inspected engineering assessment independently establishes condition or risk magnitude. The pledge is new even though the damage occurred earlier.
- **What remains inference:** Funds disbursed, work completed, total repair gap and safety-risk reduction. No causal link to deterrence of future attacks is demonstrated. Do not combine pounds with a euro programme total without reconciling accounting and pledge dates.
- **Initial priority:** medium. Dimensions 3/2/2/2/2/2; weighted priority 21.
- **Sources:**
  - [UK government announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/britain-funds-critical-repairs-to-chornobyl-nuclear-power-plant-damaged-by-russian-drone-attack) — primary donor statement published 15 October; funding paragraphs and repair purpose. Other wartime numerical claims in this release were not adopted without separate verification.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain EBRD/plant engineering document, contract milestone and disbursement evidence. What repair functions remain degraded? Is UK funding additional to earlier pledges? Avoid retrospective certainty on attack attribution based only on donor wording.
- **Provisional disposition:** D06a SUPPORTED, High confidence in announced contribution; D06b HOLD as achieved repair. Search for completion and total repair programme found secondary pledge reporting, not completion evidence.

- **Strategic mechanism:** ASSESSMENT: Broad flag-based costs could create commercial pressure for a diplomatic settlement and affect routing or registration incentives. They could also harden Spain's position and widen the dispute. No retaliation outcome is known.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Published docket FMC-2026-0463 / 26-14, comment deadline 14 December, and opportunity for hearing. The agency's release was posted 14 October and updated 15 October; the 15 October formal publication is the window anchor. The notice includes Spain's jurisdictional/legal objections and FMC's response, making the dispute legible without treating either party's legal view as a court judgment.
- **What remains inference:** Final adoption, legal survival, fees paid, final import coverage, and trade losses. Underlying confidential submissions are not public; the commission's account of Spain is not a substitute for Spain's original filing. Avoid treating all Spanish ships as weapons carriers.
- **Initial priority:** medium. Dimensions 3/2/4/3/3/2; weighted priority 27.
