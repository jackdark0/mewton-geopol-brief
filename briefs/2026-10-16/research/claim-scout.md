## Claim: C-01 — The instruction restricting Ukraine's Starshield service came from inside the White House

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters' October 15 investigation (S1), attributed to unnamed U.S. and Western officials. Analyst paraphrase of its reported proposition; empirical attribution claim. Attribution confidence high that Reuters reported it; underlying command provenance is not public.
- **Why it matters:** This would change yesterday's unknown-decision-maker assessment and identify a U.S. executive dependency affecting allied operations. It still would not identify a particular individual or motive.
- **Evidence offered for it:** S1 reports that Pentagon leaders received a slowdown message from within the White House; an additional Ukrainian account says Kyiv was told the decision came from there.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No order, signatory, transmission record, or implementation log. A senior U.S. official denies that the president requested it; this does not exclude another White House official. Bureaucratic origin and presidential authorization are separate propositions.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial. Multiple officials within one Reuters investigation; shared briefing cannot be excluded.
- **Current assessment:** plausible. Pangburn mapping: unsupported as a settled fact; supported only as attributed reporting. Preserve the distinction.
- **Confidence:** medium in the preliminary classification; anonymity and absent records prevent an upgrade.
- **Sources:** S1/L1 above; S2/L2 provides a separate public response but does not confirm the order's origin.
- **Questions for auditor:** Find a primary U.S. response or order; determine whether sources have distinct direct access. Do not turn this into a finding that Trump personally ordered punishment. What evidence would distinguish a White House policy decision from an operational implementation instruction?
- **Publication-safe correction:** Reuters reports a White House origin; the responsible official and implementation chain remain unidentified.
- **Audit status / reversal condition:** citation passages inspected; independent attribution and adversarial passes pending. A named official/document or a correction could change the verdict. Audio/video check required before quoting interview speech beyond the written report.

- **Current assessment:** weak for the broad primarily-debris proposition; do not code every individual debris explanation false. Pangburn mapping: qualified/unsupported generalization, with site-level findings requiring separate audit.

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Potential overreading of the newly issued policy announcement; no inspected actor is accused of using this exact wording. Analyst test proposition; quantitative policy claim.

- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The record explicitly identifies this as completion of the March action. S8 already distinguished faster delivery from supplemental volumes. The existence of some overdelivery does not make the whole remainder additional.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes for the decision's wording through the authoritative primary record; no independent audit of delivered quantities.
- **Current assessment:** false as stated. Pangburn mapping: false for the wholly-new-100-million framing; preserve the real acceleration decision.
- **Confidence:** high on the policy-text distinction; medium on realized supply because national delivery schedules remain unstated.
- **Sources:** S7/L6 operative bullets and S8/L6 background. Same institution, not two independent measurement systems.
- **Questions for auditor:** Find national shipment calendars and incremental volumes beyond March pledges. Distinguish authorization, drawdown, tender, loading, and consumer delivery; identify crude versus diesel.
