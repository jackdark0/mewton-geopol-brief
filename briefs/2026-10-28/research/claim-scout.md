## Method and provenance key

Claims are written as atomic propositions. Verdict and confidence are separate. The evidence packets distinguish what a source is competent to establish from whether the underlying proposition is true.

- **Primary claim source:** the actor or institution making the claim, or a direct copy of its statement.
- **Independent reporting:** reporting based on observation, document access, interviews, or another evidence stream rather than mere repetition.
- **Derivative:** repeats another outlet or official statement and is not independent corroboration.
- **Single-chain:** multiple reports ultimately depend on one source, document, witness, or institutional assertion.
- **Discovery-only excluded:** a lead that was not used as evidence.

## Claim: CENTCOM's October 16 accounting put U.S. military costs of the Iran war at least $47.1 billion through mid-October

- **Precise proposition:** A U.S. Central Command estimate dated October 16 calculated at least $47.1 billion in U.S. military costs for the Iran war through mid-October: about $29 billion for replacing expended munitions, $6.1 billion for lost equipment, and $11.9 billion in incremental operating costs.
- **Claim type:** quantitative / economic impact / military expenditure.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** CENTCOM prepared the estimate and provided it to lawmakers. Reuters, CBS News, and The Hill each reported obtaining or reviewing the estimate during the research window.
- **Why it matters:** The figure measures the fiscal and munitions burden of the war as Congress considers supplemental funding and as the administration considers renewed heavy fighting. It also bears on claims that the war is inexpensive or self-financing.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters reported Pentagon data showing more than $47 billion and gave the $29 billion and $6 billion subcomponents. CBS reported a $47.1 billion total with the same three components and an October 16 cutoff. The Hill likewise reported an October 16 CENTCOM estimate supplied to Congress. The close agreement in date and components strongly supports the existence and contents of the internal estimate.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The underlying CENTCOM document was not public in an inspected official repository, and the Pentagon had not publicly commented by the cutoff. The estimate excludes damage to U.S. bases; it also is not an all-in social cost including deaths, injuries, veterans' care, interest, macroeconomic effects, or other agencies. A September CBO estimate of more than $38 billion through August 1 is compatible with, but does not independently validate, CENTCOM's later accounting method. The components are cost estimates and obligations/replacement estimates, not necessarily cash already paid.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Reuters, CBS, and The Hill appear to have independently accessed the same primary CENTCOM estimate. That is strong corroboration of what the document says but remains one accounting lineage, not three independent cost calculations.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported, narrowly.** Supported as a CENTCOM estimate through October 16; misleading if presented as a complete all-in cost already spent.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the estimate exists and contains the reported figures; **medium** on its completeness and accounting accuracy because the document and methodology were not public.
- **Sources:**
  - **Independent document reporting:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/us-costs-of-iran-war-rise-to-over-47-billion-according-to-pentagon-estimate-4977332) — reports the Pentagon estimate, its comparison with July, its main categories, and the exclusion of base-repair costs.
  - **Independent document reporting:** [CBS News, October 27, 23:29 EDT](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/iran-war-cost-centcom-47-billion/) — reports obtaining the CENTCOM estimate and gives the $47.1 billion total, three components, October timing, and exclusions.
  - **Independent document reporting, same primary record:** [The Hill via AOL, October 27](https://www.aol.com/articles/iran-war-cost-increases-47-233242000.html) — identifies the estimate as dated October 16 and provided to Congress.
  - **Independent baseline, different method:** [AP on the September CBO assessment](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-cbo-estimate-b99089b11749f04bbc8ddb1b3c64a373) — establishes the earlier more-than-$38-billion baseline and CBO's stated uncertainty and exclusions.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the October 16 CENTCOM document or a congressional copy; determine whether Reuters, CBS, and The Hill independently obtained it; reconcile whether the reported $11.9 billion is obligated or estimated; check for overlap among personnel support and incremental-cost categories; identify every excluded cost class.

## Claim: Jordan's October 26 strikes in southern Syria killed more than 20 Islamic State operatives

- **Precise proposition:** Jordanian airstrikes in southern Syria on October 26 killed more than 20 members of Islamic State.
- **Claim type:** casualty / target attribution / military effectiveness.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Jordanian government spokesperson Mohammad al-Momani, through the state-run Petra news agency, called the number an initial operational assessment.
- **Why it matters:** The casualty and identity claim is the core justification for a cross-border use of force that killed civilians and strained relations between two U.S. partners. If the targets were not Islamic State operatives, the operational and legal narrative changes materially.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Petra reported that the strikes used field intelligence against imminent threats, killed more than 20 Islamic State operatives, and destroyed a weapons depot. Jordan acknowledged unintended civilian casualties. No names, bodies, unit identities, recovered weapons, imagery showing militants at the struck sites, or post-strike methodology were disclosed.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Syrian civil defense reported recovering seven civilian bodies, including three children and a woman. AP journalists photographed and interviewed mourners at the funeral of five members of one family and inspected a strike site. Syria's account documents civilian harm but does not, by itself, disprove militant deaths at other sites. The October 26 Jordanian military statement described many militants killed or wounded but did not identify Islamic State or give a count. Jordanian authorities had also said the three men arrested for the preceding Karak church attack were not tied to an organized group, leaving the asserted operational link to Islamic State insufficiently explained in public.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for the more-than-20 Islamic State death toll. There is independent corroboration of the strikes and at least some civilian deaths, not of Jordan's militant count or identities.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear.** The claim is possible but rests on an undisclosed Jordanian intelligence and battle-damage assessment.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that Jordan made the assessment; **low** that the public record establishes the number and identities.
- **Sources:**
  - **Primary claim source:** [Petra, October 27](https://petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-reaffirms-deep-institutional-security-coordination-with-syria-against-shared-threats) — gives Jordan's initial estimate, asserted intelligence basis, weapons-depot claim, and acknowledgment of unintended civilian casualties.
  - **Independent on-the-ground reporting:** [AP, October 27](https://apnews.com/article/syria-jordan-strikes-karak-daraa-is-5c278be71a23176a092779ef8484c213) — documents the Syrian civilian toll, funerals, a strike site, the joint investigation, and the lack of a reconciled casualty account.
  - **Independent reporting of the first official accounts:** [AP, October 26](https://apnews.com/article/jordan-church-shooting-karak-arrests-c3f87a077a08b4fcf7a15b169a83a6b5) — shows that Jordan's initial statement did not name Islamic State or provide a count and reports the Jordanian security chief's statement that the arrested attackers were not tied to an organized group.
  - **Independent synthesis, same official lineages:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/jordan-says-it-killed-more-than-20-islamic-state-militants-in-airstrikes-on-syria-4975926) — juxtaposes Jordan's militant claim, Syria's seven-civilian account, and local witness testimony.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek target folders, names, forensic identifications, strike coordinates, and satellite imagery; determine whether the claimed weapons-depot site is distinct from the documented civilian sites; ask how Jordan linked the struck people to Islamic State and to an imminent threat.

## Claim: Jordan conducted the October 26 airstrikes with prior Syrian coordination

- **Precise proposition:** Jordan notified or coordinated with the Syrian government before conducting its October 26 airstrikes in Daraa and Sweida.
- **Claim type:** diplomatic / operational coordination / sovereignty.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters reported that the Jordanian military said the strikes were conducted in coordination with Damascus. Syrian authorities directly denied prior coordination or notification.
- **Why it matters:** Prior consent would materially affect the bilateral and sovereignty dispute and the credibility of both governments' public accounts. Post-strike coordination is not evidence of pre-strike consent.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Jordan points to years of bilateral security coordination and said authorities were coordinating with Syrian counterparts to investigate the incident. The two foreign ministers agreed after the strikes to form a joint committee and develop future field and intelligence coordination. None of those public details establishes notification before the attack.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Syria's government said the operation occurred without prior coordination or notification, condemned it as a sovereignty violation, and temporarily closed the main border crossing. No inspected source supplied a timestamped notice, Syrian authorization, joint command record, or named Syrian official confirming advance consent.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No.** The two governments give incompatible accounts, and reporting traces back to those official statements.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak.** Public evidence does not establish prior coordination and Damascus directly denies it. This is not rated false because operational communications could remain undisclosed.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** The contradiction is clear; the underlying communications are not public.
- **Sources:**
  - **Primary Jordanian position / narrowing evidence:** [Petra, October 27](https://petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-reaffirms-deep-institutional-security-coordination-with-syria-against-shared-threats) — establishes past cooperation and post-strike investigation, but does not document advance consent.
  - **Independent reporting quoting both official lineages:** [AP, October 27](https://apnews.com/article/syria-jordan-strikes-karak-daraa-is-5c278be71a23176a092779ef8484c213) — reports Syria's direct denial of prior coordination and the later joint committee.
  - **Independent reporting of the initial dispute:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/jordan-says-it-killed-more-than-20-islamic-state-militants-in-airstrikes-on-syria-4975926) — records Jordan's coordination claim and Syria's denial in the same account.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the full Jordanian military statement and Syrian Foreign Ministry statement in original language; distinguish general counterterrorism liaison from strike-specific consent; identify any notice time, channel, recipient, or Syrian air-defense deconfliction record.

## Claim: El Salvador had deployed about 1,000 troops to Haiti by October 27

- **Precise proposition:** El Salvador sent approximately 1,000 military personnel to Haiti in the days after the October 18 gang ambush, dramatically expanding its contingent from at least 124 personnel.
- **Claim type:** force deployment / quantitative / intervention scope.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** One unnamed Haitian security official told Reuters that about 1,000 Salvadoran troops had arrived. Neither government publicly confirmed the number by the cutoff.
- **Why it matters:** If accurate, the reinforcement approaches half the roughly 2,000-person UN-backed Gang Suppression Force and may alter its command, rules of engagement, and relationship with Haitian forces and private contractors.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters cited the Haitian official, a U.S. military official who confirmed C-130 transport support, visible commercial and military flights, a lawmaker's photograph of uniformed soldiers aboard an aircraft, and Miami Herald reporting of the flights. AP independently confirmed an earlier 100-person special-forces arrival and a planned additional 100, while the Gang Suppression Force acknowledged that a Salvadoran contingent arrived. El Salvador's legislature had already authorized personnel, aircraft, and equipment according to mission needs.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The precise 1,000 figure is single-source. Public statements did not supply manifests, flight loads, unit names, arrival rolls, command arrangements, or mission duration. Flight activity and photographs show reinforcement but do not establish the total. Earlier confirmed figures of 100 to 200 do not contradict later arrivals, but they leave a large verification gap.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Multiple evidence streams corroborate a substantial reinforcement and U.S.-assisted airlift; only the anonymous Haitian official establishes the approximate 1,000 total.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible.** The movement is well supported, but the scale remains unconfirmed.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** on a major reinforcement; **low-to-medium** on the 1,000-person count.
- **Sources:**
  - **Single-chain origin plus observable corroboration:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/around-1000-soldiers-from-el-salvador-have-deployed-to-haiti-source-says-4976576) — provides the 1,000 figure, U.S. transport confirmation, flight-tracking evidence, image description, and both governments' lack of comment.
  - **Independent reporting:** [AP, October 22](https://apnews.com/article/el-salvador-haiti-special-forces-gangs-03ec2bf89616d8b313c54da34c24eab1) — independently establishes the first 100-person arrival, another planned 100, and the force's acknowledgment of a contingent.
  - **Primary legal authorization / does not establish actual count:** [El Salvador Legislative Assembly, September 29](https://asamblea.gob.sv/node/14133) — authorizes military and civilian personnel, aircraft, and other resources for the mission for an initial twelve months.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain arrival manifests and flight tail numbers; ask the Gang Suppression Force, Haitian government, Salvadoran defense ministry, and U.S. Transportation Command for headcounts; separate troops from aircrew, technicians, police, contractors, and rotating personnel; establish who commands them and whether all are inside the UN-backed force.

## Claim: A hostile hacker or hackers caused the FEC electronic-filing outage that began October 26

- **Precise proposition:** The Federal Election Commission took web-facing services offline because a malicious actor was attempting to breach its systems.
- **Claim type:** election integrity / cyber incident / attribution.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters reviewed an email in which an FEC electronic-filings officer told compliance firms that a bad actor was attempting to breach FEC systems and that services were shut down in response.
- **Why it matters:** The outage temporarily blocked 24- and 48-hour campaign-spending filings during the final week before U.S. midterms. A cyber incident against disclosure infrastructure is consequential, but it is distinct from an intrusion into voter registration, ballot casting, counting, or certification systems.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The internal FEC email provides a near-primary account of the operational response. The FEC's official site and status page independently confirm a major electronic-filing outage beginning late October 26, continuing through the cutoff, and an identified issue with a fix being implemented by 20:17 EDT on October 27.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The FEC's public notices did not identify a cyberattack, successful breach, actor, method, compromised data, or foreign connection. FEC officials declined to tell The Washington Post whether the outage was a hack. The public record therefore supports an outage and an internal suspicion/response, not successful compromise or attribution. The FEC does not administer voting, and the outage did not prevent ballot casting.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** The outage is independently confirmed. The hostile-actor cause is **single-chain**, resting on the internal email Reuters inspected.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible.** A hostile attempt is supported by the contemporaneous internal email, but a completed breach and any attribution remain unestablished.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** on an attempted intrusion; **low** on breach success, attacker identity, motive, or state involvement.
- **Sources:**
  - **Near-primary internal record reported by an independent outlet:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/hacker-trying-to-breach-us-federal-election-commission-system-email-shows-4976625) — reports the internal email, official FEC statement, outage effects, and the FEC's non-role in voting administration.
  - **Primary operational record:** [FEC status page](https://fecgov.statuspage.io/) — timestamps the investigation and confirms major outages in e-filing, online forms, and submission systems without publicly naming a cause.
  - **Primary public notice:** [FEC homepage](https://www.fec.gov/) — confirms the filing system was offline while campaign-finance search remained available.
  - **Independent reporting / contrary-to-certainty evidence:** [The Washington Post, October 27](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/27/fecs-filing-system-is-down-week-before-election-agency-wont-say-why/) — reports that the FEC declined to identify the cause or say whether it was a hack.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the email headers and full distribution; ask whether the bad actor triggered automated defenses or was inferred from logs; determine whether access occurred, what systems or data were exposed, whether CISA/FBI opened an incident, and whether the event affected filing deadlines or only disclosure timing.

## Claim: The five people killed in the October 27 U.S. strike on an eastern Pacific boat were active narcotraffickers or “narco-terrorists”

- **Precise proposition:** A go-fast boat struck by U.S. forces in the eastern Pacific on October 27 was actively engaged in narcotrafficking, and all five people killed aboard were properly identified as narcotraffickers or “narco-terrorists.”
- **Claim type:** target attribution / casualty / legal justification.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. Southern Command said unspecified confirmed intelligence established the vessel's active involvement and labeled the dead “narco-terrorists.”
- **Why it matters:** The claimed status of the vessel and people is the factual premise for a lethal military strike. The campaign has killed more than 230 people, while human-rights organizations dispute both its legal basis and the government's evidentiary transparency.
- **Evidence offered for it:** SOUTHCOM published a video showing a small boat moving before an explosion and asserted that it was traveling on established narcotrafficking routes. The video establishes a strike on a vessel; it does not, on its face, establish cargo, identities, organization, threat, or intent. No names, seizure, cargo imagery, coordinates, warning attempt, partner-government evidence, or intelligence summary was released.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** AP reported that the military supplied no evidence the vessel was carrying drugs. Reuters likewise noted that SOUTHCOM gave no details beyond the intelligence assertion. Human Rights Watch's September review found that the administration had not released information showing that victims in this campaign posed an imminent threat and argued that the strikes fall under international human-rights law rather than an armed-conflict targeting framework. That legal assessment does not prove this boat was innocent; it identifies the missing factual and legal showings.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for the drug cargo or the identities/status of the five people. The strike itself and the government's claim are independently reported, but all target-status reporting traces to SOUTHCOM.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear.** The public record establishes the strike and official allegation, not the narcotrafficking status of the vessel or all five dead.
- **Confidence:** **High** that SOUTHCOM made the claim and carried out the strike; **low** on the undisclosed target attribution.
- **Sources:**
  - **Primary claim source:** [SOUTHCOM post, October 27](https://x.com/Southcom/status/2115116783199735980) — contains the official assertion and strike video; the post was inspected through its direct embed because X blocked direct retrieval.
  - **Independent reporting:** [AP, October 27](https://apnews.com/article/boat-strikes-alleged-drug-boat-latin-america-cartels-trump-ef5e229630e00f370e0d1772edc34dd9) — confirms the announced strike, explains what the video shows, and states that no drug evidence was provided.
  - **Independent reporting, same official lineage:** [Reuters, October 27](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/us-military-says-it-struck-vessel-in-eastern-pacific-killing-five-4977407) — reproduces the claim and records the lack of disclosed intelligence detail.
  - **Specialist legal/advocacy analysis:** [Human Rights Watch, September 2](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/09/02/united-states-year-of-extrajudicial-killings-at-sea) — supplies the strongest contrary legal and evidentiary argument for the wider campaign; it is not incident-specific proof about the October 27 vessel.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek the target packet, identities, cargo evidence, chain of custody, coordinates, flag/registration, organization designation, partner-country consent, and assessment of imminent threat; separate the empirical drug-status question from the distinct legal question of whether lethal force was permissible.
