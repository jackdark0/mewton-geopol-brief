## Overall finding

The proposed six-story hierarchy is defensible, but the edition is vulnerable to three headline errors: turning Trump's Iran statement into a peace declaration, turning France's attribution into independently proved state direction, and turning the opening of Abu Dhabi talks into negotiating progress. The final editor must keep those distinctions visible in headlines, dek text, and bullets—not bury them in caveats.

No candidate requires removal for lack of a source. Taiwan and Hegseth require especially strict attribution. Cyprus should stay short. The Rapid Fire list must not imply effectiveness from designation counts or calculate piracy trends from incompatible denominators.

## Story-by-story adversarial tests

### 1. Iran

**Strongest plausible countercase:** Trump's “effectively over” language could be theater designed to calm markets or improve bargaining leverage. The blockade remains an act of coercion, Washington rejected Iran's existing plan, and the option to strike is explicit. Nothing in the public record shows an operational stand-down, reciprocal Iranian action, or a mutually accepted sequence.

**Risk in current analysis:** “Reduces the immediate probability” is a reasonable assessment, but it is not directly measurable. Present it as the implication of the public signal, not a quantified intelligence judgment.

**Required edit controls:**

- Headline must include the continuing blockade or “for now.”
- Do not say ceasefire, peace, settlement, or end of war as a fact.
- The first paragraph must pair the strike pause with the unresolved mechanism.
- Keep Qatar's role as mediation, not guarantee.

### 2. France cyber attribution

**Strongest plausible countercase:** The French government has motive and technical access, but the public record is incomplete. Persian-language artifacts or Iran-linked infrastructure can be planted or reused. The named unit's operational involvement and a political order are separate propositions.

**Risk in current analysis:** “Naming Iran” could be read as establishing Iran's guilt. The final wording must say “France attributed” and “ANSSI said” when describing the responsible unit and counts.

**Required edit controls:**

- Keep more than 700 affected sites and at least 15 fires as different denominators.
- Do not call the sanctions “EU sanctions”; France only said it would propose them.
- Note that independent reproduction of the attribution was not found.
- The new event is the November 9 attribution and response, not the October incident date.

### 3. Abu Dhabi talks

**Strongest plausible countercase:** The meeting may be primarily an optics exercise. Separate European consultation is not equivalent to consent, and any credible guarantee may depend on excluded actors. No joint text was visible by cutoff.

**Risk in current analysis:** “First since February” is useful context but less important than the absence of terms. Also, source pages may update after the cutoff; do not import outcomes from later versions.

**Required edit controls:**

- Use only the fact that talks opened and the pre-cutoff agenda.
- No “progress,” “framework,” “deal,” “breakthrough,” or post-cutoff readout.
- Make the European representation problem an analysis point, not an asserted participant complaint.

### 4. Taiwan

**Strongest plausible countercase:** The same observed deployment may be routine training. Taiwan has reason to interpret PLA activity through a threat lens. Without a declared exercise plan or wider corroboration, area denial is an assessed mission.

**Risk in current analysis:** “If the pattern repeats” is appropriately conditional. The headline, however, must not drop “Taiwan says.”

**Required edit controls:**

- Separate observed location and formation from inferred purpose.
- Include China's routine-training characterization.
- Do not call it a blockade drill or rehearsal as fact.

### 5. Hegseth impeachment

**Strongest plausible countercase:** A filing can be politically significant even if it has little chance of removal. Conversely, procedural weakness does not disprove the allegations. The final brief must avoid predicting an outcome without a count.

**Risk in current analysis:** A broad phrase such as “war-powers fight” could obscure that the resolution contains multiple articles with different predicates.

**Required edit controls:**

- Name the filing and selected allegation categories, then explicitly state that they are unadjudicated.
- Do not call Hegseth guilty, impeached, or removed.
- Do not infer support from leadership unless directly reported.

### 6. Cyprus

**Strongest plausible countercase:** The agreement could stall before implementation, and crossings do not narrow the federation/two-state divide.

**Risk in current analysis:** “Deal” is acceptable only when immediately limited to two crossings. “Breakthrough” is not.

**Required edit controls:**

- No opening date unless one was announced before cutoff.
- Emphasize simultaneous implementation as the test.
- Keep the unresolved political end states visible.
