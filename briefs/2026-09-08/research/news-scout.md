## Candidate: IAEA says its verification gap in Iran has passed one year
- **Event time/date:** IAEA Board statement, 7 September 2026.
- **Region / actors:** Iran; IAEA; wider nonproliferation system.
- **What changed:** Director General Rafael Grossi said the IAEA received no new information on Iran's declared nuclear material or facilities and conducted no in-field verification during the reporting period. He said continuity of knowledge over Iran's declared enriched-uranium inventories, including material enriched up to 60%, had been lost for more than a year and called that a serious proliferation concern.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - The agency is no longer describing a short inspection interruption but a long-running inability to account for declared stocks.
  - The gap makes later reconstruction of inventories harder even if access resumes.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high.
- **Source chain:** One authoritative institutional source for what the IAEA assessed; it is not independent proof of Iran's current inventory. Euronews separately reported the same statement and Iranian officials' response, but its nuclear claim derives from the IAEA.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/statements/iaea-director-generals-introductory-statement-to-the-board-of-governors-7-september-2026 — Primary text of Grossi's Board statement and exact scope of the verification failure.
  - https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/07/iaea-says-iran-blocking-nuclear-inspectors-as-hormuz-threats-mount — Contemporary report carrying both the IAEA assessment and Iranian political context.
- **Questions for auditor:** What access, if any, occurred outside the reporting period? Does Iran dispute the inventory-reconstruction problem rather than merely the legal basis for access?
