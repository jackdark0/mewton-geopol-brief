## Candidate: The trilateral summit has a nuclear agenda without an arms-control framework

- **Underlying development:** The White House confirmed a Trump-Xi-Putin meeting for November 19 and said Trump will raise nuclear reductions. Officials said no agreement is expected. New START expired in February, and China has resisted joining a three-way process while its arsenal remains much smaller than those of Russia and the United States.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The spectacle of the first three-leader meeting can obscure the missing preparatory structure: no disclosed negotiating mandate, verification concept, numerical baseline, or follow-on process.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A leader-level opening could start a new channel after bilateral arms-control collapse. It could also substitute symbolism for verifiable restraints and legitimate an unequal three-party frame Beijing rejects.
- **What evidence is observable now:** White House confirmation, date, stated agenda, acknowledgment that no agreement is expected, and public arsenal asymmetry.
- **What remains inference:** Whether China will negotiate, whether the meeting creates a durable process, and whether Ukraine is excluded in practice.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://apnews.com/article/trump-xi-putin-summit-china-5fe92253ef38100bae913834a9b74d72 (meeting and limits); https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-to-meet-putin-and-xi-in-china-white-house-says-ce785eded88cf12d (Reuters account).
- **Questions for auditor:** Has any side published preparatory principles, weapon categories, or verification proposals?

## Candidate: Europe's Ukraine delegation solves representation only on paper

- **Underlying development:** European leaders selected a team of five military powers plus EU institutions for the next Ukraine negotiating phase. Russia had demanded discussion of European security architecture; U.S. envoys say Europe will participate.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines treat “Europe at the table” as binary, while authority remains fragmented across states, the EU, and NATO.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A defined delegation can make sanctions relief, financing, and guarantees more executable. Too many principals can also create a veto-rich process or allow Washington and Moscow to shop among European positions.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Named countries, EU-institution participation, U.S. and Ukrainian announcements, and Russian public demands on NATO expansion.
- **What remains inference:** Moscow's acceptance of the exact format, the delegation's mandate, and any common European red lines beyond public principles.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-putin-trump-zelenskyy-europe-157d168cb7ff9cae318cbf6cecb3988f (team and talks); https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/11/12/us-envoys-arrive-in-moscow-for-latest-round-of-talks-with-putin-a94236/pdf (Russian positions).
- **Questions for auditor:** Who can bind Europe on sanctions, forces, and reconstruction money? Is NATO represented?

## Candidate: Iran links safeguards access to security guarantees

- **Underlying development:** Iran told the IAEA that monitoring talks cannot proceed under U.S. military threats. The agency cannot inspect the bombed facilities, verify the 60%-enriched stockpile, or locate and characterize the declared Isfahan plant.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The dispute is not just another inspection delay; Tehran is making nuclear transparency conditional on the wider coercive relationship.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A prolonged visibility gap increases uncertainty for military planners and can itself raise incentives for preventive action. Threats used to gain access may instead reinforce Iran's stated reason for denying it.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The IAEA report's quoted language, the inspection history, and the list of unknowns.
- **What remains inference:** Material diversion, the current stockpile location, Iran's intent, and whether technical interim measures are possible.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://apnews.com/article/iran-nuclear-weapons-inspections-monitoring-bushehr-war-a689242aa635c103033790bacbff7f07 (report); https://www.al-monitor.com/breaking-news (Reuters review of report).
- **Questions for auditor:** Does the report distinguish legal noncompliance from practical inaccessibility? What precise confidence-building measures did the IAEA propose?

## Candidate: Turkey-Qatar pact creates an overlapping Gulf defense web

- **Underlying development:** The two governments signed a mutual-defense agreement declaring a unified response posture to an external armed attack and providing for consultations, exercises, industrial cooperation, and training.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The official communiqué contains more operationally relevant language than the short announcement, but the treaty text, ratification status, and force commitments are not public.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Qatar already hosts U.S. and Turkish forces. A new bilateral commitment may add deterrence, consultation channels, and defense-industrial depth, but ambiguous obligations can also complicate escalation management during the Iran war.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Signed agreement, collective-defense purpose, joint-exercise and training commitments.
- **What remains inference:** Automaticity, command arrangements, basing changes, force levels, ratification, and whether the pact covers ongoing hostilities.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://www.mfa.gov.tr/turkiye-cumhuriyeti-ile-katar-devleti-arasinda-yuksek-stratejik-komite-on-ikinci-toplantisi-ortak-bildirisi--13-kasim-2026.en.mfa (primary communiqué); https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-bahrain-radar-strait-hormuz-5d73a23b5b8647d2515c35b267b36c08 (AP confirmation).
- **Questions for auditor:** Can either parliament or official gazette supply the treaty text and entry-into-force clause?

## Candidate: China's no-notice drill may erode warning reliability

- **Underlying development:** Taiwan reports a more than 100-vessel, three-theater, coast-guard and carrier operation without a public exercise name, plus simulated attacks and a submarine missile launch.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Debate centers on whether this was a blockade rehearsal, but the more durable capability effect may be normalization of ambiguous mobilization.
- **Strategic mechanism:** If large cross-theater deployments become routine and unannounced, Taiwan and allies face more false positives and less time to distinguish exercise, coercive quarantine, and attack preparation.
- **What evidence is observable now:** A large deployment acknowledged in part by Chinese statements about routine carrier activity; Taiwanese reporting of cross-theater operations; the force count declined later in the week.
- **What remains inference:** Exact scale, mission, simulation content, submarine launch, and coercive intent.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/china-stages-unannounced-pacific-drills-in-silent-intimidation-of-taiwan-officials-say-ce785ed9db8df526 (Reuters report).
- **Questions for auditor:** What public tracking confirms the operating area and force composition? Is there a Chinese navigation or exercise notice?

## Candidate: The Bahrain exchange exposes a low-cost path out of the military lull

- **Underlying development:** Iran launched two one-way drones toward Bahrain; U.S. forces intercepted them. Iran says the action retaliated for an attack in Sirik and falsely or unverifiedly claimed a radar hit.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The strategic change is not the unproved damage claim but the confirmed choice to target a Gulf location hosting U.S. assets.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Relatively cheap one-way attacks force continued air-defense expenditure and can trigger retaliation even when damage is avoided. Ambiguous attacks on Iranian infrastructure provide Tehran a claimed retaliation cycle without a negotiated attribution mechanism.
- **What evidence is observable now:** CENTCOM's intercept report, no reported damage, Iranian retaliation narrative.
- **What remains inference:** Attacker in Sirik, intended target, Iranian command calculation, and whether the lull is over.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-bahrain-radar-strait-hormuz-5d73a23b5b8647d2515c35b267b36c08 (AP); https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-november-13-2026/ (CENTCOM statement quoted).
- **Questions for auditor:** Is there imagery or Bahraini confirmation? What did U.S. forces spend to defeat the drones?

## Candidate: Hormuz data show risk, but the new tanker story lacks a vessel

- **Underlying development:** Iran claims an unnamed gas tanker struck a mine. AP separately reports the IMO recorded about 134 incidents in the Strait and wider Middle East maritime area through November 11, with 25 seafarers dead and others injured or missing.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The verified cumulative denominator is strategically important; the dramatic new incident is not yet identifiable.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Persistent incidents can sustain insurance and routing costs even without closing the strait. Anonymous incident claims can also shape risk pricing before verification.
- **What evidence is observable now:** IMO cumulative figures as quoted by AP; IRGC claim without vessel details.
- **What remains inference:** The November 13 incident itself, mine attribution, damage, cargo, casualties, and route legality.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high for the trend; low for the isolated claim.
- **Sources:** https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-bahrain-radar-strait-hormuz-5d73a23b5b8647d2515c35b267b36c08 (IMO denominator and incident claim); https://gcaptain.com/iran-says-tanker-carrying-lng-hit-mine-suffered-explosion-in-hormuz/ (Reuters claim report).
- **Questions for auditor:** Can the IMO incident list be inspected directly? Is there a distress call or AIS gap matching the claim?

## Candidate: ICC Afghanistan narrowing intersects with U.S. anti-court policy

- **Underlying development:** Reuters says ICC judges closed part of the Afghanistan investigation that could examine alleged U.S. crimes and focused it on Taliban conduct after 2021. Hours later, Secretary of State Rubio attacked ICC authority in a public speech.

- **What evidence is observable now:** Reuters descriptions of the court document and Rubio's speech.
- **What remains inference:** The decision's precise scope, finality, rationale, and any connection to U.S. pressure.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Sources:** https://www.al-monitor.com/breaking-news (Reuters ICC item); https://www.marketscreener.com/news/rubio-assails-international-court-in-speech-to-conservative-legal-group-ce785eded88ff02d (Reuters on Rubio speech).
