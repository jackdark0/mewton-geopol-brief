# Red-team review - 2026-09-11

## Finding: Mokha is not Bab el-Mandeb control

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Mokha analysis; any headline linking the city to a closed or controlled strait.
- **Problem:** A city about 80 km from the chokepoint is not itself proof of control over navigation lanes, both shores, nearby islands, or ship transits.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The verified record confirms Mokha and the UN vehicle seizure, but no inspected data prove a post-takeover traffic stoppage or complete territorial control.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The seizure is a local Yemen-front gain with reputational and humanitarian consequences, while the shipping effect may be modest or already priced in.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Use “raises risk on the approaches,” never “seized the strait” or “closed Bab el-Mandeb.”

## Finding: Jordan damage reports do not prove an air-defence failure

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Jordan base analysis and any conclusion about interception performance.
- **Problem:** Damaged aircraft would narrow the earlier public scorecard but do not identify the number of missiles, their targets, penetration mechanism, or the entire engagement denominator.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Damage reporting rests on unnamed U.S.-side sources and is publicly denied by President Trump. The Jordanian scorecard is also a participant account. Neither independently supplies a complete audit.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The reports can both partly be true if they use different denominators, define damage differently, or refer to different strike effects; public language need not choose a definitive winner.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. State that the scorecard is challenged, not disproved; do not calculate or repeat a verified 90% interception rate.

## Finding: Canada announced a mechanism, not new ready-to-use capability

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Canada–Ukraine analysis.
- **Problem:** The announcement blends marketplace access, initial contracts, a corporate partnership, a government declaration, funding, and an industrial capacity goal. Treating this as an immediate drone surge would collapse several clocks.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The primary release itself says “goal,” “up to,” and “nearly” for key elements and gives no production/delivery schedule for the promised millions.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The package may be more industrial symbolism than scalable output if supply chains, export controls, finance, or Ukrainian demand diverge.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Separate announced funds, contracts, capacity goal, delivery, and operational use.

## Finding: U.S. designations establish legal exposure, not the full factual narrative

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Iran sanctions analysis.
- **Problem:** Treasury's description of proxy ties and conduct is an official allegation set, not an independent adjudication. Broad language about “isolation” or “dismantling” could overstate the observed effect.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The decisive verified fact is the licensing presumption and blocked-property rules; no transaction-level impact or counterparty behaviour was established.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Firms may already have avoided affected activity, may use exceptions, or may reroute through non-U.S. channels.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Lead with the legal policy change and keep factual allegations attributed to Treasury.

## Finding: The IAEA development needs an unusually hard provenance boundary

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Iran safeguards analysis.
- **Problem:** The final de-restricted GOV/2026/60 text and vote were not inspected. A Quad statement reports the Director General's findings but is not itself the underlying IAEA report.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The statement uses “in the event” language about adoption, while other reporting says a step occurred. That mismatch is an audit signal, not a detail to smooth away.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The procedural event may have been narrower, later, or differently worded than commentary suggests; consequences may be entirely nonautomatic.
- **Required editorial action:** correct. State only that the available record shows a proposed/procedural step and a severe attributed verification gap; omit vote totals, final legal effect, and stockpile claims.

## Finding: ECB causation must remain the ECB's assessment

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** ECB analysis.
- **Problem:** The ECB cited Middle East inflation pressure, but its decision does not produce a counterfactual estimate of the war's share of inflation or prove a direct hit to defence spending.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The ECB also cited broader uncertainty and projected resilient growth.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Inflation and rates could be driven substantially by non-war factors; the conflict is a material contributor in the ECB's assessment, not a complete causal explanation.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Attribute the inflation-pressure judgment to the ECB and call the defence-fiscal effect a watch item.

## Finding: Algeria's unexplained rupture should not become a proxy-war story by implication

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Algeria–UAE analysis.
- **Problem:** The move is real, but the public Algerian record did not give a specific cause. Repeating contextual disputes as reasons would infer motive from correlation.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The primary statements establish severance and announced airspace scope only.
- **Best competing interpretation:** A short-term bilateral trigger, not a durable regional realignment, could explain the action.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. State the cause is unpublicized and make actual enforcement the next test.

# Analysis that survived challenge

- The Mokha takeover and UN vehicle seizure are strong, separately sourced facts; the strategic reading survives when narrowed to increased leverage and risk rather than strait control.
- Canada, Treasury, Algeria, and the ECB each made a concrete, inspectable announced act. The analysis properly distinguishes the act from its later effect.
- The Jordan and Iran safeguards sections retain uncertainty rather than force a binary verdict, provided the editorial corrections above are applied.

# Highest-risk unresolved issue

The Jordan attack is the highest-risk unresolved issue because a clean, politically useful interception narrative conflicts with detailed damage reporting and a presidential denial. The final brief should make the conflict legible without converting either combatant's or administration's account into a settled technical assessment.
