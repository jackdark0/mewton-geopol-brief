## Claim C-01: Jordan engaged exactly 20 Iranian ballistic missiles, destroyed exactly 18, and the remaining two fell in unpopulated areas without casualties

- **Claim type:** Quantitative battlefield outcome; damage and attribution.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** The Jordan Armed Forces, published by the Jordan News Agency (Petra) on September 9. The proposition contains four atoms: 20 missiles engaged; 18 destroyed; two fell in unpopulated areas; no casualties.
- **Why it matters:** The claim is being used as an exact 90% interception score and as evidence that the attack had no operational effect. It also shapes whether Iran is understood to have penetrated a U.S.- and Jordanian-defended base.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [Petra preserves the Jordan Armed Forces account](https://www.petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-armed-forces-intercept-18-iranian-ballistic-missiles-targeting-kingdom). The arithmetic is internally complete: 18 + 2 = 20. The no-casualty atom is consistent with [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/multiple-us-military-aircraft-damaged-iran-strikes-military-base-jordan/), which reported no U.S. deaths.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/multiple-us-military-aircraft-damaged-iran-strikes-military-base-jordan/) reported from people with direct knowledge that the same overnight Iranian attack struck Muwaffaq Salti Air Base, cost one A-10 a wing, and lightly damaged roughly eight F-15s. [Reuters, carried by Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/flashpoints/middle-east/2026/09/10/iran-attack-on-base-in-jordan-damaged-american-military-aircraft-us-official-says/), separately attributed the same inventory to an unnamed U.S. official. [Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/iran-damages-a-10-f-15s-with-missile-attack-on-air-base-in-jordan/) said people familiar with the matter confirmed the damage. These reports are difficult to reconcile with a literal reading that both non-intercepted missiles fell only in unpopulated areas. No radar record, impact map, debris ledger, missile-by-missile accounting, or on-record U.S. after-action report was public by cutoff. It is also unknown whether Jordan’s 20-missile denominator covered every missile in the base attack or only those Jordan says its own forces engaged.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** The aircraft-damage core has at least two newsroom streams, but all rely on unidentified U.S.-side sources rather than public physical records. The exact 20/18/2 denominator remains Jordan-sourced.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear as a package.** The no-death atom is supported; the exact denominator and “two in unpopulated areas” remainder are no longer safe to repeat without qualification.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** Confidence is limited by anonymous sourcing and the absence of a public after-action record.
- **Sources:** [Petra](https://www.petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-armed-forces-intercept-18-iranian-ballistic-missiles-targeting-kingdom) — originating Jordanian scorecard; [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/multiple-us-military-aircraft-damaged-iran-strikes-military-base-jordan/) — aircraft and interceptor details from direct-knowledge sources; [Reuters/Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/flashpoints/middle-east/2026/09/10/iran-attack-on-base-in-jordan-damaged-american-military-aircraft-us-official-says/) — U.S.-official aircraft inventory; [Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/iran-damages-a-10-f-15s-with-missile-attack-on-air-base-in-jordan/) — specialist confirmation and CENTCOM non-response.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does the 20 count cover the entire barrage at Muwaffaq Salti or a Jordanian engagement subset? Were the aircraft damaged by direct impact, debris, or blast? Can satellite imagery, base imagery, or an on-record Pentagon statement reconcile the impact locations?

## Claim C-02: Iranian missiles inflicted “heavy damage” on Al-Azraq facilities used for F-35, F-16, and F-15 operations

- **Claim type:** Battle damage; scope and quantifier.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** The IRGC, relayed by IRNA on September 9. The source says maintenance, preparation, deployment facilities, and shelters for three fighter types sustained “heavy damage.”
- **Why it matters:** If true at full scope, the strike would show Iran can impose a meaningful operational cost on a major U.S. air hub despite layered defenses. If inflated, it converts a real penetration into a much larger capability claim.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The [IRNA account reproduced by GlobalSecurity.org](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/iran/2026/09/iran-260909-irna02.htm) identifies the base, facility types, aircraft families, and alleged damage. Later U.S.-sourced reporting supports a real damage core: [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/multiple-us-military-aircraft-damaged-iran-strikes-military-base-jordan/) reported one badly damaged A-10 and roughly eight lightly damaged F-15s; [Reuters](https://www.militarytimes.com/flashpoints/middle-east/2026/09/10/iran-attack-on-base-in-jordan-damaged-american-military-aircraft-us-official-says/) reported the same basic inventory from a U.S. official.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The U.S.-sourced inventory does not identify a damaged F-35 or F-16. It describes the F-15 damage as light and the aircraft as returned to service. It establishes aircraft damage, not the claimed scale of damage to maintenance facilities or shelters. Iran released no inspectable battle-damage assessment tied to each facility in the sources reviewed. CENTCOM had not issued an on-record damage account by cutoff.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** A material-damage core is supported; the aircraft mix, facility list, and “heavy” quantifier are not independently corroborated.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak as stated, with a supported core.** Iranian missiles appear to have damaged U.S. aircraft, including one seriously, but the broader heavy-damage claim exceeds the public evidence.
- **Confidence:** **Medium-high** on that classification; **medium** on the precise U.S. damage inventory.
- **Sources:** [IRNA reproduction](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/iran/2026/09/iran-260909-irna02.htm) — originating Iranian proposition; [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/multiple-us-military-aircraft-damaged-iran-strikes-military-base-jordan/) — direct-knowledge damage account; [Reuters/Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/flashpoints/middle-east/2026/09/10/iran-attack-on-base-in-jordan-damaged-american-military-aircraft-us-official-says/) — U.S.-official account; [Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/iran-damages-a-10-f-15s-with-missile-attack-on-air-base-in-jordan/) — specialist report and scope limitation.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain an on-record U.S. damage assessment. Check commercial imagery for hangar or shelter damage. Determine whether the A-10/F-15 reports are genuinely independent or share the same small official source pool.

These must not be collapsed into one clear ten-ship denominator.
- **Why it matters:** This would be Iran’s largest declared maritime attack of the war, change assessments of U.S. naval vulnerability, and affect insurance, shipping, and retaliation decisions.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The [Press TV account](https://www.presstv.co.uk/Detail/2026/09/09/775941/IRGC-targets-two-US-vessels-violating-ships-Strait-Hormuz) names the bundled quantities and alleges damage. [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-09/iran-attacks-us-base-in-jordan-after-tankers-sunk/107134124) reported the tanker New Andros suffered minor hull damage without injuries or cargo leakage and described additional merchant-vessel incidents reported to UKMTO.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** CENTCOM told AP that no U.S. Navy warship was struck and all attempted attacks failed. No independent image, casualty report, port call, repair record, or on-record ship account supported damage to either named destroyer. UKMTO and Iraq support attacks on several merchant vessels but do not establish exactly eight tanker hits, Iranian responsibility for each, or heavy damage. The ten additional ships are not individually identified in the reviewed record. The IRGC translation may also double-count the same ten as two U.S. vessels plus eight tankers.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for damage to the two destroyers; **partial** for attacks on merchant vessels; **no/unknown** for the ten additional vessels.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear as a package.** The merchant-attack core is supported. The exact inventory, attribution of every incident, and claimed destroyer damage are weak.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the public record does not establish the full package; **medium** on the merchant-vessel subset because incident details remain incomplete.

Seek naval imagery or ship-status evidence for DDG-119 and DDG-53. Trace whether AP, Reuters, ABC, and Guardian merchant counts all derive from the same UKMTO notices.

## Claim C-04: Iran directly guided the Houthi campaign that captured Mokha and did so to open a new front against the United States

- **Claim type:** Command attribution, intent, and causal contribution.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** [Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/iranian-arms-and-advice-helped-yemen-s-houthis-seize-key-red-sea-city-sources-say-ce785bded18df525), citing Yemeni-government, Iranian, and regional sources on September 10. Its report contains several distinct atoms: Tehran told the Houthis to escalate against Saudi Arabia; promised more funds, weapons, and senior officers; IRGC commanders traveled to Yemen; the Guards directed the west-coast campaign; and Iran’s purpose was to open a new U.S. war front.
- **Why it matters:** Direct operational command would make the Mokha advance more clearly part of the U.S.-Iran war and strengthen arguments for action against Iran or its forces. Longstanding Iranian support alone does not establish command of this operation.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters cites two Iranian sources for an escalation instruction and promised support, a regional diplomat for senior-commander travel, and five Yemeni military sources who cited communications intercepts and prisoner accounts for IRGC direction. The report also names IRGC commander Abdolreza Shahlaei as the alleged director. A specialist quoted by Reuters said the weapons used reflect Iranian or smuggling-network supply.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** A third Iranian source in the same Reuters report said the Mokha move was a Houthi decision, although it benefited Iran. Tehran publicly says the Houthis are allies, not proxies, and a senior Iranian official told [Reuters](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-iran-war-to-end-after-us-midterm-elections-threatens-to-attack-pickaxe-mountain-ce785bdedb8ffe27) that Iran does not control them. [AP’s independently reported takeover](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-iran-houthi-rebels-red-sea-mokha-e4e799701b382799a955969c212800ea) confirms Houthi control of Mokha through opposing officials, residents, and doctors but does not independently establish Iranian direction. No intercept, prisoner statement, travel record, order, or weapons-chain document was published for inspection.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial at best.** Iranian material support is a supported background relationship; direct command and the asserted U.S.-front purpose remain Reuters-sourced through confidential witnesses.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible but unclear.** The multi-source report is substantial, but command and intent rely on inaccessible evidence, and one Iranian source narrows the claim to a Houthi decision.
- **Confidence:** **Medium-low.** Source diversity raises plausibility; opacity, wartime incentives, and the missing records cap confidence.
- **Sources:** [Reuters detailed report](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/iranian-arms-and-advice-helped-yemen-s-houthis-seize-key-red-sea-city-sources-say-ce785bded18df525) — confidential-source command case and internal counterclaim; [Reuters follow-up](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-iran-war-to-end-after-us-midterm-elections-threatens-to-attack-pickaxe-mountain-ce785bdedb8ffe27) — Iranian denial and current operational context; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-iran-houthi-rebels-red-sea-mokha-e4e799701b382799a955969c212800ea) — independent confirmation of the territorial result, not Iranian command.
- **Questions for auditor:** Can any communications intercept or captured-prisoner evidence be independently described? Did multiple Reuters sources learn the claim from one Yemeni-government intelligence assessment? Is Shahlaei’s presence current and independently established? Separate supply, advice, strategic encouragement, campaign direction, and tactical command.

## Claim C-05: The capture of Mokha gives the Houthis operational control of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait

- **Claim type:** Emergent strategic narrative; territorial control versus maritime-control capability. This is a paraphrased narrative, not a verified quotation from one claimant.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Headline and commentary shorthand after the September 10 takeover. Some coverage says the Houthis “expanded control” over the strait, while more careful accounts say the capture brings them closer to control or increases leverage.
- **Why it matters:** Treating a coastal-city seizure as completed control of a global chokepoint can exaggerate an immediate supply cutoff and drive escalation or market narratives. Understating the new threat would also be wrong.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-iran-houthi-rebels-red-sea-mokha-e4e799701b382799a955969c212800ea) independently confirmed Houthi forces throughout Mokha and described the city as vital to Red Sea shipping. [Reuters](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-iran-war-to-end-after-us-midterm-elections-threatens-to-attack-pickaxe-mountain-ce785bdedb8ffe27) reported, from Yemeni military sources, advances toward the Hanish islands and said the capture gives a direct presence on the strait’s approaches. Houthi threats and earlier attacks demonstrate a capability to endanger shipping from land outside the narrow waterway itself.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Mokha is about 80 kilometers from Bab el-Mandeb. Reuters used conditional language about future control and reported government forces relocating to Dhubab and Perim Island, which its sources described as key to holding the strait. The Houthi-run navigation center said navigation remained safe for all except Saudi vessels. No source inspected established that the Houthis control both shores, the recognized navigation lanes, Dhubab, or Perim, or that the Mokha capture itself stopped traffic. AP reported that Bab el-Mandeb shipping had already fallen about 60% since attacks began in 2023, so current traffic weakness cannot be attributed wholly to this takeover.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Yes** for the takeover; **partial** for expanded threat and leverage; **no** for completed operational control of the strait.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak as stated.** The supported correction is that Mokha materially improves Houthi access and leverage on the approaches while leaving control of the strait unresolved.
- **Confidence:** **High** on the scope distinction; **medium** on current front-line geography beyond Mokha.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-iran-houthi-rebels-red-sea-mokha-e4e799701b382799a955969c212800ea) — takeover, distance, resident evidence, and pre-existing traffic decline; [Reuters](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-iran-war-to-end-after-us-midterm-elections-threatens-to-attack-pickaxe-mountain-ce785bdedb8ffe27) — conditional control language, Hanish claim, Dhubab/Perim caveat, and Houthi navigation statement; [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/10/houthis-yemen-mokha-port-red-sea/) — government withdrawal to Dhubab and official Yemeni acknowledgment of the setback.
- **Questions for auditor:** Verify Hanish, Dhubab, and Perim control at cutoff. Obtain vessel-transit data specifically after the Mokha capture. Separate ability to threaten, selective interdiction, effective blockade, and legal/physical control.

## Claim C-06: Regional oil flows are “two-thirds or more” of prewar levels, showing the United States has substantially reopened the Strait of Hormuz

- **Claim type:** Quantitative policy-success narrative; numerator, denominator, route, and time-window firewalls.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNN on September 6 that, including pipelines, “we’re probably two-thirds or more of preconflict flows.” Current AP coverage describes U.S. progress in loosening Iran’s grip. The second clause—therefore the strait is substantially reopened—is a common inference, not part of Wright’s exact route-qualified wording.
- **Why it matters:** The metric bears on U.S. war aims, energy prices, naval burden, and whether Iran retains economic leverage. Mixing strait traffic with bypass pipelines can make a regional-flow recovery look like a maritime reopening.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [AP’s September 11 analysis](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-hormuz-trump-yemen-houthis-2b126da67b09de2f328846eb656abe0e) reports Kpler estimates of 8.4 million barrels per day in non-Iranian oil exports in September, rising to 10.8 million when alternative routes are included, against about 14 million before the war. Those stated numbers produce 60.0% for the 8.4/14 strait-related comparison and 77.1% for 10.8/14 including alternatives. [Kpler’s methodology](https://www.kpler.com/blog/explainer-the-nuances-of-reading-hormuz-oil-flows-data) explains that strait transits, total Hormuz clearance, and wider regional measures are distinct counting lines and cannot be casually added.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Wright’s public 9-million-barrel strait estimate did not disclose commodity scope, counting line, date basis, or revisions; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-strait-hormuz-ship-attack-a52beec77dc90af3d040d0553837ad20) said it appeared above 28-day tracker averages. Reuters reported only seven vessel transits on September 9, half the recent average, while warning the newest ship-tracking data are preliminary. A barrel-volume recovery is not the same denominator as vessel count, total commercial traffic, or normal freedom of navigation. The 10.8-million figure includes oil that bypasses the strait.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Kpler supports a major regional petroleum-flow recovery under an explicit wider measure. The exact official 9-million strait figure and the “reopened strait” inference remain definition-sensitive.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible for regional petroleum flows; weak if restated as two-thirds of normal Strait of Hormuz traffic.**
- **Confidence:** **High** on the denominator warning; **medium** on the latest flow share because recent tracking data revise upward.
- **Sources:** [AP, September 11](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-hormuz-trump-yemen-houthis-2b126da67b09de2f328846eb656abe0e) — current Kpler comparison and exact Wright wording; [AP, September 6](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-strait-hormuz-ship-attack-a52beec77dc90af3d040d0553837ad20) — original public estimate and tracker challenge; [Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/explainer-the-nuances-of-reading-hormuz-oil-flows-data) — primary methodology, counting lines, revision lag, and non-additivity; [Reuters](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-iran-war-to-end-after-us-midterm-elections-threatens-to-attack-pickaxe-mountain-ce785bdedb8ffe27) — September 9 vessel-count setback.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the CNN transcript and define Wright’s numerator. Ask whether 14 million excludes Iranian exports and LNG. Use one matched commodity scope, route, and time window. Do not compare barrels with ships without explaining vessel size and cargo mix.

## Explicit limitations

- Several key claims depend on unnamed wartime sources. CBS, Reuters, and Air & Space Forces may be separate editorial checks but are not automatically independent physical evidence.
- No public radar tracks, satellite battle-damage imagery, U.S. after-action report, aircraft maintenance record, destroyer repair record, or full merchant-vessel incident ledger was located by cutoff.
- UKMTO’s dynamic incident page exposed only limited text through the research interface; AP and ABC provided the incident details inspected here. The auditor should retrieve the underlying notices directly.
- Iranian state-media translations disagree about whether the maritime denominator is ten total vessels or ten plus ten additional vessels. No arithmetic should be built on that wording until the Persian original is checked.
- Front-line control around Hanish, Dhubab, and Perim was reported through military sources and was not independently mapped here. Mokha’s takeover is much better supported than the surrounding control claims.
- Kpler says newest flow data are confirmed floors that can revise 1.5-2 times higher. Current-day ship counts should not be treated as final barrel volumes.
- No source set was exhaustive. The cutoff excludes reports published after 09:01:32 UTC on September 11 even if a webpage later updated.
