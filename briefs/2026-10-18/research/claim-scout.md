## Claim: C-01 — A supertanker struck a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz during the present window

- **Origin / principal promoters:** IRGC Navy statement, reproduced by IRGC-linked Tasnim and Iranian state media; subsequently reported by Iran International and Euronews. The proposition concerns the reported mechanism of one incident, not the number of tankers lost.
- **Why it matters:** The mechanism matters for mine-clearance claims, shipping risk, and the distinction between a mined route and a deliberate missile or drone strike. An unattributed fire does not identify its cause.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Tasnim's October 17, 11:22 article attributes an explosion and fire to an IRGC Navy statement. Iran International carried the same account at October 17 02:53 GMT+1, or 01:53 UTC, placing this statement within the edition window. The reporting describes a tanker carrying more than two million barrels; that is an attributed cargo claim, not measured oil lost or spilled.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No inspected source supplies a ship name, IMO number, owner confirmation, precise coordinates, independently authenticated original footage, or forensic evidence establishing a mine. The Reuters Connect video listing is a StringersHub asset drawing on IRIB and other footage; its disclaimer explicitly withholds Reuters verification or endorsement. A fire video alone cannot establish mechanism, cargo, date, or a second distinct event. Euronews supplies competing US control messaging, not an independent accident investigation. No inference of falsity follows from missing confirmation.
- **Independent corroboration:** no. The inspected reports share the IRGC statement lineage for the mine mechanism. Different publishers are not different witnesses.
- **Current assessment:** unclear. Supported that the IRGC made the claim; the underlying mine strike remains unverified in the inspected record.
- **Confidence:** medium. The source dependence is identifiable, but absent incident identification and technical evidence prevent a stronger underlying-event conclusion.

## Claim: C-02 — The United States military supported the passage of the tanker in C-01

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The same IRGC Navy statement; Tasnim and Iran International relay the allegation. This is a separate attribution proposition from the cause of the fire.
- **Why it matters:** Actual US escort or routing advice would materially change the implications for deterrence and operational credibility. General US protection of shipping does not establish an escort for this vessel.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Iranian account accuses the US military of supporting the passage. The StringersHub storyline repeats that accusation. These are one claimant's account, not three sources of operational knowledge.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No named ship, escorting warship, track, bridge communication, US operational acknowledgment, or owner account is supplied. Euronews reports broad US efforts to protect navigation, which cannot be attached to this unidentified tanker. Alternative explanations include independent commercial routing, unsupported attribution, or an incident different from the one assumed by downstream reports. The inspected evidence cannot select among them.
- **Independent corroboration:** no.
- **Current assessment:** weak. The record contains an explicit accusation but lacks evidence specific to a US role in this passage. This is not a finding that US involvement was disproved.
- **Confidence:** medium. The inferential gap is substantial; the underlying operational record is unavailable.

## Claim: C-03 — A White House instruction caused the satellite-service slowdown reported by Ukraine

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters' reporting from two anonymous US officials and one Western official; Ukrainian officials separately described the disruption. The newly changed element is Rubio's categorical October 17 denial, including a conflicting description of which service Ukraine possessed.
- **Why it matters:** A deliberate restriction would suggest a US policy constraint on Ukrainian operations. A technical fault would imply a different remedy. The product distinction also affects what authority or contract could have controlled the service.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters, carried by Defense News on October 16, reports an instruction from inside the White House to the Pentagon. Its sources do not identify the decision-maker or implementer. That report is older context for the present-window denial, not a newly discovered order.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The October 17 State Department transcript reproduced by Forth records Rubio denying a White House instruction. He distinguishes commercial Starlink from Starshield, says Ukraine lacks the latter, and describes its requested access as under discussion. No inspected written directive, contract, terminal configuration, engineering log, or independently measured mission effect resolves the conflict. Both the identity of the product and the cause need audit; do not silently substitute one product name for the other.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial. Reuters reports multiple anonymous sources, but their underlying access and independence cannot be inspected. Reports of service degradation do not independently establish its policy cause. Forth reproduces an official statement, not an independent technical finding.
- **Current assessment:** unclear. Reuters supplies a substantial reported allegation; Rubio supplies an explicit contrary official account. Neither account becomes established solely through status or repetition.
- **Confidence:** medium. Attribution, nomenclature, and technical records remain unresolved.

## Claim: C-04 — The administration intends to use a 60-day period to review the waived Russia sanctions and tariffs

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Rubio's October 17 remarks. This is an attributed policy-intention proposition; automatic legal expiry after 60 days is a different proposition.
- **Why it matters:** The public review horizon affects expectations about pressure on Moscow and energy-importing partners. It must not become a guaranteed activation date.
- **Evidence offered for it:** In the reproduced transcript, Rubio describes the President wanting a 60-day period to assess economic effects. This is new explanatory messaging about the October 16 waiver.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The inspected order's section 4(d) makes continuation depend on a subsequent official determination, not an express 60-day sunset. A review horizon therefore does not establish a binding deadline, an agreed Russian concession, or eventual activation. No independently inspected implementing amendment was found in this pass.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial. The dated order directly establishes the operative text, while the statement is available through a press-release mirror. They establish different things and are not two confirmations of an automatic expiry.
- **Current assessment:** supported as attributed intention, conditional on authenticating the reproduced transcript. Any claim that the order itself automatically expires after 60 days is contradicted by its inspected text.
- **Confidence:** medium. The policy intention rests on an official-text reproduction; the legal-text distinction is stronger.

## Claim: C-05 — Sisi sought parliamentary authority for defensive intervention if Saudi Arabia requests it

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Sisi's October 17 military-academy address; Reuters and The National report it. This proposition concerns a conditional request, not an executed deployment.
- **Why it matters:** The request creates a concrete decision point in the Saudi-Houthi conflict. Announced willingness, legislative authorization, a Saudi request, force assignment, deployment, and combat are separate stages.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters and The National both report the conditional authorization request and parliament's announced Sunday meeting. Reuters gives the announced meeting time as October 18 10:00 UTC, four hours after this edition's cutoff. The National also reports that parliamentary approval is required.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** These accounts do not establish approval by cutoff, receipt of a Saudi deployment request, units assigned, an operational mandate, or new Egyptian forces deployed. They share the same speech as the source of Sisi's intention; independence of reporting is not independent observation of military action. Historical coalition membership cannot establish a new deployment.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes for the reported speech/request, through separately bylined Reuters and The National accounts; no for an actual new intervention. Original Egyptian speech or parliamentary record remains to be inspected.
- **Current assessment:** supported as a conditional authorization request. An unconditional commitment or completed intervention exceeds the inspected evidence.
- **Confidence:** medium. Reporting converges, but the primary record and operational particulars remain absent.
