## Candidate N01: Iran publicizes seven-day terms; late report says Trump rejected them

- **Event time/date:** September 25; late Reuters update September 26 01:55 BST = September 25 20:55 EDT, before cutoff.
- **Region / actors:** Iran, United States, Qatar, Hormuz.
- **What changed:** Araghchi publicly described a seven-day conditional reopening proposal. Later Reuters relayed a WSJ report that Trump rejected it and regarded renewed bombing after the November elections as likely. Reuters explicitly could not confirm the WSJ account.
- **Why this may matter:** Public terms and reported rejection change yesterday's anonymous phased-plan discussion into a sharper diplomatic decision point.
- **Initial priority:** High.
- **Confidence in basic event:** High that the public proposal exists; moderate in the reported private rejection, low in future bombing predictions.
- **Source chain:** Iranian minister → reporting; unnamed US officials → WSJ → Reuters → syndicator for rejection. The latter is one lineage, not Reuters confirmation.
- **Sources:** [Reuters via Geo](https://www.geo.tv/latest/683639-iran-proposes-reopening-hormuz-ending-war-within-seven-days), September 25, body paragraphs beginning with the New York dateline: proposal, conditional sequence and unresolved Iranian messaging. [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/allies-unite-behind-saudi-arabia-as-houthis-step-up-attacks-ce785adfdb8ff523), updated headline and opening paragraphs: WSJ rejection report and Reuters nonconfirmation.
- **Counterevidence / uncertainty:** Reuters also records a US official describing constructive mediated discussions. Rejection of this proposal does not establish an end to all diplomacy; seven days is conditional, not a countdown already started.
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve original WSJ report and named US/Iran/Qatar records. Were terms formally transmitted and authorized? What precisely was rejected? Do not treat future bombing as decided operational policy.
