## C-01 — Claim: Trump rejected the Iranian seven-day ceasefire proposal by September 25, 2026.

- **Type / scope:** Empirical decision claim; the specific proposal described during the New York meetings, not a rejection of all possible negotiations.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** The Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed U.S. officials, as relayed by Reuters and Iran International. Attribution to WSJ is secure in the inspected relays; direct WSJ retrieval failed. Do not attribute this as a public Trump announcement.
- **Why it matters:** An actual rejection would materially narrow the immediate Hormuz opening described in yesterday's brief. Incorrectly upgrading a leak to an official decision would misstate the diplomatic position.
- **Evidence offered for it:** S1 directly records the report. S2 repeats the rejection account and describes Iran as awaiting an official U.S. response. This establishes a consequential report in circulation, not independent confirmation of the private decision.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** S1 explicitly says Reuters could not immediately verify it; S2 retains that qualification. S2 also reports a prior anonymous U.S. characterization of mediator discussions as constructive. That earlier characterization does not disprove a later rejection. No inspected rejection notice, named U.S. confirmation, or delivered diplomatic message establishes whether rejection was final or communicated.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** in the inspected record. Reuters and Iran International identify WSJ as their origin for this proposition.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear** as an established decision; a credible but unconfirmed report. **Pangburn disposition:** HOLD / UNVERIFIABLE pending the original report or independent evidence. This is not a false verdict.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** in the provisional HOLD classification, because the source dependence and Reuters verification limitation are explicit; low in asserting the underlying decision unconditionally.
- **Sources:** S1, S2, S3, below. S1 is the shortest publication-safe account; S2 adds the response-status distinction; S3 is another relay, not a new witness.
- **Strongest countercase:** WSJ may have multiple well-placed officials and accurate private information that will precede any public acknowledgment. Failure to obtain the original must not be mistaken for evidence of error.
- **Questions for auditor:** Can the original WSJ text be inspected? How many officials, with what access, described the decision? Is there a dated White House or mediator response before cutoff? Was the reported rejection of this exact text, an earlier version, or one condition?
- **Publication-safe wording:** Reuters relayed a WSJ report that Trump rejected the proposal, while explicitly saying it could not independently confirm the report.

## C-02 — Claim: Iran's latest proposal includes transferring its uranium stockpile abroad after dilution.

- **Type / scope:** Empirical proposal-content claim; not actual shipment, agreed implementation, or transfer of uranium still enriched to 60%.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Mohamad Ali Harisi's original reporting for The National, citing sources familiar with Tehran's position (S4). Attribution confidence high for what the outlet reports; underlying officials remain unnamed.
- **Why it matters:** Exporting material after dilution could provide bargaining space that a blanket no-concessions headline obscures. Conversely, an unauthorized or inaccurately reported offer could create a false impression of progress.
- **Evidence offered for it:** S4 describes dilution followed by third-party transfer under an IAEA-related mechanism. Its reported chronology is essential to the proposition.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** S5 reports a senior Iranian official refusing concessions including shipment of highly enriched uranium abroad. No inspected signed proposal, named Iranian endorsement of export, receiving-state agreement, or IAEA implementation document resolves the discrepancy. Dilution inside Iran and export afterward could coexist with refusing export of material while highly enriched; that is a possible reconciliation, not a finding about the negotiators' intended meaning.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for the transfer term. S4 and S5 appear to be separate original reporting chains, but anonymous-source overlap and authority cannot be established.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear. Pangburn disposition:** HOLD / UNVERIFIABLE on the precise authorized offer. There is a material reporting tension; it is premature to label it a demonstrated internal policy split.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** in preserving uncertainty; low on the private proposal's exact authorized terms.
- **Sources:** S4 supports the specific dilution-then-transfer sequence; S5 supplies the strongest narrowing/contrary report. These sources do not prove a delivery occurred.
- **Denominator / technical caution:** “60%” describes enrichment level in the reported proposal, not the fraction of Iran's total stockpile offered. Do not infer current stockpile mass, surviving inventory, bomb count, or breakout time from it.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does refusal concern HEU before dilution or any uranium exports? What dilution endpoint and receiving country are proposed? Who authorized each position? Is the concession contingent on prior sanctions relief? Can a named spokesperson or original proposal resolve the ambiguity?
- **Publication-safe wording:** Reports differ on uranium terms; The National describes dilution followed by export, while Reuters quotes an official opposing shipment of highly enriched material. The actual authorized offer remains unverified.

## C-03 — Claim: Trump told aides he expects renewed bombing of Iran after the November 2026 midterms.

- **Type / scope:** Reported private statement about an expectation. Separate from C-01's decision and from the future event itself.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** WSJ's unnamed U.S. officials through S1 and S3; no inspected direct recording, transcript, or named witness.
- **Why it matters:** The account may affect assessments of escalation risk and the time available for diplomacy. Converting it into an operational order or guaranteed future attack would overstate the evidence.
- **Evidence offered for it:** S1 relays the reported statement to aides. S3 also attributes the expectation to WSJ and retains uncertainty about the scale and durability of the position.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** S1's inability to verify applies to the report. S3 says the position could change and describes reservations about major combat. Neither supplies a strike order, date, target list, or force commitment. Continued diplomacy does not disprove a private expectation of future strikes.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No.** This is the same WSJ lineage as C-01, despite multiple hosts.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear. Pangburn disposition:** HOLD / UNVERIFIABLE for the alleged private statement. A categorical prediction that bombing will happen is not established by this evidence.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** in the limitation; low in asserting the private statement as independently verified.
- **Sources:** S1 establishes the reported allegation; S3 preserves qualifications often lost in headline repetition.
- **Motive / causation caution:** The timing alone does not establish an electoral motive for withholding attacks. Capability, munitions constraints, diplomatic signaling, and political calculation are possible explanations; none should be presented as the cause without specific evidence.
- **Questions for auditor:** Was this an expectation, preference, contingency, or instruction? Does the original distinguish them? Is the wording “after” being wrongly turned into “because of” the election? Is any operational step independently documented?
- **Publication-safe wording:** The same unconfirmed WSJ account says Trump privately expects renewed bombing after the midterms; that is not a verified military timetable.

## C-04 — Claim: Every U.S. action requested in Iran's new seven-day proposal was already included in the June memorandum of understanding.

- **Type / scope:** Documentary-content comparison with the literal quantifier “every.” It is distinct from whether a prior commitment is legally binding, currently operative, or already breached.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi's September 25 remarks at the UN, directly reported by Reuters (S6). Attribution confidence high in this direct report. Source wording: “They are all already in the MOU.”
- **Why it matters:** This frames the offer as implementation of prior commitments. Scope, conditions, and sequence determine whether that framing is accurate; shared subject matter alone is insufficient.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The June text reproduced by UCSB (S7) contains provisions concerning hostilities, blockade relief, oil waivers, and frozen assets. There is a substantial documentary core to Araqchi's comparison.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** UCSB explicitly labels its text a **draft**, with signing blanks. It cannot authenticate the final signed instrument. Its provisions have different sequencing and timing: blockade removal begins upon signing and completes within 30 days; broader sanctions termination follows an agreed final-deal schedule; asset availability depends on implementation with procedures to be agreed. Without both complete final texts, “all” cannot be verified at the level of conditions and timing.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial** for overlap of subject matter; **no** for exact equivalence of final terms. A document replica is evidence about its own text, not automatic proof that both governments accepted that version.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear** for the exact universal. **Pangburn disposition:** HOLD on final-text equivalence; Qualified if used to imply identical sequence or immediate unconditional performance. Do not call Araqchi's literal assertion false merely because timing differs: an action can appear in both documents under different conditions.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate**: inspected text supports the overlap and exposes the limits; missing authenticated versions prevent high confidence.
- **Sources:** S6 for what Araqchi said; S7 for the available June draft and its explicit provenance caveat. June material is older context required to test this new assertion.
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve the signed June instrument and full new proposal. Compare action, obligor, timing, conditions, verification, and counterpart obligations separately. Which terms are unchanged and which are accelerated? Avoid adjudicating breach from one side's press account.
- **Publication-safe wording:** Araqchi says the requested actions already appear in the June MOU. An inspected draft shares those subjects but has distinct conditions and timing; exact equivalence remains unverified.
