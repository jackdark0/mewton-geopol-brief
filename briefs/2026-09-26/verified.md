## Audit method

The three scout files were complete and were preserved. This audit re-opened the strongest source records, searched for primary documents and contrary evidence, checked publication times against the fixed cutoff, split compound claims, and separated verdict from confidence. The Pangburn AI Empirical Fact-Checking System and the Pangburn Analysis Forensic Fact-Checking Replication Standard were applied as separate sibling standards; their numbering systems are not merged.

The labels below describe the evidence available by the cutoff:

- **CONFIRMED:** strongly established by direct or convergent evidence.
- **SUPPORTED:** credible evidence supports the proposition, but a material limitation remains.
- **CLAIMED:** a relevant actor or source asserts it; independent verification is insufficient.
- **DISPUTED:** credible evidence materially conflicts.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** available evidence does not substantiate the proposition.

No selected-candidate count is used as a denominator for the world's events, any speaker's accuracy, or a misinformation rate.

## Story: Iran publishes a seven-day Hormuz plan while a reported U.S. rejection remains unconfirmed

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- **[CONFIRMED]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi publicly described a conditional plan under which hostilities would stop, the United States would lift its naval blockade, waive oil sanctions and release frozen Iranian funds, and Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days after U.S. acceptance. Reuters' inspected report says the countdown had not begun and that Tehran was waiting for a U.S. response. [Reuters via Geo](https://www.geo.tv/latest/683639-iran-proposes-reopening-hormuz-ending-war-within-seven-days)
- **[SUPPORTED]** The plan materially changes the public record because it supplies an attributable sequence and conditions rather than only anonymous descriptions of a possible phased deal. Exact operative text was not obtained.
- **[CLAIMED]** The Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed U.S. officials, reported that President Trump rejected the proposal and told aides he expected renewed bombing after the November midterms. Reuters explicitly said it could not immediately verify the report. [Reuters via Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/international/3982787-trump-rejects-iran-ceasefire-expects-renewed-bombing-after-midterms-wsj-reports)
- **[DISPUTED]** The nuclear component is internally unresolved in public reporting. The National reports that sources familiar with Tehran's position described dilution of 60%-enriched uranium followed by transfer abroad under an IAEA-recognized mechanism. Reuters separately quotes an unnamed senior Iranian official rejecting shipment of highly enriched uranium abroad while acknowledging possible dilution. The statements may refer to different material states or negotiating authority; neither source proves an authorized final term. [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/25/iran-offers-uranium-compromise-as-part-of-plan-to-break-us-deadlock-sources-say/) [Reuters via Internazionale](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/09/25/iran-will-make-no-nuclear-concessions-iranian-official-says)
- **[SUPPORTED]** An available June Islamabad memorandum draft contains overlapping subjects: termination of hostilities, phased blockade removal, oil waivers, access to frozen funds, and on-site down-blending under IAEA supervision. The American Presidency Project labels the text a draft with blank signing fields, so it cannot establish that every current demand was already in an authenticated final agreement. [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/islamabad-memorandum-understanding-between-the-united-states-america-and-the-islamic)
- **[SUPPORTED]** Hormuz is constrained rather than literally sealed to every oil movement. Reuters reports Kpler's projection that Saudi exports through Hormuz would rise from about 0.9 million barrels per day in August to 3.6 million in September. That does not establish normal, safe or unrestricted passage. [Reuters via gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/gulf-of-oman-ship-to-ship-oil-transfers-reach-limit-as-saudi-exports-surge/)

### Material claims not established

- That Washington delivered an official or final rejection before the cutoff.
- That renewed U.S. bombing after the midterms is an operational decision, timetable, or order.
- That the seven-day plan's full text was mutually accepted, authorized across Iran's power centers, or already in force.
- That Iran agreed to export uranium while it remained highly enriched, or that the reported dilution-then-transfer sequence is an authorized offer.
- That every current Iranian demand is identical in timing and conditions to an authenticated June agreement.

### Source provenance

- Araghchi's public remarks are one named Iranian official source carried by Reuters; they establish what he proposed publicly, not U.S. acceptance.
- The rejection and post-midterm bombing claims flow from unnamed U.S. officials to the Wall Street Journal and then to Reuters and other relays. Multiple republications are not independent confirmation.
- The National's uranium account and Reuters' unnamed-official account are separate reporting lineages, but source overlap and relative authority are unknown.
- The American Presidency Project record is directly inspectable but explicitly marked as a draft.
- Kpler, Vortexa and LSEG provide distinct commercial measurements inside the Reuters shipping report; their underlying datasets were not independently reproduced.

### Open questions

- Did Washington transmit a formal response, and if so, which version and condition did it reject?
- Does Iran's authorized nuclear position allow export only after dilution, or no export at all?
- What are the final June text, the current proposal text, and their action-by-action differences?
- What traffic baseline, vessel class and observation window underlie claims about effective closure or reopening?

### Recommendation

- **Include major.** Lead with the public proposal and reported rejection as separate propositions. Put the uranium and June-text issues in contested claims. Do not publish a future-bombing timetable as fact.

## Story: Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey begin translating their defense pact into military coordination

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- **[CONFIRMED]** The three countries' military chiefs met in Riyadh on September 25. Saudi Arabia's Defense Ministry said they agreed to deepen military and intelligence cooperation and integrate defensive capabilities under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement. [Arab News, carrying the ministry account](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/saudi-arabia-pakistan-and-turkiye-to-deepen-military-cooperation-amid-escalating-houthi-attacks-3003175)
- **[CONFIRMED]** The chiefs reaffirmed collective-defense commitments and said they would begin translating them into effective military cooperation. The same source records a separate meeting between Saudi Defense Minister Khalid bin Salman and Pakistan's army chief.
- **[SUPPORTED]** The meeting is a practical implementation step beyond the pact's signing because it moves discussion to intelligence sharing, defensive integration and staff coordination.

### Material claims not established

- No inspected source identifies new deployed units, interceptor transfers, combat missions, basing changes, an integrated command structure, or rules of engagement.
- The pact text itself was not independently authenticated during this audit. Arab News reports that it treats an armed attack against one as an attack against all three, but the legal and operational limits remain unavailable.
- The meeting does not establish that Pakistan or Turkey has entered combat against the Houthis.

### Source provenance

- The substantive account originates with the Saudi Defense Ministry and defense minister, relayed by Arab News. It is authoritative for the meeting and Saudi institutional position, but not independent evidence of implemented capability.
- Photographs credited to SPA and the ministry support occurrence of the meeting, not its operational consequences.

### Open questions

- Which units, sensors, interceptors, intelligence feeds or logistics systems will be shared?
- Will there be a standing headquarters, joint air-defense picture or replenishment mechanism?
- What domestic approvals or caveats limit each party's obligations?

### Recommendation

- **Include major.** Phrase it as coordination and implementation planning, not a new deployment or declaration of war.

## Story: ArcelorMittal says its Kryvyi Rih complex cannot safely and sustainably restart

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- **[CONFIRMED]** ArcelorMittal told Ukraine's government that it could not restart its Ukrainian subsidiary in a safe and sustainable manner after four missile strikes in five weeks. Its September 25 regulatory notice says the latest strike occurred September 21. [ArcelorMittal regulatory notice](https://corporate.arcelormittal.com/media/news/regulatory-news/arcelormittal-announces-that-it-is-unable-to-safely-and-sustainably-restart-operations-at-arcelormittal-kryvyi-rih-following-multiple-recent-missile-strikes)
- **[CONFIRMED]** The company said it would preserve infrastructure so restart options remain after peace. This is an inability to restart under current conditions, not a declared permanent abandonment.
- **[CONFIRMED]** ArcelorMittal expects an approximately $1 billion non-cash impairment, principally for property, plant and equipment. The amount is an accounting impairment, not a cash outflow or a measured loss to Ukraine's whole economy.
- **[CLAIMED]** ArcelorMittal attributes five deaths, 17 injuries and extensive production-facility damage to the four strikes. The company is direct on its operations and employees, but no independent casualty or damage audit was inspected.

### Material claims not established

- Permanent closure or demolition of the complex.
- The plant's exact pre-strike output, workforce impact, lost export volume, tax loss or replacement cost.
- Intentional targeting of civilians or the plant as such. Physical damage does not by itself establish targeting intent.

### Source provenance

- The regulatory notice is a direct corporate primary source and is strong evidence of the company's operating decision and expected accounting treatment.
- The company has an interest in describing damage and seeking state support; its strike and casualty account was not independently replicated here.

### Open questions

- What infrastructure will be preserved and at what cost?
- How much steel, iron ore and export capacity was operating immediately before the latest stoppage?
- What support or security changes would make a restart possible?

### Recommendation

- **Include major.** This is a strategic industrial effect, not merely another daily strike tally. Preserve the temporary-versus-permanent distinction.

[Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/25/eu-countries-agree-to-release-long-stalled-66-billion-in-military-aid-for-ukraine)

The Pentagon declined comment. [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-administration-to-meet-ukraine-assistance-deadline-ce785adfd18af125)
- **[SUPPORTED]** Reuters says initial deliveries began in September, while remaining deliveries are expected before October 2029. One source says the package excludes Patriot interceptors. This is one Reuters source chain, not independently inspected contracts or shipping records.
- **[SUPPORTED]** President Trump had publicly pledged in July to allow Ukrainian production of Patriot systems, but the scope and industrial pathway remained unspecified. AP reported that a license might cover interceptors, assembly or selected components and that production could take years. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/us-ukraine-russia-patriot-license-trump-797bbb29923bcba14f8e8ba652e98499)
- **[CLAIMED]** Zelensky's September 25 description of a final U.S. licensing decision is credible evidence of his account of a private exchange, but no current license document, variant, producer or schedule was inspected.

### Material claims not established

- That the EU's €6.6 billion had been legally released or paid by the cutoff.
- That the remaining U.S. $93 million was already obligated by the cutoff.
- That all $400 million was delivered, or that the package solves Ukraine's near-term air-defense shortage.
- That Ukraine possesses a complete, effective license for domestic production of full Patriot batteries or interceptors.

### Source provenance

- Kaja Kallas's announcement is the originating EU political source; Euronews provides direct reporting and the allocation breakdown.
- The U.S. obligation and delivery details come from three unnamed sources in one Reuters report. Multiple relays do not create independent confirmation.
- AP's July report is older context based on Trump's public pledge and named experts; it narrows what a license would mean but does not prove September execution.

### Open questions

- Which EU legislative acts remain, and when will each allocation become usable?
- Which U.S. contracts have been signed, for what items, and when are delivery milestones?
- What exactly does the Patriot license cover, and which technology-transfer or supply-chain steps remain?

### Recommendation

- **Include major.** Combine EU and U.S. developments around the commitment-versus-capability gap. Put the Patriot claim in context rather than treating it as immediate production.

## Story: Energy systems adapt to constrained Gulf flows, but Europe's problem is currently price and preparedness rather than physical shortage

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- **[SUPPORTED]** Reuters reports that ship-to-ship oil transfers in the Gulf of Oman are congested after Saudi Arabia redirected exports from Yanbu through Hormuz. Kpler projected September Saudi Hormuz exports at 3.6 million barrels per day versus about 0.9 million in August. Vortexa said transfer operations were taking nearly 10 days, up from five to seven. [Reuters via gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/gulf-of-oman-ship-to-ship-oil-transfers-reach-limit-as-saudi-exports-surge/)
- **[SUPPORTED]** The reporting identifies queues for tugboats, labor and transfer equipment and says buyers are considering India, Malaysia or direct delivery as alternatives. These are operational responses, not proof of a fixed physical ceiling.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The European Commission said on September 25 that gas supply remained stable despite lower storage than historical levels and that protected-customer supply was guaranteed under current regulatory conditions. [European Commission](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/gas-coordination-group-continues-work-winter-preparedness-2026-09-25_en)
- **[SUPPORTED]** In a letter inspected by Reuters, Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen asked governments to consider measures that sustain storage injections or reduce gas and electricity demand. Reuters, citing Gas Infrastructure Europe, reported EU storage at about 70%, 12 percentage points below the same time a year earlier. [Reuters via EnergyNow](https://energynow.com/2026/09/eu-warns-of-energy-price-crisis-asks-countries-to-consider-curbing-demand-letter-shows/)
- **[CONFIRMED]** The letter proposes consideration of measures; it does not enact EU-wide rationing. Reuters explicitly reports no current physical European supply shortage.

### Material claims not established

- A complete physical closure of Hormuz, a permanent transfer-capacity ceiling, or normalized safe passage.
- That a 2.7 million-barrel-per-day month-to-month increase equals new global supply; it is a route change and projection.
- That Europe is currently rationing energy, experiencing a physical gas shortage, or certain to miss winter needs.
- That every member state will adopt the commissioner's suggested demand measures.

### Source provenance

- Reuters combines trade-source interviews with Kpler, Vortexa, Oil Brokerage and LSEG data. These are named commercial strands but not a public raw dataset.
- Saudi Aramco declined comment and Bahri did not respond.
- The Commission record is primary for its security-of-supply assessment. Reuters' letter report is direct document reporting, while the storage figure comes from GIE.

### Open questions

- How much transfer delay is waiting time versus the operation itself?
- Can alternative transfer sites absorb redirected volumes without new bottlenecks?
- Which EU states will adopt demand restraints, and how quickly can storage injections improve?

### Recommendation

- **Include major.** Link maritime logistics to European preparedness while clearly separating high prices and vulnerability from actual shortage.
