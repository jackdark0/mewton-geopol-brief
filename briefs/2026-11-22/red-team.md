## Adversarial findings

### 1. The Gulf lead risks an attribution braid

**Failure mode:** Readers may hear Bahrain's attribution, the IRGC's target claim, UKMTO's projectile reports and Iran's mine account as one proven campaign.

**Correction:** Use separate sentences and separate verbs. Bahrain **said** Iran attacked a facility. UKMTO **reported** two tankers hit by unknown projectiles. Iran **claimed** a mine incident. No source inspected joins those facts.

**Forbidden headline:** “Iran attacks three tankers and U.S. bases.”

### 2. Bahrain's casualty line is a cutoff trap

**Failure mode:** Later reporting adds casualties, but it appeared after 02:00 ET.

**Correction:** If casualties are mentioned, say Bahrain's preliminary cutoff-time report said no injuries. Do not update the number from later Sunday coverage.

### 3. Sparta II attribution is still an assessment

**Failure mode:** “Ukraine struck” turns a Finnish likelihood assessment and a Russian accusation into an independently proven fact.

**Correction:** “Finland assessed a Ukrainian attack as likely.” Also remove any statement that the ship carried military equipment. Its prior logistics links and sanctions do not establish voyage cargo.

### 4. The Russian helicopter may be rescue spillover, not escalation

**Failure mode:** Treating the suspected airspace violation as a deliberate Russian test inflates the incident.

**Correction:** State that Finland investigated it and officials left open a rescue or monitoring link. The important point is incident-management complexity.

### 5. COP31's state is a draft, not an outcome

**Failure mode:** “COP31 dropped fossil fuels” implies an adopted decision.

**Correction:** “The newest presidency draft omitted fossil-fuel language.” Repeat near the top that it had not been adopted by the cutoff. Do not import later EU reactions or the final disposition.

### 6. The electrification denominator must remain explicit

**Failure mode:** “35 percent clean electricity” changes both the numerator and denominator.

**Correction:** The target is electricity's share of **global final energy consumption**, not the clean share of electricity generation. The draft does not by itself specify the fuel mix.

### 7. The autonomous-weapons story is vulnerable to treaty inflation

**Failure mode:** “States agreed rules” or “states agreed a treaty” overstates a procedural mandate.

**Correction:** Say states agreed to elaborate an instrument and recommend by 2028 whether it should become a binding protocol. Consensus and legal form remain unresolved.

### 8. SOFA does not entail a base

**Failure mode:** A Status of Forces Agreement sounds operationally larger than the public evidence.

**Correction:** Describe it as a legal framework for potential personnel and activities. The public declaration does not disclose basing rights, deployments, force size or jurisdictional detail. Also note that the signature occurred near the front edge of the approximate window; do not call it a same-morning development.

### 9. The Saudi item lacks a second evidence line

**Failure mode:** Repetition by a news outlet appears to independently corroborate the coalition.

**Correction:** Attribute launch count, target and interceptions to the coalition in one compact Rapid Fire item. Al Jazeera is a transmission and context source, not independent confirmation.

### 10. Calendar precision

**Failure mode:** Calling November 29 part of the verified seven-day calendar without a start time can put it outside the 168-hour horizon from the 02:00 cutoff.

**Correction:** Calendar should end November 28. Mention the Gagauzia runoff only in Rapid Fire, not the core calendar. Use OPCW **31st** session, not 30th.
