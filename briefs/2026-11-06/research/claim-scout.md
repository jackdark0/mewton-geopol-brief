# Claim Scout — 2026-11-06

## Methodology and timing

- **Frozen research cutoff:** 2026-11-06 02:00:00 America/New_York (2026-11-06 07:00:00 UTC).
- **Approximate discovery window:** 2026-11-04 20:00:00 EST through the cutoff (2026-11-05 01:00:00 UTC through 2026-11-06 07:00:00 UTC).
- **No-post-cutoff rule:** No fact first reported or source first published after the frozen cutoff is used as evidence. Material with ambiguous publication timing was not retained. Older sources appear only where they establish provenance or the state of a continuing claim; they do not substitute for current verification.
- **Method:** Claims below are atomized at proposition level. Each assessment distinguishes an observable or documented core from any stronger inference about motive, state direction, completed delivery, agreement, or future outcome. Multiple reports based on the same official statement, anonymous source, leaked document set, or orbital dataset are treated as one source lineage rather than independent confirmation. Search snippets and summaries were used only to find sources, not as evidence.
- **Assessment vocabulary:** **Supported** means the retained proposition is directly established by inspected evidence; **plausible** means evidence points toward it but material proof is missing; **unclear** means the available evidence cannot resolve it; **weak** means the evidence offered does not adequately support the proposition as stated; **false** is reserved for propositions contradicted by stronger evidence. Confidence is reported separately.

## Claim 1 — Iran transmitted a specific nuclear proposal to the United States through Qatar, and Washington is reviewing it

**Timing:** The claim emerged on 2026-11-05 and was reported before the cutoff. Qatar had publicly described an ongoing exchange of proposals by 2026-11-03.

**Origin / principal promoters:** A Reuters report attributes the proposition to one unnamed senior Iranian official. Axios, citing unnamed regional sources, separately describes a Qatari draft sequencing de-escalatory steps. Qatar publicly promotes its mediator role but has not publicly authenticated the specific document described by Reuters or the exact Axios sequence.

**Why it matters:** If accurate, this is evidence of an active negotiating channel during the war and a potentially testable path from maritime de-escalation to resumed nuclear talks. The distinction between a proposal being transmitted, a Qatari discussion paper, and terms accepted by Iran or the United States is strategically important.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- Reuters reports that an unnamed senior Iranian official said Iran sent a nuclear proposal to the United States through Qatar and that Washington was reviewing it. The same source said discussion would begin only after Washington met seven Iranian demands; the proposal itself was not published.
- Axios reports, from unnamed regional sources, a Qatari two-day sequence under which US steps and an Iranian invitation to IAEA inspectors would precede reopening the Strait of Hormuz and resumption of nuclear talks.
- Qatar's foreign ministry confirms high-level Qatari-Iranian coordination and, in an earlier statement, says Doha was facilitating an exchange of proposals between Washington and Tehran.

**Evidence against / missing:** No inspected US statement confirms receipt or review of the alleged Iranian proposal. No inspected Qatari statement authenticates the exact document, its authorship, the seven demands, or the Axios sequence. The Reuters and Axios descriptions may refer to different artifacts: an Iranian proposal, a Qatari bridging paper, or informal sequencing ideas. Neither document is public.

**Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Official Qatari statements independently corroborate the mediator channel and exchange of proposals, but not the exact proposition that Iran transmitted this specific nuclear proposal or that Washington was reviewing it. Reuters and Axios rely on different unnamed-source descriptions, but the overlap is insufficient to establish that they describe the same document.

**Current assessment:** **Plausible, but qualified.** The active Qatari channel is supported. The specific transfer, authorship, contents, and US review status remain unverified. It would be premature to describe the reported sequence as an agreed framework.

**Confidence:** **Medium.** Confidence is raised by Qatar's public confirmation of mediation and proposal exchange, and lowered by anonymous sourcing, absence of a document, and ambiguity over whether reports describe the same proposal.

**Sources:**

- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2026/11/05/iran-sent-nuclear-proposal-to-the-us-via-qatar-senior-iranian-official-tells-reuters/) — supports the claim that an unnamed Iranian official described transmission of a proposal and US review; does not independently verify it.
- [Axios, Nov. 5](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/05/trump-iran-war-national-security-team-decision) — supports the reported Qatari two-day sequence and identifies its sourcing as regional officials; does not show acceptance by either belligerent.

- [Qatar Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Nov. 3](https://mofa.gov.qa/en/qatar/latest-articles/latest-news/details/2026/11/03/advisor-to-prime-minister--official-spokesperson-for-ministry-of-foreign-affairs--qatar-continues-its-efforts-to-bridge-gap-between-washington--tehran) — confirms Qatar was facilitating an exchange of proposals; older context, not proof of the Nov. 5 document.

Are the seven demands and two-day sequence compatible, or are they competing proposals? Did either side accept any step before the cutoff?

## Claim 2 — The Russia-Ukraine war is close to a broad settlement involving all of Europe

**Timing:** President Donald Trump made the claim on 2026-11-05 after meeting Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev. The relevant reports were published before the cutoff.

**Origin / principal promoters:** Trump is the principal promoter. The claim followed his meeting with Dmitriev at the White House. US envoys separately met Ukrainian, NATO, and European officials in Brussels, but no inspected Ukrainian or European source endorsed Trump's forecast or described a common settlement text.

**Why it matters:** A genuine multilateral settlement would materially alter the war, Europe's security architecture, sanctions, and alliance commitments. Treating a forecast as a negotiated outcome could obscure the lack of public terms and the absence of Ukraine from the meeting Trump cited.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- Trump said the war was close to settlement and described the expected agreement as broader than Ukraine and involving Europe.
- Trump met Dmitriev, while other US representatives were engaged with Ukraine, NATO, and European officials in Brussels.
- A narrow, temporary ceasefire around Oleshky had already produced a discrete humanitarian result, showing that limited arrangements were possible.

**Evidence against / missing:** No public draft, agreed principles, territorial formula, security guarantee, sanctions sequence, or implementation mechanism was available. The Ukrainian side was not present at Trump's meeting with Dmitriev. No inspected Ukrainian, European, or NATO statement confirmed that a broad agreement was near. Reuters notes that Trump has repeatedly predicted that a deal was close.

**Independent corroboration:** **No for proximity to settlement; yes for the meetings.** AP, Reuters, and the White House independently establish diplomatic activity and the earlier localized ceasefire. They do not independently establish that a comprehensive agreement was near.

**Current assessment:** **Weak as a claim of imminent settlement.** Active diplomacy is supported. The stronger proposition that the war was close to a broad, Europe-wide agreement was a forward-looking assertion without inspectable terms or multi-party assent at the cutoff.

**Confidence:** **High** that the imminence claim was not substantiated publicly by the cutoff; **low** confidence about private negotiating progress because key talks and texts are not public.

**Sources:**

- [Associated Press, Nov. 5](https://apnews.com/article/ukraine-melania-trump-oleshky-ceasefire-putin-envoy-3ae35835dbc87ab348478c62c6c25ee3) — supports Trump's statement, his meeting with Dmitriev, the separate Brussels engagement, and the limited Oleshky ceasefire context.
- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-he-met-putin-envoy-sees-ukraine-deal-soon-ce785fdddd89fe21) — supports Trump's forecast and the absence of disclosed details; notes his earlier similar forecasts.
- [White House, Nov. 5](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/11/first-lady-melania-trump-unites-russia-and-ukraine-at-the-white-house-following-historic-ceasefire/) — primary evidence for the administration's presentation of the localized Oleshky ceasefire; does not establish a comprehensive settlement.

**Questions for auditor:** Is there a public joint statement or convergent Ukrainian, Russian, European, or NATO account of agreed terms? What issues, if any, were resolved in the Dmitriev meeting? Is the claimed Europe-wide scope a security framework, a sanctions arrangement, or rhetorical framing? What concrete milestone would make “close” falsifiable?

## Claim 3 — Two tankers and a tugboat caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, one after hitting a mine, and Iran seized two US drones

**Timing:** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps made the claims late on 2026-11-04 or early 2026-11-05, within the research window. All retained reporting predates the cutoff.

**Origin / principal promoters:** The IRGC and Iranian state media are the sole identified origin for the exact three-vessel account and the two-drone seizure. Reuters, AP, and Tasnim transmit that origin rather than independently confirming the events.

**Why it matters:** Verified attacks or accidents involving multiple commercial vessels would affect Hormuz transit risk, insurance, oil prices, and escalation calculations. A US drone seizure could imply a separate military encounter. The claims are especially vulnerable to wartime information operations because vessel identities and locations were withheld.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- The IRGC said one tanker hit a mine, another supertanker caught fire, and a tugboat caught fire after passing through the strait. Iranian reporting also said two US drones were seized.
- Oil-market reporting shows traders were reacting to tanker-attack reports and to a wider series of maritime incident reports in the region.

**Evidence against / missing:** No vessel names, flags, IMO numbers, coordinates, crew accounts, casualty figures, satellite imagery, owner statements, insurer notices, or mine-recovery evidence were supplied. AP said the claims could not be independently verified. No inspected US statement confirmed loss or seizure of two drones. The UK Maritime Trade Operations public site did not provide an identifiable notice matching the exact three-vessel account during this review. Broader incident counts cannot be used to confirm these particular vessels.

**Independent corroboration:** **No for the exact incidents.** Reuters, AP, and Tasnim trace the account to the IRGC/state media. Market reaction and other maritime reports corroborate elevated regional risk, not these particular events.

**Current assessment:** **Unclear.** The reported incidents may have occurred, but the exact claim remains unverified and single-lineage. The mine mechanism and drone seizure require separate proof and should not be bundled as established facts.

**Confidence:** **High** that public evidence was insufficient at the cutoff; **low** confidence on whether the underlying incidents occurred because wartime maritime information can emerge with delay.

**Sources:**

## Claim 4 — Leaked documents prove Russia delivered 90 helicopter-launched missile systems to Iran

**Timing:** The document reporting appeared on 2026-11-05. The underlying schedule is described as a May 2024 production document covering planned work through December 2025.

**Origin / principal promoters:** Dallas Analytics obtained and shared the leaked Russian documents. The Guardian and The Insider analyzed the same underlying document set. Some headlines and social retellings compress a production/export plan into a completed delivery.

**Why it matters:** Confirmed delivery of 90 launchers for LMUR-class missiles would indicate a substantial expansion of Iranian stand-off strike capacity and deeper Russian military support during the Iran war. Confusing a schedule with delivery overstates both current capability and the evidence for wartime Russian resupply.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- A leaked Vympel schedule reportedly lists 90 launchers under export project K-10 and assigns production or delivery milestones from late 2024 through December 2025.
- Separate documents in the leak reportedly associate K-10 with Iran.
- Public imagery has supported prior delivery of Russian helicopters to Iran, making the launcher plan technically and commercially coherent.

**Evidence against / missing:** Both the Guardian and The Insider explicitly say the documents do not establish that the launchers were delivered. No shipping record, acceptance certificate, serial list, Iranian inventory record, imagery of the launchers in Iran, or official confirmation was produced. Guardian and The Insider are not independent evidence for delivery because both rely on the Dallas Analytics document set. Helicopter delivery does not entail launcher delivery.

**Independent corroboration:** **No for completed delivery.** There is multi-outlet analysis of one leaked-document lineage. The supported core is a planned Russian production/export program associated with Iran, not demonstrated fulfillment.

**Current assessment:** **Weak** as a completed-delivery claim; **plausible** as evidence of a procurement or production arrangement. The safe formulation is that leaked documents describe a plan tied to Iran for 90 launchers, while actual delivery remains unproven.

**Confidence:** **High** that the inspected documents, as reported, do not prove delivery. **Medium** confidence in the plan's authenticity and Iranian destination because the original full document set and authentication chain were not available here.

**Sources:**

- [The Guardian, Nov. 5](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/05/russia-agreed-to-supply-iran-90-helicopter-missile-launchers) — reports the schedule, K-10/Iran linkage, and the explicit limitation that delivery is not established.
- [The Insider, Nov. 5](https://www.theins.org/en/news/299223) — analyzes the same Dallas Analytics material, distinguishes confirmed helicopter imagery from unclear launcher shipment, and therefore is same-lineage corroboration rather than independent proof.
- [Daily Beast, Nov. 5](https://www.thedailybeast.com/trumps-claim-that-vladimir-putin-is-not-helping-tehran-crumbles-as-secret-russia-iran-deal-revealed) — illustrates the stronger “deal revealed” framing while also acknowledging that the documents do not confirm delivery.

**Questions for auditor:** Can Dallas Analytics provide hashes, full documents, provenance, and authentication? Is K-10 unambiguously Iran in contemporaneous records? Are there customs, logistics, satellite, parade, exercise, or battlefield indicators of launcher arrival? Does “90 launchers” refer to launch rails, complete launcher assemblies, or another unit of count?

## Claim 5 — Russia's Cosmos 2612 satellite rehearsed an attack on ICEYE X36

**Timing:** The latest close-approach reporting was published 2026-11-05 before the cutoff. The observed maneuvers form a continuing sequence documented earlier in 2026.

**Origin / principal promoters:** Commercial orbital observers identified the close approach. Space-security specialist Greg Gillinger characterized it as an attack rehearsal; former US space official John DiNanno said the behavior was consistent with on-orbit weapons operations. Russia had not supplied an explanation in the inspected reporting.

**Why it matters:** ICEYE synthetic-aperture radar satellites support Ukrainian surveillance. A demonstrated Russian ability to rendezvous with such a satellite could indicate counterspace preparation and raise escalation and resilience questions. Motive must be separated from the observable orbital behavior.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- Independent orbital tracking showed Cosmos 2612 maneuvering into unusually close proximity to ICEYE X36 after a sequence analysts described as deliberate.
- Specialists said the geometry could support inspection, intelligence collection, electronic interference, or a kinetic attack.
- The behavior reportedly repeated or extended a prior approach sequence, making accidental conjunction less likely.

**Evidence against / missing:** A close approach does not reveal intent. No weapon deployment, physical contact, destructive effect, command record, Russian admission, or attack order was observed. ICEYE X36 remained productive in the reporting. Analysts identified non-attack possibilities, including inspection and intelligence collection. “Attack rehearsal” is an expert inference, not an observed event.

**Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Multiple tracking and open-source analyses corroborate a deliberate close approach. They do not independently establish an attack rehearsal or Russia's intended end state; some analyses may rely on overlapping public orbital elements.

**Current assessment:** **Plausible but unproven.** Deliberate, potentially threatening rendezvous behavior is supported. The narrower motive claim that it rehearsed an attack remains speculative and should be attributed to analysts.

**Confidence:** **High** on the close approach and deliberate maneuvering; **low-to-medium** on attack-rehearsal intent because the same observable behavior permits several explanations.

**Sources:**

- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Russia%2Bmay%2Bhave%2Brehearsed%2Battack%2Bon%2Bsatellite,%2Bspecialist%2Bsays,%2Bflagging%2Banother%2Bapproach/27292594.html) — supports the latest approach, expert interpretations, alternative purposes, lack of Russian comment, and continued X36 operation.
- [Ars Technica, Oct. 2026](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/10/russian-spacecraft-maneuvers-in-extreme-proximity-to-satellite-used-by-ukraine/) — older context supporting the prior deliberate approach sequence based on commercial tracking; does not establish motive.
- [KeepTrack open-source analysis](https://keeptrack.space/deep-dive/cosmos-2612-iceye-x36-rendezvous) — supports orbital reconstruction while documenting data uncertainty and limits.
- [Axios, Oct. 17](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/17/russia-ukraine-satellite-space) — older report supporting LeoLabs' deliberate-approach assessment while noting uncertainty about intent.

**Questions for auditor:** What is the closest-approach distance and uncertainty envelope from independent sensor networks? Did X36 show communications, attitude, or payload anomalies? Does Cosmos 2612 carry any observed manipulator, jammer, or other payload? Have US Space Command, ICEYE, or Russia issued a primary statement? What observation would distinguish inspection from attack rehearsal?

## Claim 6 — Finland's Fitburg verdict proves Russia directed sabotage of Baltic undersea infrastructure

**Timing:** The Helsinki District Court announced its judgment on 2026-11-05 before the cutoff. The judgment was not final because the defendants could appeal.

**Origin / principal promoters:** The court convicted the Russian captain and Azerbaijani boatswain of aggravated sabotage. A stronger state-direction narrative may arise from the captain's nationality, the ship's route or ownership context, and the strategic target, but the inspected court release and reporting do not attribute the conduct to the Russian state.

**Why it matters:** The difference between criminal sabotage by crew members and state-directed hybrid warfare changes the appropriate legal, diplomatic, and alliance response. Nationality is not evidence of state command.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- The district court found that the anchor was intentionally dragged for about 130 kilometers, damaging two cables and endangering other cables and a gas pipeline.
- The Russian captain and Azerbaijani boatswain were convicted of aggravated sabotage and received prison sentences.
- The infrastructure and Baltic context make hostile-state involvement a reasonable investigative question.

**Evidence against / missing:** The court release does not identify a Russian government order, handler, payment, command relationship, or state objective. AP reports that the court did not find the captain explicitly ordered the anchor lowered, although it found both defendants knew what was happening. An earlier investigator did not publicly speculate that a state actor was responsible. The defendants denied wrongdoing and planned to appeal, so the judgment was not final.

**Independent corroboration:** **Yes** for the conviction and intentional sabotage finding through the primary court release, Reuters, and AP. **No** for Russian state direction.

**Current assessment:** **False as stated if presented as proof of Russian state direction.** The verdict supports intentional sabotage by the convicted individuals at the district-court stage. It does not prove direction by the Russian state.

**Confidence:** **High.** The primary court summary defines what was adjudicated, while the missing state-attribution evidence is central rather than peripheral. Confidence does not extend to questions that may emerge in an appeal or intelligence investigation.

**Sources:**

- [Finnish Courts, Nov. 5](https://www.tuomioistuimet.fi/ajankohtaista/tiedotteet/karajaoikeus-on-antanut-tuomion-ns-fitburg-asiassa/) — primary source for the verdict, intentional anchor drag, damage, sentences, and non-final status; does not attribute state direction.
- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Finland%2Bcourt%2Bconvicts%2BRussian%2Bcaptain,%2BAzerbaijani%2Bcrew%2Bmember%2Bover%2BBaltic%2BSea%2Bcable%2Bdamage/27292911.html) — corroborates the convictions, sentences, denials, and planned appeal.
- [Associated Press, Nov. 5](https://apnews.com/article/cad289ba853cf13b126e9e44ee87ea04) — adds the court's finding on knowledge versus an explicit order and the earlier investigator's caution about a state actor.

**Questions for auditor:** Does the full judgment discuss motive, communications, payments, ownership, or external tasking beyond the public summary? Have Finnish police, prosecutors, intelligence services, or the government made a separate state-attribution assessment? What remains open on appeal? Avoid converting citizenship into command attribution.

## Claim 7 — China's government or a Chinese state-backed group directed the Crafty Falcon intrusions against Indian financial firms

**Timing:** India's Computer Emergency Response Team issued its advisory on 2026-11-04. Reuters reported on it on 2026-11-05 before the cutoff.

**Origin / principal promoters:** CERT-In identified a campaign it calls Crafty Falcon and named several AI-enabled offensive tools. Reuters reports that researchers have linked at least two of the tools to China, but CERT-In did not identify the attacker, its location, or a state sponsor. The state-attribution claim is an inference from tooling and broader geopolitical context.

**Why it matters:** State attribution could turn cybercrime or espionage into a bilateral security issue between nuclear-armed neighbors. Publicly available tools create a provenance trap: a tool's developer, prior user, language, or reported origin does not by itself identify the operator in a particular intrusion.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- CERT-In documented a campaign against Indian financial targets and use of ARTEX, PentAGI, Cairn, and CyberStrikeAI.
- Reuters says researchers have linked at least two tools to China.

**Evidence against / missing:** CERT-In's public advisory does not attribute Crafty Falcon to China, a named group, or a state sponsor. Reuters says the tools are publicly available and that the advisory did not say who was behind the breaches or where the attacker was located. No command-and-control registration, infrastructure overlap, malware lineage, operator-language artifact, victim telemetry, or government attribution was presented to connect the incidents to the Chinese state.

**Independent corroboration:** **No for Chinese state direction.** CERT-In independently supports the campaign and tool-use proposition. The China link concerns some tools, not the identity or sponsor of the operator in these intrusions.

**Current assessment:** **Weak.** The campaign and use of AI-enabled offensive tooling are supported. Attribution to China, much less the Chinese state, is unsupported by the public evidence inspected.

**Confidence:** **High** that the public advisory does not establish state attribution; **low** on the true actor because technical indicators may be held privately.

**Sources:**

- [CERT-In advisory CIAD-2026-0058, Nov. 4](https://cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0058&pageid=PUBVLNOTES02) — primary evidence for the Crafty Falcon campaign and named tools; does not publicly attribute an actor or state sponsor.
- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/India%2Bflags%2BChinese-linked%2BAI%2Btools%2Bin%2Bhacking%2Bcampaign%2Bagainst%2Bfinancial%2Bfirms/27292881.html) — supports the reported China associations for some tools and explicitly records the attribution limits and public availability of the tools.

**Questions for auditor:** Can CERT-In or affected firms provide indicators that tie the operator, rather than the tools, to a known Chinese cluster? Which researchers made the tool-origin links, and what exactly did they establish? Were the tools modified or deployed through infrastructure shared with prior attributed campaigns? Is there any Indian government attribution beyond the advisory?

## Claim 8 — India and the Philippines are finalizing a $700–800 million purchase of five BrahMos systems and 60 missiles

**Timing:** Reuters published the claim on 2026-11-05 before the cutoff. The negotiations concern an expansion of an existing Philippines-India missile relationship.

**Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters attributes the exact quantity, value, and “final stage” characterization to three unnamed sources with direct knowledge. A Philippine defense spokesperson confirms President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. wants additional BrahMos systems, and the defense secretary confirms they are on a procurement wish list, but neither publicly confirms the reported terms or a finalized agreement.

**Why it matters:** A purchase at this scale would expand Philippine coastal denial capacity in the South China Sea and deepen Indian defense exports into Southeast Asia. The strategic signal differs depending on whether this is political interest, budget planning, final negotiation, or a signed contract.

**Evidence offered for it:**

- Reuters' three direct-knowledge sources describe talks for five systems, 60 missiles, and a value of roughly $700–800 million.
- Philippine officials publicly confirm an intention to acquire more BrahMos systems and their presence on a defense wish list.

**Evidence against / missing:** No contract, notice of award, budget authorization, delivery schedule, offset agreement, or joint announcement was public. The exact figures and stage rely on unnamed sources. India's defense ministry, the Philippine defense ministry, and BrahMos Aerospace did not provide confirmation to Reuters. A procurement wish list is not a signed sale.

**Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Philippine official comments corroborate demand for more BrahMos systems. They do not independently corroborate five systems, 60 missiles, the price, or that talks were in the final stage.

**Current assessment:** **Plausible, but qualified.** Expansion talks and Philippine interest are supported. The exact package and proximity to agreement remain unconfirmed; describe them as reported negotiating terms, not a completed sale.

**Confidence:** **Medium.** Three direct-knowledge sources and official interest raise confidence, while anonymity and the absence of a contract or official term sheet limit it.

**Sources:**

- [Reuters syndication, Nov. 5](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/India%2Bin%2Bfinal-stage%2Btalks%2Bto%2Bexpand%2Bmissile%2Bsale%2Bto%2BPhilippines,%2Bsources%2Bsay/27292896.html) — supports the anonymous-source package details and the narrower official confirmation of Philippine interest; records the lack of ministry or company confirmation.

**Questions for auditor:** Is there a Philippine budget line, Indian export approval, contract notice, or official package description? Does “system” mean a battery, launcher group, or another unit? Are the 60 missiles additional rounds or inclusive of training and reserves? Has either government moved from a wish list to binding procurement?
