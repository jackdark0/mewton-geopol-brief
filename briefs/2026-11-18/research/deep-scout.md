## Candidate: Allied disagreement shifts from weapons quantities to Ukraine's theory of effect

- **Underlying development:** The United States urged front-line and midrange support and argued that deep strikes are counterproductive; German and British ministers publicly rejected that logic. Ukraine asked for technologies to attack ballistic-missile launchers and more long-range capability.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The visible quarrel can obscure the operational issue: allocation of scarce interceptors, strike weapons and political permission.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Aid priorities can shape Ukraine's target set, defense-industrial spending and bargaining leverage even without a formal prohibition.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Public ministerial statements; U.S. emphasis on front-line systems; Ukrainian request; no inspected binding aid directive.
- **What remains inference:** Whether Washington will condition transfers or intelligence support; whether deep strikes harden Russian resolve or materially reduce war capacity.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/colby-ukraine-deep-long-nato-strikes-starshield-e570c549277a14f97ad24319f2f1bf7e); [NATO](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2026/11/17/opening-remarks-by-the-nato-secretary-general-at-the-udcg); [Ukraine](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/shorazu-rosiya-obiraye-novu-eskalaciyu-tozh-ukrayinski-raket-107369).
- **Questions for auditor:** Separate effectiveness against front-line advance, war economy, public morale and bargaining—four different claims.

## Candidate: Iran creates a reciprocal-strike ladder around Bahrain and Hormuz

- **Underlying development:** Iran attacked a U.S. radar facility in Bahrain; the United States struck an alleged IRGC launch site on Qeshm.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The individual attacks caused little publicly reported damage, but their location connects Gulf bases, Iranian coastal systems and commercial transit.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Small attacks can normalize retaliation, raise force-protection burdens and let either side misread limited action as preparation for a larger campaign.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Three Iranian attacks on U.S. targets in Bahrain over five days; public interception/no-casualty claims; U.S. retaliatory-strike report.
- **What remains inference:** Iranian coercive theory, destroyed capability, and whether escalation is centrally bounded.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-trump-israel-bahrain-attacks-582e0b5d51be06f0ed630fac24f39b72).
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek direct CENTCOM, Bahraini and IRGC records; do not infer a new war phase from tempo alone.

## Candidate: Baghdad tests whether a militia-disarmament timetable has coercive content

- **Underlying development:** Iraq's prime minister announced a planned government entry into Kataib Hezbollah's Jurf al-Sakhar stronghold amid oil-smuggling allegations; the group threatened to resist.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** A local inspection dispute is also an institutional contest over armed sovereignty and revenue.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Successful entry could strengthen state enforcement and the mid-2027 disarmament plan; a retreat or clash could expose the state's dependence on militia consent.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Announced entry, public threat, official dismissals and investigation; no entry completed by cutoff.
- **What remains inference:** Smuggling scale, Qaani's mediation and whether either side intends combat.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iraq-iran-kataib-hezbollah-jurf-alsakhar-fed4063af6dbf61fd4fb6f5f5c3d278d).
- **Questions for auditor:** Watch Friday deadline, force composition, legal orders and militia withdrawals.

## Candidate: Gaza transition remains blocked by sequencing rather than missing institutions

- **Underlying development:** The Board of Peace says the technocratic administration, civilian police and international stabilization force cannot deploy because required political and security decisions remain absent; Israeli restrictions and Hamas activity persist.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Formal bodies and pledged funds create an appearance of implementation that the operational sequence does not support.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Each actor can cite another's noncompliance to delay its own step, producing a self-reinforcing implementation trap.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The Board's November report as inspected by AP; the U.N.'s September report already documented no NCAG entry or ISF deployment.
- **What remains inference:** Whether collapse is imminent and which single concession could unlock the sequence.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/board-of-peace-us-israel-gaza-hamas-189d58d05fa984cc9573f98ec7d638be); [U.N. September report](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/unsco-report-to-the-ad-hoc-liaison-committee-september-2026/).
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the November filing and map each obligation to responsible actor and prerequisite.

## Candidate: CS identification and Russian attribution must remain separate evidence layers

- **Underlying development:** OPCW-designated laboratories confirmed CS-related compounds in evidence from a 2024 incident; Ukraine's national investigation attributes the munition and delivery to Russian forces and alleges a broader organized supply system.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines compress technical assistance and national attribution into an OPCW attribution.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A well-supported attribution can raise treaty-compliance and sanctions costs; overstating OPCW's role would weaken the case.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Chemistry confirmation, national report, described military documents/training materials and an alleged Russian-source video.
- **What remains inference:** Authenticity and completeness of nonpublic documents; command level; prevalence beyond the incident.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [OPCW note](https://www.opcw.org/sites/default/files/documents/2026/11/c31s01%28e%29.pdf); [Ukraine national report](https://www.opcw.org/sites/default/files/documents/2026/11/c31nat01%28e%29.pdf); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/ea32f272904869d3423de0246140584b).
- **Questions for auditor:** Quote the legal prohibition but attribute the incident conclusion to Ukraine, not the OPCW.

## Candidate: Japan and the IEA start building a regional energy-shock institution

- **Underlying development:** Japan announced an IEA-Japan Oil Security Hub for Asia and linked it to POWERR Asia and AZEC 2.0.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** It is infrastructure for future crisis coordination, not a dramatic one-day oil release.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Shared rules, technical capacity and stockpile coordination can reduce unilateral hoarding and improve response speed during Hormuz disruptions.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Announced hub and leaders' political endorsement.
- **What remains inference:** Membership, binding authority, stock volumes and release triggers.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Sources:** [Japan](https://japan.kantei.go.jp/105/diplomatic/202611/1117azec.html); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/3d7df3b2038c83441874c8c4be09eda5).
