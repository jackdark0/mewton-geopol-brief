## Candidate: Washington and European allies split over Ukrainian deep strikes

- **Event time/date:** November 17, Brussels.
- **Region / actors:** United States, Ukraine, Germany, United Kingdom, NATO.
- **What changed:** U.S. Under Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby publicly said deep strikes had not helped end the war or blunt Russian advances and urged support for front-line and midrange operations. Germany's Boris Pistorius called criticism of legitimate strikes on non-civilian targets "quite a stupid thing," while Britain's Wes Streeting said deep strikes degrade Russia's war machine. Ukraine separately asked partners for long-range technology and weapons.
- **Why this may matter:** The dispute concerns the theory of victory and what allied aid should enable, not just one weapons package.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high.
- **Source chain:** AP directly observed the NATO event; NATO and Ukraine provide primary event records.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/colby-ukraine-deep-long-nato-strikes-starshield-e570c549277a14f97ad24319f2f1bf7e); [NATO opening record](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2026/11/17/opening-remarks-by-the-nato-secretary-general-at-the-udcg); [Ukraine](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/shorazu-rosiya-obiraye-novu-eskalaciyu-tozh-ukrayinski-raket-107369).
- **Questions for auditor:** Did Colby announce a binding aid restriction? What evidence supports either side's causal claim about deep strikes?
