## C-01 — Claim: Washington accepted the core conditions of Iran's seven-day proposal, while requesting additional clauses

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, in an interview with Iraq's Al Ekhbariya, relayed by Iranian state news agency IRNA. Attribution to Araghchi is **high confidence as reported**, with the full original interview not inspected.
- **Type / scope:** Diplomatic-position claim about a private US response; not a claim that a final agreement was signed or implemented. Relevant report: October 21. IRNA's inspected archive lists the item at 10:03 a.m. that date; the item page omits its timestamp and timezone.
- **Verbatim anchor:** IRNA attributes this to Araghchi: “The Americans informed us that they agreed to these conditions”. The same passage immediately adds that the Americans sought more clauses.
- **Why it matters:** A claim of acceptance can change perceptions of who is blocking a settlement and how close Hormuz reopening is. Acceptance with unresolved additions needs different treatment from an executable agreement.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [IRNA's interview account](https://irna.com/en/news/86298957/), paragraphs beginning with Araghchi's description of the initiative, directly records his account of Washington's answer.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No US response document, agreed text or mediator confirmation was inspected. Araghchi's own conditional sequence still places further negotiations after reopening. [The National's October 21 report](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/gulf/2026/10/21/araghchi-says-strait-of-hormuz-will-stay-closed-until-us-agrees-to-irans-terms/), body paragraphs on the Iraqi television interview and his Financial Times opinion article, reports continued contacts alongside his pessimism about progress. Those statements can coexist; they do not resolve what Washington accepted.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for actual US acceptance. Other outlets reporting this interview share Araghchi's underlying account. Reporting that contacts continue is not independent proof of the terms.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear.** Supported that Araghchi made the claim; the substantive private US position is unverified. Do not promote it to a completed deal or use absence of confirmation as proof it is false.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** in this classification, because the originating position is available but the other party's actual response is missing.
- **Strongest fair countercase:** Araghchi may accurately describe private assent that Washington has chosen not to announce. That possibility is why this remains unresolved rather than false.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Iran says the US accepted its proposed conditions with additions; the inspected record does not establish a final agreement.
- **Sources / lineage:** [IRNA item](https://irna.com/en/news/86298957/) is the direct state-media account of Araghchi's position; [IRNA archive](https://irna.com/en/allnews/) supplies its dated listing. [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/gulf/2026/10/21/araghchi-says-strait-of-hormuz-will-stay-closed-until-us-agrees-to-irans-terms/) is secondary context, not a second negotiating witness. Its displayed article update is October 21, 11:41 AM; unrelated navigation and suggested stories are excluded.
- **Questions for auditor:** Can a pre-cutoff US transcript, Qatari/Pakistani readout or response text establish whether assent was conditional? Does the full original interview support the translation? Distinguish acceptance of a framework, acceptance of every condition and implementation.

## C-02 — Claim: A supertanker carrying more than two million barrels struck an Iranian naval mine south of Hormuz and caught fire

- **Origin / principal promoters:** IRGC Navy through IRNA and other Iranian media. **High** attribution confidence to the institutional claim, not to event truth.
- **Type / scope:** Empirical event, quantity and mechanism. The parent proposition contains separately testable atoms: a particular ship was hit; it carried the stated cargo; the cause was a naval mine. None should inherit confirmation from another atom. The October 20 statement does not establish a precise incident time.
- **Why it matters:** A verified mine strike could change judgments about mine clearance, shipping risk and the need for further military action. An unidentified claimed strike cannot establish a new loss of that cargo volume.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [IRNA's report](https://irna.com/en/news/86298772/) directly records the IRGC account. Its archive dates publication to October 20, 11:36 p.m. This is a continuing lead whose publication likely predates the window if the archive uses Tehran time; the hour's timezone is not printed.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The statement supplies no ship name, IMO identifier, owner, verifiable coordinates or cargo manifest. [gCaptain's inspected October 20 article](https://gcaptain.com/iran-claims-more-tankers-hit-mines-in-hormuz-but-evidence-remains-elusive/), opening body paragraphs, notes missing identifiers and possible repetition across announcements. No independent event match was located in this search.
- **Chronology firewall:** [JMIC Update 106](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/update-106-jmic-advisory-note-18-october.pdf?rev=86f67da1821f4dcdb4e27868b6679729), page 2, addresses **October 17** mine allegations and has a cutoff of October 18 at 13:00 UTC. It cannot refute a genuinely later October 20 event. Its page 4 warning about attempted mining also prevents reading non-confirmation as a declaration that the waterway is mine-free.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for these three atoms. IRNA, Reuters relays and derivative coverage of the IRGC announcement form one claimant lineage. JMIC supplies distinct maritime monitoring, but the inspected dated report covers earlier incidents.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak / uncorroborated; HOLD on event identity and mechanism.** No basis to call it confirmed or definitively false.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that this packet lacks independent support; **low** on the underlying event's truth.
- **Denominator / quantity control:** More than two million barrels is a claimed cargo aboard an unidentified vessel, not measured oil lost, removed from supply or spilled. Do not sum it with earlier unnamed tanker claims: duplicate incidents have not been excluded.
- **Strongest fair countercase:** An owner might withhold a vessel's identity and AIS may be incomplete. Those limits weaken arguments from silence but supply no positive verification.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Iran claims an unnamed supertanker hit a mine; independent confirmation of the ship, cargo and cause was not located.
- **Sources:** [IRNA](https://irna.com/en/news/86298772/) establishes the claim; [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/iran-claims-more-tankers-hit-mines-in-hormuz-but-evidence-remains-elusive/) documents the identification gap; [JMIC PDF, pages 2 and 4](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/update-106-jmic-advisory-note-18-october.pdf?rev=86f67da1821f4dcdb4e27868b6679729) is carefully dated contrary/context evidence about earlier allegations and continuing risk.
- **Questions for auditor:** Find a pre-cutoff owner, flag-state, insurer or UKMTO incident match. Is the new statement a repeat of an earlier claim? Does imagery prove ship, place, date and mechanism? If no new evidence emerged in-window, omit this from the main daily change list and retain only as a continuing caution.

## C-03 — Claim: The US had issued mandatory departure orders across Gulf diplomatic missions by the cutoff

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Initial anonymous US-official reporting about wider departure preparations, easily expanded into a completed regional evacuation. Reuters' inspected updated account distinguishes Saudi orders from other possible family withdrawals. Do not attribute the broader completed-order formulation to Reuters as a verbatim statement.
- **Type / scope:** Administrative status, geography and timing. Mandatory order, staffing adjustment, planning, departure of family members, departure of non-emergency employees and evacuation of every employee are different propositions.
- **Why it matters:** The scope affects estimates of regional escalation risk. Staff protection does not itself establish a decision to attack Iran or a timetable for an attack.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [Reuters, inspected through MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/us-preparing-to-issue-ordered-departure-of-some-personnel-from-gulf-embassies-us-officials-ce785cd9dd8af526), lead and paragraph on four anonymous US officials, describes planned withdrawals of family members from other missions. Its displayed modification time is October 22, 04:43 IST, equivalent to October 21, 23:13 UTC: pre-cutoff.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** That same Reuters version reports spokesperson Tommy Pigott's narrower Saudi-only order. [AP's complete story via CityNews Vancouver](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/10/21/us-orders-some-diplomats-to-leave-saudi-arabia-as-fears-of-escalating-houthi-attacks-rise/), paragraph beginning with the order's geographic limitation, distinguishes other Gulf staffing changes. [Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/categories/middleeast/1079838), first three body paragraphs, reports the named spokesman's statement as well. Individual orders for other missions and completed evacuation records were not inspected.
- **Verbatim anchor:** Pigott's reported formulation is “non-emergency US government employees and their family members in Saudi Arabia only”. This is a reported quote, not an audio-verified transcript.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Reuters and AP are separate reporting organizations, but their confirmation of formal scope rests on the State Department. Anonymous plans have no inspected documentary confirmation. Rudaw repeating Pigott is additional access to the same statement, not another independent order.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak as the broad completed-order proposition; supported only in the narrower Saudi scope.** Broader planning may be real and compatible with the narrower order. The distinction is status and timing, not necessarily proof that one side lied.
- **Confidence:** **Medium**, because named on-record scope is clear but private plans and other missions' actual records are unavailable.
- **Strongest fair countercase:** Wider withdrawals could already be arranged informally or separately. A Saudi-only public order would not disprove that possibility.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Washington ordered non-emergency personnel and their families to leave Saudi Arabia; broader regional family withdrawals were reported as plans, with other staffing changes described separately.
- **Sources / access:** [MarketScreener's Reuters version](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/us-preparing-to-issue-ordered-departure-of-some-personnel-from-gulf-embassies-us-officials-ce785cd9dd8af526) provides the opposition within one report. [CityNews/AP](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/10/21/us-orders-some-diplomats-to-leave-saudi-arabia-as-fears-of-escalating-houthi-attacks-rise/) shows October 21 publication and last update at 13:41 local publisher time. [Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/categories/middleeast/1079838) is dated October 21. The [Saudi embassy's October 21 alert](https://sa.usembassy.gov/security-alert-u-s-mission-to-saudi-arabia-october-21-2026/) returned HTTP 403 and is **not inspected evidence**.
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve the actual alert/Pigott post and any separate mission orders available before cutoff. Which staff classes and countries are involved? Preserve the distinction between an order and people already leaving. Confirm that no later evacuation announcement is imported into this edition.

## C-04 — Continuing claim: Iran is delaying a deal primarily to damage Trump in the US midterms

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Anonymous Iranian officials and an insider in Reuters' October 20 reporting. This is an older continuing interpretation from yesterday, not a newly established October 21 policy decision.
- **Type / scope:** Motive and causal-priority claim. One electoral incentive is a weaker proposition than the assertion that it is the primary reason for delay.
- **Why it matters:** A predominantly electoral strategy implies different bargaining incentives from regime-security fears, distrust, incompatible nuclear terms or disputes over sequencing.
- **Evidence offered for it:** [Reuters' complete report via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/iran-prolongs-talks-stalemate-influence-102559000.html), opening paragraphs and the section on political pressure, quotes sources describing an intention to deny Trump a victory.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The same report describes fear of renewed attacks, distrust and an expert assessment emphasizing regime survival. No internal ranking of motives or binding decision record was published. October 21's [IRNA account of Araghchi's interview](https://irna.com/en/news/86298957/) supplies a public account of continuing contacts, not an independent test of private motive.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for the primary-motive formulation. [The News' October 21 publication](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1444167-iran-prolongs-talks-in-stalemate-to-influence-us-midterms-say-officials) is explicitly Reuters and is the same lineage, not a fresh confirmation. Anonymous sources' organizational independence cannot be established from the published account.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear** as a primary explanation; **plausible** as one incentive. Do not equate an official briefing with direct access to final strategic intent.
- **Confidence:** **Low** in ranking the motives. **Medium** that public evidence does not resolve that ranking.
- **Strongest fair countercase:** Officials with direct access may accurately report a deliberate electoral strategy. Their evidence should be attributed and retained; public security concerns might also be bargaining language. Neither alternative establishes which motive dominates.
- **Publication-safe correction:** Anonymous officials describe electoral leverage as part of Iran's strategy; the record also supports security and trust explanations and does not rank them decisively.
- **Sources:** [AOL/Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/iran-prolongs-talks-stalemate-influence-102559000.html) shows update October 20, 10:27 UTC, outside this edition's window; [The News/Reuters](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1444167-iran-prolongs-talks-in-stalemate-to-influence-us-midterms-say-officials) repeats the story on October 21. Both were inspected, with the duplicated lineage explicitly preserved.
- **Questions for auditor:** Is there genuinely new in-window evidence about motive? Otherwise do not recycle this as a major development. Seek direct documents or attributable statements before claiming motive priority; compare alternative security and sequencing explanations.
