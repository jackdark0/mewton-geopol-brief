## Candidate: Mecca pact moves from signature toward an operating alliance

- **Underlying development:** Turkey's presidential approval completes its domestic ratification of the joint-defence agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan. The published text creates collective-defence language, a Strategic, Political and Defence Committee, and cooperation on readiness, interoperability and defence industry.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Coverage often compresses the text into a Muslim-world "Article 5" and misses the clauses that preserve consultation, domestic law, needs and available capabilities.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Ratification creates a standing forum through which Saudi Arabia can seek Turkish and Pakistani military, intelligence, air-defence or industrial support during Houthi and Iran-related attacks. The agreement increases the political cost of refusing assistance, but it does not specify an automatic force commitment.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Treaty text; Turkish parliamentary approval; presidential ratification decision; active attacks on Saudi territory.
- **What remains inference:** Entry into force across all three parties; the form of assistance; whether nuclear deterrence, Turkish forces or combat operations are contemplated; whether the pact will be used in Yemen.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://legalbank.net/resmi-gazete-dosyalar/20261127/598f62475c664bbcaee9d5b3c214885b.pdf - Official Gazette treaty text and Turkish presidential approval.
  - https://cdn.tbmm.gov.tr/KKBSPublicFile/D28/Y5/T2/DosyaKomisyonRaporunuVerdi/cd70ebe9-711b-42fe-8baf-896ee6b8eb29.pdf - parliamentary ratification record.
  - https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/turkiye-enacts-law-approving-mecca-defense-pact-229381 - description of consultation and domestic-law conditions.
- **Questions for auditor:** Verify the exact entry-into-force clause and whether every party completed notifications. Separate treaty duty from political expectations arising from the Jazan attacks.

## Candidate: EU's 24-state Ukraine-financing coalition lacks a legal act

- **Underlying development:** Twenty-four leaders supported the additional 2027 Ukraine-financing proposal, with Bulgaria limiting participation to the non-military portion, but the European Council did not adopt it and will return in December.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines can turn broad political backing into an approved EUR45 billion loan or merge it with the separate, already adopted EUR45 billion allocation from the existing EUR90 billion facility.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Failure to reach unanimity delays procurement and budget certainty. A large supporting coalition may still create pressure for an alternative intergovernmental or enhanced-cooperation route, but no such workaround was adopted at cutoff.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Official Council result; 24-state support; Bulgarian limitation; December deferral.
- **What remains inference:** Which states blocked adoption; whether the Commission proposal will be amended; whether a coalition mechanism can lawfully substitute; timing and conditionality of any future disbursement.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**

## Candidate: Baghdad acquires judicial cover but not yet uncontested physical control

- **Underlying development:** Iraq's Federal Supreme Court backed federal authority to secure every international border, including Kurdistan-administered territory. Border Forces announced implementation of the prime minister's closure order, while Kurdistan officials and some local reporting said gates remained open.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The change is neither merely rhetorical nor a completed takeover. It is a three-part shift: executive order, judicial backing and the beginning of security implementation.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Federal control of border roads, customs and oil-export approval can reduce KRG revenue autonomy and increase Baghdad's leverage over disputed fields. Physical enforcement, however, requires cooperation or coercion against established Kurdish security structures.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Court ruling reported by AP; federal deployment and closure orders; Border Forces implementation statement; protests in Irbil.
- **What remains inference:** Which crossings or roads were actually closed; whether federal forces entered KRG-controlled sites; whether oilfield management changed; whether escalation becomes armed.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/iraq-krg-baghdad-oil-borders-kurds-protest-irbil-erbil-1b0e8cb02a09ce16b8885c60f4733a44 - court, deployments, protests and constitutional dispute.
  - https://news.iq/en/iraq-shuts-unofficial-crossings-border-control/ - Border Forces implementation statement based on INA.
  - https://theinsightinternational.com/iraq-border-forces-begin-closing-unofficial-crossings-kurdistan-gates-open-november-26/ - contrary local evidence on gate status.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain the judgment itself and distinguish borders from oil authority. Seek named-site evidence before using "takeover" or "closure" as accomplished facts.

## Candidate: Jazan converts Houthi reach from attempted disruption into observed operational effect

- **Underlying development:** A hostile aerial projectile caused a refinery fire, one death, injuries and a temporary halt to some operations. The coalition attributed it to the Houthis and claimed a 50-target response.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The important shift is not another launch count; it is verified human and operational effect at Saudi energy infrastructure after an earlier Ras Tanura attempt lacked demonstrated target damage.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Even a short interruption raises insurance, defence and redundancy costs and may encourage a Saudi escalation cycle. The military significance depends on which units stopped, the repair time and whether repeated attacks can defeat defences.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Saudi Energy Ministry's casualty, fire and partial-suspension statement; coalition attribution and counterstrike claim; JMIC risk advisory.
- **What remains inference:** Weapon origin and type; full damage; throughput loss; independent battle damage in Yemen; whether Saudi strategy has changed beyond a retaliatory sortie.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/666565/saudi-arabia/al-maliki-jazan-refinery-was-attacked-by-houthi-militia - Energy Ministry details.

## Candidate: A lethal Kuwait incident tests attribution under an active regional war

- **Underlying development:** Two Italian technicians died after a drone attack at a site supporting Kuwaiti Skyguard air-defence detection. Kuwait attributed the drones to Iran; Iran denied involvement and proposed a joint investigation.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The most consequential fact is not only the deaths but the mismatch between a declared Iranian attack and Tehran's denial during an existing war, with no public forensic chain yet available.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A confident but weakly evidenced attribution could trigger escalation with Iran; an unresolved attribution can also weaken deterrence. Italian casualties create alliance and domestic pressure even though the victims were civilians working under contract.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Kuwaiti detection/interception account; Italian confirmation of deaths and work site; Iranian denial.
- **What remains inference:** Drone ownership, launch origin, intended target, direct impact versus interceptor debris, and command responsibility.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.kuna.net.kw/ar/News?newsId=3340245 - Kuwaiti military statement.
  - https://www.esteri.it/it/sala_stampa/archivionotizie/comunicati/2026/11/attacco-con-droni-in-kuwait-due-cittadini-italiani-deceduti/ - Italian death and employment confirmation.

- **Questions for auditor:** What evidence did Kuwait use to identify Iran? Did Italy independently assess attribution? Were the deaths caused by the incoming drone, interception or secondary debris?

## Candidate: Badme front may be crossing from proxy/civil war into direct interstate combat

- **Underlying development:** ACLED says Ethiopian government-aligned forces fought Eritrean and Tigrayan units through the night and captured Gual Gemhalo before moving closer to Badme.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** It is a local control change in a dense and confusing set of Ethiopian factions, but the reported involvement of Eritrean state forces would transform the conflict category.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Sustained Ethiopian-Eritrean combat could draw scarce forces away from internal fronts, destabilize Red Sea access politics, and activate regional alignments involving Sudan, Egypt and Gulf actors.
- **What evidence is observable now:** ACLED event log; resident accounts of shelling; prior independently reported troop concentrations and artillery/drone exchanges.
- **What remains inference:** Direct Eritrean participation in this specific clash, border crossing, durable control, casualties, intent to widen the war, and outside sponsorship.
- **Initial priority:** high but evidence-limited.
- **Sources:**
  - https://acleddata.com/ethiopia-alerts-archive - event-level record.

## Candidate: North Korea-Russia rupture claim has high strategic value but one provenance chain

- **Underlying development:** Ukraine's Defence Intelligence says Pyongyang temporarily stopped missile and artillery shipments, Russian military flights and a requested troop expansion after a diplomatic dispute over South Korea.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The claimed suspension spans matériel, airspace and manpower rather than one shipment. Conversely, sensational summaries omit that every public account originates with a Ukrainian belligerent agency.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A real pause would reduce Russian access to KN-23 missiles and shells and give Pyongyang leverage over Moscow's Korea policy. A temporary pause could also be bargaining rather than alliance collapse.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The HUR publication; prior established military cooperation; no public North Korean/Russian confirmation.
- **What remains inference:** Actual stoppage, duration, inventory effect, motive, requested troop numbers, and whether existing North Korean units remain unchanged.
- **Initial priority:** high only as a contested claim; otherwise rapid fire.

## Candidate: Australia's proxy-attack sanctions make an intelligence judgment operational

- **Underlying development:** Australia imposed counter-terrorism financing sanctions on two named men it assesses orchestrated 2024 antisemitic arson attacks through proxies for the IRGC.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The action names alleged intermediaries for the first time and turns a broad ASIO attribution into individual legal restrictions.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Naming and freezing assets can disrupt networks and signal willingness to impose costs for state-directed activity abroad. The credibility of the deterrent depends on evidence, allied coordination and enforceable assets or travel links.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Ministerial release; sanctions action; earlier public ASIO attribution.
- **What remains inference:** The full chain from IRGC to the two men to local perpetrators; criminal liability; available assets; Iranian command knowledge.
- **Initial priority:** medium.

## Candidate: UNCTAD's flat-volume forecast measures aggregate stress, not uniform collapse

- **Underlying development:** The 2026 Review of Maritime Transport projects 0% growth in global seaborne-trade volume after 1.9% in 2025 and attributes the slowdown to chokepoint disruption and trade-policy uncertainty.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** It is a system-wide denominator that disciplines claims about Hormuz, Red Sea diversions and tariff shocks, but the aggregate hides regional and cargo divergence.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Longer routes absorb ship capacity, raise fuel and insurance costs, increase port congestion and shift relative advantage toward resilient hubs. Developing states with weak connectivity bear disproportionate costs.
- **What evidence is observable now:** UNCTAD report, forecast, regional port-call data and described corridor disruption.
- **What remains inference:** Causal share attributable to any one war or policy; final 2026 volume; distribution across cargo types; permanence into 2027.
- **Initial priority:** medium, likely rapid fire or context.
- **Sources:**
  - https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/rmt2026overview_en.pdf - report overview and methodology context.
  - https://unctad.org/news/global-shippings-changing-map-where-trade-connections-are-growing-and-vulnerabilities-are - regional port-call changes and resilience discussion.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect the full report's methodology and forecast dates. Avoid using 0% aggregate growth as evidence of zero trade or contraction in every region.
