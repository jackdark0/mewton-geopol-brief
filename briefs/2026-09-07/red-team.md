## Finding: The Hormuz lead risks pulling a pre-window military event into the headline

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** The first story's framing of "direct U.S.-Iran military escalation" and the editorial rationale for placing it first.
- **Problem:** The U.S. tanker strikes occurred before the research window. The in-window change is Iran's prospective restricted-zone announcement and the ensuing measurement dispute, not a newly observed U.S. strike. A compressed final brief could easily erase that boundary.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** `verified.md` supports the strike as pre-window context and says no exact strike time was available. It supports the September 6 Iranian announcement but found no implementing order, coordinates, insurer guidance, or documented interdiction.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Iran may be repackaging existing restrictions as a new administrative regime rather than creating new control over passage.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Lead with the prospective Iranian regime and label the tanker strikes explicitly as pre-window context. Do not say the zone is operational.

## Finding: The Hormuz commercial-leverage mechanism outruns observed behavior

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** "Iran may be able to extract bargaining leverage from administrative and insurance uncertainty" and the suggestion that incompatible public flow figures could encourage operational overconfidence.
- **Problem:** The mechanism is plausible, but the record contains no changed insurer guidance, owner behavior, flag-state instruction, blacklisting, or government decision based on the disputed numbers. The analysis occasionally moves from an available mechanism to an implied effect.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The Iranian announcement itself mentions insurance and future passage. That supports intended pressure, not realized leverage. The U.S. throughput methodology is undisclosed and the comparison series are not like-for-like; this proves a public measurement gap, not what either government privately believes.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The announcement may be signaling for domestic or negotiating purposes while convoy behavior and commercial risk remain unchanged.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. State that the measure is designed to create commercial uncertainty and that its effect is untested. Present the denominator conflict as limiting public evaluation, not as evidence of decision-maker overconfidence.

## Finding: The Lebanon headline can imply that unverified means disproven

- **Problem:** The initiating drone claim and command-center claim are not independently established, but that does not make them false. The analysis is careful in the body; the headline and mechanism can still read as a judgment against the IDF account.

- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Say the observable retaliation is clearer than the public evidence for its trigger and sensitive target. Attribute both disputed propositions exactly and avoid treating absence of public proof as disproof.

## Finding: "Control" of Hormuz needs a defined question, not a binary winner

- **Severity:** minor
- **Targets:** Show topic asking who controls a chokepoint.
- **Problem:** Military transit, throughput, reliability, insurance cost, and rule-setting are different dependent variables. Asking for one winner can recreate the denominator problem the analysis correctly identifies.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The U.S., Iran, and specialist observers use incompatible measures, and the proposed Iranian rules were not yet implemented.
