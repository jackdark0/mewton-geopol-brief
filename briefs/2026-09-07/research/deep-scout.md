## Candidate: Iran moves from disrupting Hormuz traffic toward a declared counter-blockade regime

- **Underlying development:** On September 6, Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei announced that Tehran would define a new restricted maritime zone extending from the line of the U.S. naval blockade toward Gulf loading ports. He said uncoordinated vessels could be put on an Iranian sanctions list, with insurance and future-passage consequences. The announcement followed a U.S. Central Command account of September 5 strikes that disabled or destroyed three tankers it described as part of an IRGC oil network after alleged Iranian missile attacks on two U.S. warships.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The immediate coverage centers on whether the Strait is physically open. The less visible change is Iran's attempt to turn episodic interdiction into an administrative counter-regime: designated geography, pre-clearance, vessel blacklisting, insurance pressure, and future access penalties.
- **Strategic mechanism:** If implemented, the policy could create two competing systems of maritime authorization around Hormuz. Even without total physical closure, uncertainty over which authority can safely guarantee passage can raise insurance costs, deter crews and owners, complicate U.S.-organized convoys, and widen the set of commercial actors exposed to retaliation. The U.S. tanker strikes also suggest a new coercive rule aimed directly at Iran's revenue and logistics fleet, increasing Tehran's incentive to target commercial shipping in reply.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Rezaei made the announcement through Iranian state media; AP independently reported the comments and noted that the zone's location and operating rules were not specified. CENTCOM published tanker names, locations, claimed dispositions, and video. Iranian state television separately acknowledged that a tanker near Kharg Island was struck, but did not corroborate all three U.S. claims or the asserted IRGC ownership. The U.S. says it has redirected dozens of commercial vessels; Iran says traffic is nearly stopped. Those control claims conflict.
- **What remains inference:** No boundary, legal instrument, enforcement procedure, insurer response, or independently verified interdiction under the announced scheme was observable by the cutoff. It may be a new coercive architecture, or only a rhetorical relabeling of Iran's existing restrictions. The precise time of the September 5 CENTCOM action should be checked before treating it as a within-window event rather than context for the September 6 Iranian response.
- **Evidence classification:** **SUPPORTED** for the Iranian policy announcement (high confidence); **PARTLY SUPPORTED** for the three-tanker strike narrative (moderate confidence); **HOLD** on whether the zone produces additional operational control (low confidence).
- **Initial priority:** **high** — triage score **39/39** (consequence 5, escalation 5, U.S. relevance 4, novelty 3, audience value 3).

## Candidate: Conflicting Hormuz throughput measures obscure how much energy leverage Iran still has

- **Underlying development:** On September 6, U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said flows through Hormuz averaged more than nine million barrels per day and, with bypass pipelines, reached at least two-thirds of pre-conflict levels. AP reported that the figure appeared higher than estimates from TankerTrackers.com and other analysts. Iran's security chief, by contrast, said the Strait was effectively closed and traffic had fallen from more than 100 vessels to only a handful of essential ships.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines often select one side's number and convert it into a verdict about who controls the Strait. The measurement dispute itself is strategically important because AIS suppression, delayed cargo accounting, ship-to-ship transfers, convoys, and bypass-pipeline volumes can produce incompatible but superficially precise totals.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Markets, allies, and adversaries calibrate sanctions tolerance, naval commitments, and escalation choices against perceived flow restoration. If Washington overstates throughput, it can underestimate Iran's economic leverage and the cost of sustaining protection. If public trackers undercount dark or batched traffic, they can overstate disruption and encourage escalation or panic buying.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Wright made the nine-million-barrel claim in a broadcast interview and acknowledged that flows were not fully restored and passage still depended on U.S. naval work. AP identified a discrepancy with outside tracking. A transparent public tracker describes a method that combines AIS observations, satellite imagery, destination adjustments, and a fixed bypass estimate, while warning about limitations. The IEA's February 2026 factsheet gives a pre-conflict baseline of roughly 20 million barrels per day of crude and products through Hormuz in 2025 and describes limited Saudi and Emirati bypass options.
- **What remains inference:** No common denominator or independently audited daily series was available by the cutoff. It is unclear whether competing claims measure cargo that crossed the Strait, cargo discharged, convoy capacity, stock releases, crude only, all liquids, or Strait flows plus bypasses. Iran's near-closure claim and the U.S. restoration claim are both interested-party statements.
- **Evidence classification:** **SUPPORTED** that U.S. and Iranian officials made sharply conflicting claims (high confidence); **SUPPORTED** that public methods and time windows differ (moderate-high confidence); **HOLD** on the true current daily flow (low confidence).
- **Initial priority:** **high** — triage score **31/39** (consequence 4, escalation 3, U.S. relevance 4, novelty 3, audience value 2).

- **Underlying development:** Iran announced that, from September 8, the third-tier gasoline price for consumption above the subsidized monthly quotas would rise from 5,000 to 10,000 toman per liter. The first 60 liters at 1,500 toman and the next 50 liters at 3,000 toman remain unchanged. Government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani said the combined 110-liter quota covers most consumers.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The common shorthand that Iran doubled gasoline prices obscures the incidence. The government protected the two subsidized tiers while placing the increase on high-volume consumption, a design that may be intended to conserve fuel and reduce unrest while the blockade and currency pressure constrain supply.
- **Strategic mechanism:** The measure can cut excess demand and imports while shifting wartime costs toward heavier users. Politically, it tests whether a segmented price rise can avoid the unrest associated with broad fuel-price shocks. Economically, it is a signal that the state sees the fuel imbalance as serious enough to act despite regime-sensitivity around gasoline.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Multiple reports carry the same tier values, effective date, and government explanation. AFP reported that the lower quotas remain unchanged. Iran International reported the spokesperson's claim that the protected allocation covers about 85 percent of the population and cited earlier official statements about a daily fuel deficit. The rial was also reported weaker on the black market during the conflict, but the exact rate series is not independently audited here.
- **What remains inference:** The reports trace largely to one government briefing, so they are multiple outlets but not fully independent evidence. There is no observed consumption response, enforcement record, revenue use, or protest reaction before implementation. The government's 85-percent coverage assertion lacks a published method. The measure may be a limited conservation adjustment rather than a broad fiscal or legitimacy crisis.
- **Evidence classification:** **SUPPORTED** for the announced tier change (moderate-high confidence); **PARTLY SUPPORTED** for the government's incidence claim (moderate-low confidence); **HOLD** on social and regime-stability effects (low confidence).

- **What evidence is observable now:** The official state election portal showed all precincts reporting before the cutoff. That same reporting judged the proposed plan unlikely to be implemented under current conditions.

- **Evidence classification:** **SUPPORTED** for the joint diplomatic position (high confidence); **PARTLY SUPPORTED** that it further constrains practical implementation (moderate confidence); **HOLD** on any change in Israeli or U.S. policy (low confidence).
