## Candidate: Conditional restraint creates a public Iran decision rule

- **Underlying development:** Trump tied renewed U.S. attacks to whether diplomacy progresses toward a strong agreement while preserving explicit infrastructure threats. The Qatari prime minister was scheduled to meet Trump and Vance on November 9.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Headlines can flatten this into either "peace talks" or "new threats." The strategic change is the public condition connecting the two.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A conditional pause can give mediators room to sequence reciprocal steps while retaining coercive leverage. It can also fail if Washington and Tehran define "progress" differently.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Trump's statement, Rubio's no-breakthrough assessment, the White House schedule, and Qatar's continuing intermediary role.
- **What remains inference:** That the condition binds future U.S. action; that a common text exists; that Iran accepts nuclear talks before sanctions or blockade relief.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:** [Reuters](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-he-is-highly-unlikely-to-order-iran-strikes-if-talks-progress-ce785fd2d08ef423) supports the public condition and threat; [Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-to-meet-qatars-prime-minister-as-iran-diplomacy-faces-hurdles-ce785fd3d989f021) supports the schedule and negotiating gap; [Qatar MOFA](https://mofa.gov.qa/en/qatar/latest-articles/latest-news/details/2026/11/03/advisor-to-prime-minister--official-spokesperson-for-ministry-of-foreign-affairs--qatar-continues-its-efforts-to-bridge-gap-between-washington--tehran) establishes Qatar's stated mediation role, not agreement.
