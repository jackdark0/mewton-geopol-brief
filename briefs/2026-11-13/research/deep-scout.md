## Candidate: UK sanctions move from producer coverage to a broader energy-revenue chain

- **Underlying development:** The UK added Slavneft, LLC Vostok Oil, petrochemical and coal firms, UAE-linked traders, and vessels carrying Russian oil or LNG. The legal notice lists named entities and statements of reasons; the political release says Slavneft completes UK sanctions coverage of Russia's ten major oil producers.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Ukraine-diplomacy headlines overshadowed a concrete coercive measure announced at the same summit.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Asset freezes and transaction prohibitions can deny UK-linked finance, insurance, brokering, and professional services. Trader and vessel designations may raise evasion costs across the chain, but only if ownership is traced, listings are updated, and partner jurisdictions enforce or mirror them.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The November 12 designation notice names Slavneft, Vostok Oil, NIPIGAZ, SIBUR, coal companies, traders, and vessels; government says the package includes shadow-fleet transport and all ten major producers.
- **What remains inference:** “Top ten” depends on an unstated ranking. Revenue loss, production effects, and third-country compliance are not measured.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6af5ad18cc0dbfec767a509f/Sanctions_Notice__Russia__12_November_2026.pdf - legal designations and reasons.
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ukraine-set-to-be-focus-of-epc-summit-as-uk-sanctions-russias-top-ten-oil-producers - claimed strategic scope.
- **Questions for auditor:** Count all new entity and vessel designations; identify exposed UK services and assets; find an independent production/revenue ranking; check whether U.S./EU sanctions already cover the same nodes.

## Candidate: Starshield is a privately operated battlefield control point with unclear public authority

- **Underlying development:** Ukraine reports that Starshield again stops supporting fast-moving military systems above 161 km/h in occupied territory and along the front; SpaceX says there was no change. The service is tied to Pentagon contracts, while public accounts conflict over SpaceX, Pentagon, and presidential control.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Coverage frames the episode as a Musk-Zelensky dispute, but the strategic issue is service-control architecture for an allied military capability.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A geofence or speed parameter can disable drone-interceptor communications faster than a formal aid suspension. Ambiguous control makes deterrence and planning unreliable because Ukraine cannot tell which technical limits are contractual, political, safety-related, or temporary.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Zelenskyy publicly specified a 161 km/h threshold and an operational effect; the Ukrainian presidency reported frontline disruptions; SpaceX issued a direct denial of any new change.
- **What remains inference:** No independent telemetry establishes the current limit. Timing does not prove the change was ordered to pressure Kyiv in negotiations.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/mi-znovu-zafiksuvali-obmezhennya-shvidkosti-starshield-na-na-107285 - exact Ukrainian claim.
  - https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/situaciya-z-pereboyami-v-roboti-starshield-i-neobhidnist-pos-107301 - asserted air-defense effect.
  - https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/ukraine-says-spacex-starshield-is-restricted-again-spacex-claims-it-isnt/ - denial and control ambiguity.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek Pentagon contract terms, telemetry, prior configuration baseline, and a precise answer to whether “no change” means no newly imposed change or no restriction at all.

## Candidate: China's exercise suggests cross-theater western Pacific integration, not only Taiwan pressure

- **Underlying development:** Taiwan says more than 100 PLA Navy and coast guard vessels operated across the western Pacific and around Taiwan, including forces from three theater commands and the Liaoning carrier group. China did not name the exercise and called carrier operations routine.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The lack of a formal exercise name reduced headline attention even though Taiwanese officials describe a larger deployment than the 2025 named Taiwan drill.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Coordinating northern, eastern, and southern fleets can rehearse blockade, anti-intervention, logistics, and command-and-control across the first island chain. Omitting a formal announcement can normalize mobilization and compress warning time.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Joseph Wu publicly identified continuing formations east of the Philippines; Reuters reported two Taiwan officials and one foreign intelligence source; the reported vessel count had fallen to roughly 70 by Friday.
- **What remains inference:** Simulated target sets, the ICBM launch link, and coercive intent rest largely on Taiwanese assessments. No neutral order of battle or Chinese exercise plan was inspected.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/china-stages-unannounced-pacific-drills-in-silent-intimidation-of-taiwan-officials-say-5024072 - deployment, provenance, and competing account.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain commercial imagery or ship-tracking corroboration; separate coast guard from naval hulls; test whether weather/training cycles explain timing better than APEC coercion.

## Candidate: U.S. custody of Venezuelan resource revenue creates leverage without full transparency

- **Underlying development:** A State Department-released KPMG record says $12.26 billion in oil and other resource sales entered a U.S.-managed mechanism through September, nearly all disbursed, with $168.6 million remaining. U.S. officials vetted transactions; buyer identities are redacted.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The headline “money returned” obscures the institutional novelty: a foreign power controls approval and custody of another state's principal revenue stream.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Transaction approval gives Washington leverage over spending, sanctions compliance, the interim authority, and election timing. It may reduce diversion risk while also concentrating discretionary control and political responsibility in U.S. agencies.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Aggregate receipts, residual balance, selected direct payments, foreign-exchange disbursement mechanism, buyer count, and redactions.
- **What remains inference:** Independent beneficial-recipient verification, whether all proceeds are Venezuelan sovereign property in practice, and the political conditions attached to releases.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/venezuela-oil-sales-maduro-trump-6982bfdc5d64482e3500eea39891f0d1 - report figures and redactions.
  - https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/us-handled-12-billion-of-venezuelas-oil-revenues-report-says-5023141 - Reuters on custody and congressional transmission.
  - https://www.finance.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/media/doc/09172026lettertotreasuryandstaterevenezuelanresourcestransactions.pdf - prior oversight questions about discretion and transparency.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect the actual report; reconcile gross receipts, total disbursements, direct payments, fees, and closing balance; distinguish audit assurance from management reporting.

## Candidate: Yemen sanctions renewal shifts monitoring toward maritime enforcement gaps

- **Underlying development:** Resolution 2837 renewed the 2140 travel-ban and asset-freeze regime, extended the Panel of Experts, requested six members, and added dedicated maritime-security expertise while retaining the open-ended arms embargo.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** A renewal sounds routine, but the panel redesign follows renewed Houthi territorial fighting and attacks affecting two strategic waterways.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Better maritime expertise can improve vessel, flag, component, and financing investigations, which may produce designations or interdiction evidence. Monitoring cannot itself stop shipments or create authority absent member-state action.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Consensus adoption, renewed dates, maritime specialization, condemnation of escalation, and continuing arms embargo.
- **What remains inference:** Whether the panel will be fully staffed, whether evidence will lead to designations, and whether states will act on findings.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/whatsinblue/2026/11/yemen-vote-on-a-draft-resolution-renewing-the-sanctions-regime-2.php - draft changes and identified enforcement gaps.
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/this-resolution-demonstrates-this-councils-determination-to-take-decisive-action-against-reckless-attacks-by-the-houthis-in-yemen-uk-explanation-of-vo - adoption and penholder explanation.
- **Questions for auditor:** Read final S/RES/2837; identify changes from S/RES/2801; confirm whether dual-use components received operative restrictions or only reporting.

## Candidate: MINUSCA drawdown begins before the state fully replaces its security function

- **Underlying development:** Resolution 2836 lowers MINUSCA's military ceiling from 14,046 to 12,000, concentrates the mission in high-risk border areas, and sets a framework for gradual task transfer to CAR authorities and the UN country team.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Peacekeeping mandate renewals are treated as procedural despite changing the balance among UN forces, national forces, and Russian-linked security providers.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Fewer authorized troops may force geographic prioritization, creating gaps that national units or outside security partners fill. A planned handover can strengthen sovereignty if capacity exists, or entrench dependence on less accountable providers if it does not.
- **What evidence is observable now:** A 2,046-person ceiling reduction (14.6%), border-area prioritization, and a one-year mandate.
- **What remains inference:** Actual departure numbers, timing, security substitution, civilian-protection effect, and the role of Africa Corps or Wagner-linked personnel.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Sources:**
  - https://press.un.org/en/2026/sc16507.doc.htm - adopted resolution summary.
  - https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/whatsinblue/2026/11/central-african-republic-vote-on-minuscas-mandate-renewal-4.php - force-ceiling and transition context.
- **Questions for auditor:** Compare authorized and deployed strength; map proposed reductions; inspect the Secretary-General transition assessment and CAR capacity indicators.

## Candidate: UK-Ukrainian naval-drone production turns battlefield learning into allied capacity

- **Underlying development:** UFORCE is expanding UK factories and says UK-built drone boats will use more than 70% British content, with production for Ukraine, Britain, and allies.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Industrial announcements are easy to dismiss as jobs policy, but they can move a wartime design into a durable allied supply chain.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Local production spreads know-how, creates surge capacity, and reduces single-country manufacturing risk. It can also expose design and export-control dependencies.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Announced factories, job and apprenticeship plans, customer set, and local-content threshold.
- **What remains inference:** Order book, production rate, system performance, government equity or subsidy, and delivery timeline.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ukraines-drone-pioneers-bring-battle-tested-tech-and-high-skilled-jobs-to-britain - government announcement.
- **Questions for auditor:** Find investment terms, contracts, unit output, components still sourced abroad, and export licensing.

## Candidate: Europe's Ukraine role is constrained by an institutional representation problem

- **Underlying development:** U.S. and Ukrainian officials say Europe will join the next talks, while European leaders have not selected who speaks for the EU, NATO Europeans, the E3, the Coalition of the Willing, or the wider EPC. Costa published common principles, not a mandate for one negotiator.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** “Europe included” reads like closure, but participation without authority or a defined constituency can produce parallel messages.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Russia and the United States can exploit fragmented representation, while any security guarantee needs buy-in from governments supplying forces, money, sanctions, and EU legal instruments.
- **What evidence is observable now:** U.S. envoy and Zelenskyy statements, EU involvement claim, Costa's principles, and no named representative.
- **What remains inference:** Putin's durable assent, the next format, and whether one person could bind multiple institutions.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/dublin-epc-europe-ukraine-russia-war-2a96d57210055edd2e76f71693e0a37c - institutional alternatives and public positions.
  - https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/meetings/international-summit/2026/11/12/ - common European principles.
- **Questions for auditor:** Identify any agreed selection mechanism or mandate after the EPC; distinguish a spokesperson from an empowered negotiator.
