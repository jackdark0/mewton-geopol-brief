## Finding: The mediator draft can become a phantom agreement

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** "U.S.-Iran mediators have a sequence, not convergence," especially the maritime-first mechanism.
- **Problem:** The analysis correctly says no common deal exists, but repeated discussion of "the draft" can still make an anonymous, possibly overlapping source account feel like an agreed negotiating document.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** AP attributes the sequence to regional officials; no Qatari, Omani, Iranian or U.S. text was inspected. Axios confirms U.S.-official discussion but does not independently authenticate the same draft. Trump denied the reported relief offer.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Mediators may be circulating incompatible trial balloons rather than refining one shared framework.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Use "a draft described by AP's regional sources" or "a reported sequence," never "the deal" or "the proposal" without attribution. Lead with absence of convergence.

## Finding: Do not infer presidential loss of control from conflicting briefings

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** "principal-agent question" and claim that conflicting signals may slow mediation.
- **Problem:** Public contradiction can reflect unauthorized briefing, deliberate bargaining ambiguity, a changed position or a denial of a stronger formulation. The evidence does not identify which.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Anonymous officials described willingness to exchange relief for nuclear steps; Trump called reports of an offer untrue. No internal decision record establishes staff defiance or presidential repudiation of an authorized message.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Officials and Trump may be distinguishing willingness to consider relief from a transmitted offer, not contradicting policy substance.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Ask whether Washington has authorized a transmissible offer; avoid language implying an internal command breakdown.
