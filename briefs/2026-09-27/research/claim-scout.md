## Claim C-03: Iran had “no deviation” from IAEA safeguards and the attacked nuclear centers were completely under surveillance

- **Origin / principal promoters:** President Masoud Pezeshkian in a [CBS interview recorded September 25 and transcript published September 25 at 21:10 EDT](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/transcript-iranian-president-masoud-pezeshkian-face-the-nation-transcript-09-27-2026/). His exact translated phrases are: “We had no deviation from that framework” and the centers/sites were “completely under their unprecedented surveillance and inspections regime.” The interpreter's English is the inspected text; Persian audio was not independently checked.
- **Why it matters:** These assertions bear directly on whether nuclear inspection demands can be dismissed as already satisfied in renewed U.S.–Iran talks. They must not be confused with whether Iran has built a nuclear weapon, which the inspected IAEA report does **not** establish.
- **Atomic propositions / type:** (a) At the relevant attack time, the sites he meant were fully under IAEA surveillance and inspection; (b) Iran had not deviated from the applicable safeguards framework (empirical/legal compliance). The interview appears to refer backward across the 2025 and 2026 attacks, so the exact attack date and “framework” require clarification.
- **Evidence for / against matrix:**

  | For | Against or missing | Scope/provenance judgment |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pezeshkian asserts both propositions and points to attacks on nuclear sites while talks and some safeguards processes existed. | [IAEA GOV/2026/50](https://www.iranwatch.org/sites/default/files/iaea-gov-2026-50.pdf), especially paras. 16, 22, 27–28, 38–43, records a Board finding of non-compliance; no Agency access to declared facilities during its reporting period; no current verification of the 60%-enriched uranium stockpile; and loss of continuity of knowledge after June 2025. The Agency also notes attacks and safety-related pauses, which complicate the chronology. | Lack of verification does **not** prove weaponization or diversion. The IAEA's non-compliance finding does directly challenge a blanket “no deviation” claim. “Completely” and “unprecedented” exceed what the record can sustain, especially with Additional Protocol access absent since 2021. |

- **Independent corroboration:** Pezeshkian's words are directly documented by CBS. The IAEA report is the primary institutional record for its verification status and Board determinations; the [September 9 U.K./French/German/U.S. Board statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/implementation-of-the-npt-safeguards-agreement-in-iran-quad-statement-to-the-iaea-board-of-governors-september-2026) repeats the report and is **derivative** on its facts.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak** as a blanket claim; a narrower statement that some inspections occurred before some attacks is supportable. No conclusion on whether Iran has an active weapons program follows from this verdict.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the blanket compliance/surveillance wording conflicts with the IAEA's documented limits and non-compliance finding; **medium** on the exact historical date implied by the speaker.
- **Questions for auditor:** Check original-language audio/translation and the surrounding attack chronology. Identify which facilities and date he meant, and assess each atomic proposition against the IAEA's dated access record rather than a single current-status summary.

## Claim C-04: U.S. “control” means the Strait of Hormuz is open for normal commercial passage

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Trump and the White House have repeatedly claimed U.S. control; the [White House's August 28 release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/08/president-trump-was-right-america-controls-the-strait-of-hormuz/) is older background recording the exact official position. [Reuters' September 26 report](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/09/26/trump-rejects-iranian-proposal-to-open-hormuz-and-end-fighting) says Trump again asserted control during the proposal rejection. Do not misquote his older exact wording as words uttered September 26.
- **Why it matters:** “Control,” “some escorted oil movements,” and “normal safe passage” imply different levels of military success and different risks to global energy flows.
- **Atomic proposition / type:** U.S. forces can maintain normal, broadly accessible commercial passage through the strait as of the cutoff (operational status), a testable strong interpretation of “control.” Legal sovereignty is a separate question not adjudicated here.
- **Evidence for / against matrix:**

  | For | Against or missing | Scope/denominator judgment |
  |---|---|---|

- **Questions for auditor:** Ask the White House/CENTCOM to define the operational metric; obtain like-for-like daily/all-type transit data and attack/insurance indicators before asserting either full reopening or total closure.

## Claim C-05: U.S. measures prevent **any country** from exporting fuel to Cuba

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez's September 26 UN remarks, in a [Reuters video transcript published September 26 at 17:55 EDT](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-predicts-us-cuba-deal-says-military-action-unlikely-ce785adcd88bf227). Reuters transcribes his English as saying the U.S. energy blockade “prevents any country from exporting fuel to Cuba.” This is an attributed translation from the speech, not independently checked against original Spanish audio.
- **Why it matters:** A literal global prohibition has different legal and strategic meaning from tariffs, sanctions risk, and an effective commercial deterrent. Cuban energy scarcity is real enough to matter, but the mechanism and absolute quantifier need precision.
- **Atomic proposition / type:** As of September 26, U.S. action makes it impossible for every foreign country to export any fuel to Cuba (universal empirical and causal claim).
- **Evidence for / against matrix:**

  | For | Against or missing | Scope/provenance judgment |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rodríguez describes coercive pressure; [six UN special rapporteurs' April 23 communication](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30947) records serious concerns about the January U.S. order's impact on fuel and human rights. Reuters says Cuba faces fuel shortages and blackouts, also affected by failing infrastructure. | The [January 29 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/addressing-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-cuba/) authorizes **possible additional tariffs on U.S. imports from countries providing oil to Cuba** after specified findings and decisions; it does not itself prohibit all other countries from shipping fuel. The UN rapporteurs' letter says it does not prejudge the accuracy of all alleged effects. | An economic measure can chill trade severely without making every shipment impossible. Other U.S. measures may also apply, and this search did not establish a complete September vessel/shipment census. The causal contribution of U.S. pressure versus Cuban infrastructure and policy is not quantified. |

- **Independent corroboration:** Yes for the existence/design of the U.S. order (official text) and UN experts' concerns; **no** for the literal “any country” quantifier.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak / materially overstated** as a literal universal. The supported core is forceful U.S. pressure on third-country fuel supply with serious humanitarian concern.
- **Confidence:** **Medium**; the order's mechanism is clear, but current enforcement and the complete September shipment record remain unverified.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect original Spanish speech/audio and any later U.S. orders or maritime interdiction notices; identify actual September fuel deliveries, licenses and exclusions before giving a categorical current-flow judgment.

## Claim C-06: Cuba's energy restrictions are legally equivalent to a naval blockade and therefore an “act of war”

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Rodríguez in the same [September 26 Reuters video transcript](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/trump-predicts-us-cuba-deal-says-military-action-unlikely-ce785adcd88bf227). This is his legal characterization, not a court finding.
- **Why it matters:** An “act of war” label can imply a military response threshold and obscure the difference between economic coercion, a physical blockade, illegality, and an armed attack.
- **Atomic propositions / type:** (a) The U.S. tariff/sanctions regime is legally equivalent to a physical naval blockade; (b) the applicable international law categorically classifies this conduct as an “act of war.” These are distinct legal propositions.
- **Evidence for / against matrix:**

  | For | Against or missing | Legal-stage judgment |
  |---|---|---|
  | UN rapporteurs' [April communication](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30947) calls the January order a serious violation of international law, including non-intervention and self-determination. That is an expert allegation with a cited human-rights framework. | The [executive order's operative text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/addressing-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-cuba/) provides for contingent tariffs, not a declared physical cordon around Cuban ports. The UN Charter distinguishes [threat/use of force](https://legal.un.org/repertory/art2.shtml), [economic interruption without armed force](https://legal.un.org/repertory/art41.shtml), and [self-defence after an armed attack](https://legal.un.org/repertory/art51.shtml). No inspected tribunal decision applies the “act of war” label to this specific current measure. | A possible international-law violation does not automatically establish armed force or a naval blockade. Conversely, the order alone may not describe the full coercive campaign. |

- **Independent corroboration:** No independent adjudication of Rodríguez's exact legal equivalence was found. UN rapporteurs substantiate legal concern, not his particular armed-force classification.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear as an overall legal characterization; weak as a categorical, already-adjudicated statement.** Do not report “act of war” in the publication voice.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** in the distinction between the measures and legal stages; **low** on any final legal verdict without a full inventory of physical enforcement and a specialized legal review.
- **Questions for auditor:** Establish whether any contemporaneous physical interdiction of Cuba-bound ships exists, identify the precise claimed legal test and any U.S. reply to the rapporteurs, and consult primary case law before any affirmative legal conclusion.
