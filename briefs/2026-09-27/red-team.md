# Red-team review - 2026-09-27

## Finding: Do not equate Trump's public refusal with a verified written-response chain

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Iran story, especially "public rejection of Iran's seven-day Hormuz proposal" and any claim that the diplomatic sequence is formally settled.
- **Problem:** Trump said he rejected "their proposal," but the exact written text he saw, its version, and its transmission through Qatar remain unavailable. The analysis correctly distinguishes public and mediated answers but occasionally writes as though the same document is definitely at issue.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Reuters and Al Jazeera establish the public statement. Araghchi said no definitive answer had reached Iran through mediators. No Qatari note or offer text was inspected.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Trump publicly rejected the package as he understood or described it, while formal bargaining over a revised text or channel-specific answer remained open.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Say Trump publicly rejected Iran's announced package; do not say the exact written offer was formally rejected through the agreed channel.

## Finding: The post-midterm bombing report must remain separate from the rejection

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Iran story and contested-claims section.
- **Problem:** Proximity in one paragraph can make a confirmed public refusal appear to corroborate an anonymous report about future bombing.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** All inspected repetitions of the private remark trace to the Wall Street Journal. Reuters could not verify it. No order or force-posture decision was found.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The reported remark may describe Trump's expectation, bargaining rhetoric, or a source's interpretation rather than an operational decision.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify and atomize. Use separate sentences and an explicit "not independently corroborated" label.

## Finding: The U.S.-China economic wording is not harmonized

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** U.S.-China story.
- **Problem:** The White House says recommendations covering $30 billion of goods in each direction; China says a $30 billion reciprocal tariff-reduction arrangement. Those formulations differ on denominator and legal stage.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Neither side published tariff lines, rates, revenue effects, or an effective date.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The governments are describing a still-unfinished negotiating outcome for trade value, not an executed $30 billion tariff cut.
- **Required editorial action:** correct. Lead with the White House's "recommendations" and describe China's stronger term separately. Never add the directions or label $30 billion as revenue.

## Finding: The military crisis memorandum is Chinese-attributed

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** U.S.-China story.
- **Problem:** The analysis describes three possible stabilizers and at points treats the military memorandum as a joint output. The inspected U.S. fact sheet omits it.

## Finding: A 55% rate is coherent arithmetic, not independent validation

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Ukraine jet-drone story.
- **Problem:** More than 1,500 divided by more than 2,730 is approximately 55%, but both numerator and denominator come from one speech and mix "intercepted" with "suppressed." It cannot be compared directly with ordinary-Shahed kill rates.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** No engagement log, observation period beyond month-to-date, electronic-warfare definition, or independent count was published.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Kyiv is using a broad operational-effect measure that may be valid for command decisions but is not a transparent shoot-down statistic.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Call it Ukraine's claimed intercept-or-suppression rate and never translate it to 45% "getting through" or a kill probability.
