## Candidate: Houthi forces close on Aden after government coastal withdrawal

- **Event time/date:** 2026-11-24.
- **Region / actors:** Yemen; Houthi forces; internationally recognized government forces.
- **What changed:** Government military officials told AP and Reuters that Houthi forces had advanced to about 60 km from Aden after government-aligned forces withdrew from positions around Bab el-Mandeb. Government forces said they had formed defensive lines north and west of Aden. JMIC separately assessed that the Houthis hold the vacated Red Sea coastal positions and that pressure toward Aden continues.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - It puts the internationally recognized government's interim capital under more direct pressure.
  - It consolidates Houthi positions around a strategic shipping chokepoint even without a new commercial-shipping attack.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** medium-high on the withdrawal and pressure; medium on the reported distance and exact line of control.
- **Source chain:** AP and Reuters cite Yemeni government officials; JMIC is a separate maritime-security assessment drawing on incident and tracking data.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-iran-gaza-israel-houthis-libya-november-24-2026-ba01cb036a849ba69873da5000ee08d5) supports the government account of the advance and defensive line; [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Houthis%2Bwont%2Bseize%2BAden%2C%2BYemeni%2Bgovernment%2Bforces%2Bsay/27393395.html) independently reports the same briefing and redeployment; [JMIC Update 116](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/jmic_advisory_update_116_22-nov.pdf?rev=7d2a81d10d1d467cbe75f405f2392f59) supports continued pressure and Houthi possession of vacated coastal positions.
- **Questions for auditor:** What independently observable evidence fixes the front line? Does control of coastal positions translate into new anti-shipping capability or only territorial leverage?

- **Source chain:** Iran's statement is one interested source chain. JMIC and UKMTO are separate maritime reporting channels, but their incident lists do not identify the same three Iranian-claimed vessels.

## Candidate: Congo orders another cobalt-export halt

- **Event time/date:** decision announced 2026-11-23, effective 2026-11-30.
- **Region / actors:** Democratic Republic of Congo; cobalt producers and buyers; battery and defense supply chains.
- **What changed:** ARECOMS said all Congolese cobalt exports will be suspended from November 30 until explicitly restarted. Previously authorized but unexported volumes lose automatic protection. The authority will review the measure after four months, but review does not guarantee reopening.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - The world's largest producer is using export control to manage global price and inventory conditions.
  - The measure can tighten near-term supply while increasing uncertainty around quota allocation and inventories.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high.
- **Source chain:** Reuters and S&P Global inspected the ARECOMS communication; the Congolese press agency separately reported the announcement.
- **Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/congo-halts-cobalt-exports-again-to-tackle-oversupply-support-prices-industry-regulator-says-ce7851dada89f121) reports the regulator's decision and review terms; [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/112326-drc-temporarily-suspends-cobalt-exports) details quota treatment and market reaction; [ACP](https://acp.cd/economie/la-rdc-suspend-la-commercialisation-du-cobalt-des-le-30-novembre-2026/) confirms the official announcement.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does the order cover refined material already outside Congo? What inventory buffers and alternative supply can absorb a four-month-or-longer halt?
