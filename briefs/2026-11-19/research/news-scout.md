## Candidate: U.S., China and Russia hold their first three-leader meeting

- **Event time/date:** November 19 in Shenzhen; the first outcome reporting was public before the fixed cutoff.
- **Region / actors:** United States, China, Russia; APEC.
- **What changed:** Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met together for the first time. Chinese and Russian officials described a declaration on U.N. reform, energy supply, nuclear-risk dialogue and official immunity at the International Criminal Court; AP reported that the United States had not released the document by cutoff. The public declaration did not mention Ukraine or Iran even though Trump said those wars were discussed.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - It creates a high-level channel among the three largest nuclear powers after New START expired.
  - It gives Moscow diplomatic status while Ukraine and U.S. allies were absent.
  - The difference between a joint process and a nonbinding communique is strategically decisive.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high; moderate on the precise common commitments because the U.S. text was unavailable.
- **Source chain:** AP inspected and attributed the outcome to Chinese and Russian officials; the missing U.S. release limits three-sided confirmation.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-xi-putin-nuclear-ukraine-iran-a04dc76ed8e786b087e2c1ea9598783a) establishes the meeting, reported declaration and source limitation. [White House arrival notice](https://www.whitehouse.gov/videos/president-donald-j-trump-arrives-in-shenzhen-china-where-hell-participate-in-the-apec-summit-%F0%9F%87%BA%F0%9F%87%B8/) confirms Trump's APEC arrival, but not the trilateral outcome.
- **Questions for auditor:** Was a common text released before 02:00 Eastern? Did any side commit to a negotiating mechanism, timetable or officials below leader level? What exactly did Trump say about Ukraine, Iran and the ICC?

## Candidate: Iran expands its retaliatory attacks to Kuwait and again targets Bahrain

- **Event time/date:** Early November 18.
- **Region / actors:** Iran, Kuwait, Bahrain, United States and Gulf mediators.
- **What changed:** Iran said it attacked U.S. air-defense, drone and missile infrastructure at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base; Kuwait said it intercepted drone attacks. Bahrain said its defenses repelled several Iranian attacks. The attacks followed a U.S. strike on an IRGC launch site on Qeshm Island and extended the latest exchange beyond Bahrain.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - Kuwait became an active target in the renewed exchange.
  - Repeated attacks on U.S. host states raise the chance that force protection and diplomacy pull in opposite directions.
  - Gulf states are trying to prevent a larger U.S.-Israeli campaign while absorbing Iranian retaliation.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high that attacks and interceptions occurred; low on Iranian claims about the targets hit or damage.
- **Source chain:** Iranian target claims came through state media; Kuwait and Bahrain supplied separate official accounts. AP separately reported regional diplomacy from four anonymous regional officials.
- **Sources:** [AP regional escalation report](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-kuwait-gulf-attacks-bahrain-b1f0b530cffe951e6fcc1e27b2e1af94) supports the attacks, retaliation sequence and diplomatic push. [AP regional update](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-news-iran-bahrain-us-november-18-2026-b71b1d25f3cff987b4b125196ecb4c3a) gives the Iranian, Kuwaiti and Bahraini accounts and notes no immediate Kuwaiti damage report.
- **Questions for auditor:** What objects crossed Kuwaiti and Bahraini airspace? Were there impacts, casualties or damage? Can the target descriptions be independently confirmed? Did CENTCOM issue a pre-cutoff statement?

## Candidate: Saudi-led coalition opens a large Yemen air campaign as Riyadh plans for months of war

- **Event time/date:** November 18; the underlying renewed fighting began earlier in the fall.
- **Region / actors:** Saudi Arabia, internationally recognized Yemeni government, Houthis, United States.
- **What changed:** The Saudi-led coalition said it struck Houthi missile warehouses and drone workshops across rebel-held territory. An unnamed diplomat said Saudi officials had told allies the war could continue another six months and were seeking U.S. offensive support. The Houthis said at least 17 people were killed; UNICEF separately reported 64 children killed and 64 injured since September 3.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - Riyadh appears to be planning a sustained coercive campaign rather than a short punitive wave.
  - U.S. refusal or acceptance of offensive support could change the campaign's scale.
  - Civilian harm and displacement can constrain coalition operations and complicate negotiations.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high on the air campaign; moderate on the Saudi six-month planning account; low on target effects and attribution of all casualties.
- **Source chain:** AP combines a Saudi coalition claim, a Houthi casualty claim, UNICEF reporting and one anonymous diplomat. These are distinct sources for distinct propositions, not independent confirmation of one battle account.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-news-iran-bahrain-us-november-18-2026-b71b1d25f3cff987b4b125196ecb4c3a) supports the coalition announcement, diplomatic account and UNICEF figures.
- **Questions for auditor:** How many targets were actually struck, and with what effects? Does the six-month planning claim reflect an authorized Saudi policy? What is the civilian/combatant breakdown of the reported deaths?

## Candidate: United States and Venezuela sign a military-cooperation agreement

- **Event time/date:** Announced November 18 in Caracas.
- **Region / actors:** United States, Venezuela and Venezuela's interim authorities.
- **What changed:** U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the two governments signed an agreement covering joint training and intelligence sharing. The text, scope, duration, oversight and implementation schedule were not available in the inspected record.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - It moves the relationship from post-intervention political control toward operational security integration.
  - Intelligence sharing and training could reshape Venezuelan institutions and regional security alignments.
  - The deal arrives without a date for Venezuela's next presidential election.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high on Hegseth's announcement; medium on legal status and low on implementation details.
- **Source chain:** AP directly observed Hegseth's Caracas speech and separately describes the political context; no agreement text was inspected.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/hegseth-venezuela-maduro-trump-e02dd6173e575c644a37dd417d84296d) supports Hegseth's announcement, stated cooperation areas and missing election timetable.
- **Questions for auditor:** Is the signed text public? Who signed for Venezuela? Does it authorize deployments, basing, operations or only training and exchanges? What domestic approval is required?

## Candidate: European Commission proposes another EUR 45 billion for Ukraine in 2027

- **Event time/date:** November 18.
- **Region / actors:** European Commission, EU member states, European Parliament, Ukraine and international financing partners.
- **What changed:** The Commission proposed a new limited-recourse loan of up to EUR 45 billion, split between EUR 25 billion in defense assistance and EUR 20 billion in budget support. The package is meant to cover two-thirds of an estimated EUR 70 billion 2027 financing gap and still requires legislative adoption.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - It attempts to make Ukrainian state and military financing predictable through 2027.
  - It places more of the burden on EU borrowing while asking partners to fill the remaining gap.
  - Member-state resistance and the need for co-legislator approval make the headline amount a proposal, not delivered support.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high.
- **Source chain:** direct European Commission proposal plus independent AP reporting on member-state divisions.
- **Sources:** [European Commission](https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-proposes-eur45-billion-additional-financial-support-ukraine-2027-2026-11-18_en) supplies amounts, structure, conditions and next steps. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/europe-ukraine-loan-russia-summit-8c1baf00b55973a3534096aae2cc73a2) reports the proposal and resistance from several member states.
- **Questions for auditor:** What voting rule applies to each instrument? Which states can opt out or block? Are interest, guarantees and Russian-asset windfalls described consistently across the proposal and reporting?

## Candidate: Ethiopia and Eritrea mass forces near their border amid disputed clash reports

- **Event time/date:** Reported November 18; cited incidents dated November 12-13.
- **Region / actors:** Ethiopia, Eritrea, armed groups in Tigray, ACLED and International Crisis Group.
- **What changed:** Reuters reported that the two armies were within striking distance after a border build-up. ACLED said Ethiopian drones targeted likely Eritrean positions on November 12 and the armies clashed on November 13; a source close to a pro-Ethiopian militia disputed that direct clashes occurred, and Reuters could not independently confirm them.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - Direct state-to-state combat could widen Ethiopia's Tigray fighting into another Horn of Africa war.
  - The disputed Badme area carries strong escalation history.
  - The evidence is strong enough to warrant attention but not a categorical claim that war resumed.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** medium on the build-up; low-to-medium on direct clashes.
- **Source chain:** Reuters relies on ACLED, ICG, two militia-linked sources and official nonanswers; these are not all independent observation streams.
- **Sources:** [Reuters republication](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Ethiopia%2Band%2BEritrea%2Bamass%2Bforces%2Balong%2Bborder,%2Bheightening%2Bwar%2Bfears,%2Bsources%2Bsay/27366323.html) preserves the conflicting evidence and nonconfirmation.
- **Questions for auditor:** Is there imagery or a direct official record of the force concentrations? Can ACLED's incident sources be separated from the militia-linked accounts? Did any direct clash occur within this edition's window?

## Candidate: Myanmar's military-backed government says it will review ASEAN ties

- **Event time/date:** November 18.
- **Region / actors:** Myanmar and ASEAN.
- **What changed:** Min Aung Hlaing told Parliament that his government would review its future relationship with ASEAN while continuing bilateral talks with members. The statement followed ASEAN's renewal of top-level restrictions and its finding of insufficient progress on the five-point peace plan.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - A real downgrade would weaken ASEAN's already limited leverage.
  - Bilateral outreach may be intended to divide the bloc without a formal withdrawal.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high on the statement; low on any operational change.
- **Source chain:** AP reports the speech; Myanmar state-aligned coverage repeats the government's account; ASEAN's November 15 review is a separate primary record.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/min-aung-hlaing-asean-peace-plan-turmoil-97e863b842b3a3ad805635b11a7aea8c) supports the speech and context. [ASEAN decision](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/FINAL-ASEAN-Leaders-Review-and-Decision-on-the-Implementation-of-the-Five-Point-Consensus-15-November-2026.pdf) supplies the bloc's position.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does “review” have a legal or policy meaning? Is withdrawal contemplated, or only reduced engagement? Which ASEAN members share Myanmar's procedural objections?

## Candidate: Hormuz oil flows fall while the United States returns to two carriers

- **Event time/date:** Flow estimate through November 16; carrier departure reported November 18.
- **Region / actors:** Strait of Hormuz, U.S. Navy, Iran and energy markets.
- **What changed:** Kpler's preliminary data put crude transiting only the strait at 8.5 million barrels per day, about half the prewar level, while all regional export routes carried 15 million barrels per day, about 64% of the prewar level. The USS George Washington departed after a brief three-carrier overlap, leaving two carriers and a large escort force.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - The flow denominator changes the story: strait-only traffic and all-route exports show different levels of disruption.
  - The carrier departure looks like planned rotation rather than a force collapse.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high on ship movements; medium on preliminary private flow estimates.
- **Source chain:** AP cites Kpler for shipping data and the U.S. Pacific Fleet for the carrier; the two propositions are independent.
- **Sources:** [AP regional update](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-news-iran-bahrain-us-november-18-2026-b71b1d25f3cff987b4b125196ecb4c3a) reports both estimates and their denominators. [AP carrier report](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-aircraft-carrier-uss-george-washington-f72d5aa085500412989183e606bfadfd) confirms the two-carrier posture.
- **Questions for auditor:** Are the prewar bases and route definitions explicit? Did flows recover after November 16? Was the carrier move scheduled before the current attacks?
