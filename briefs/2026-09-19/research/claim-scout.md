## Claim: H.R. 5334 immediately imposes a 500 percent tariff on all Russian imports and a 100 percent tariff on countries buying Russian energy

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The bill's headline tariff ceilings and shorthand accounts of the legislation. Political supporters have emphasized severe secondary economic pressure; some summaries collapse authorization, required review, and actual implementation into one step.
- **Why it matters:** The difference changes the expected timing and scale of economic impact. An immediate fixed tariff would be a shock already in force; a ceiling implemented after determinations and subject to waiver is leverage whose effect depends on executive choices.
- **Evidence offered for it:** H.R. 5334 contains a section authorizing duties “up to 500 percent” on Russian products and a section providing duties “up to 100 percent” for certain countries that continue specified Russian oil or gas purchases or are determined to be leading sanctions-evasion facilitators. The president signed the bill September 18.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The enrolled text does not set the maximum percentage as the automatic universal rate on signature. It requires or authorizes later executive determinations, defines covered categories, preserves exceptions and wind-down provisions, and gives the president a national-interest waiver. The third-country mechanism is directed at countries in specified top-five categories, not every country that buys any Russian energy.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes — the enrolled bill is controlling; the White House confirms signature; AP separately describes the executive discretion concern.
- **Current assessment:** false.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:** [GPO enrolled H.R. 5334](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr5334enr/html/BILLS-119hr5334enr.htm) supplies exact operative language; [White House signing notice](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/congressional-bill-h-r-5334-signed-into-law/) establishes enactment; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/donald-trump-russia-sanctions-ukraine-lindsey-graham-d1715ad5e8feccba76bfae51c5f28c45) reports the vote and debate over presidential discretion.
- **Questions for auditor:** Build an atomic list of what is mandatory, what is discretionary, which provisions have a 30-day clock, and what the waiver/reporting process actually constrains.

## Claim: The United States has obtained permanent control over Greenland's security and all related decisions

- **Origin / principal promoters:** President Trump's public characterization of the prospective agreement, amplified in headline shorthand. It is stronger than the wording released by the Danish and Greenlandic governments.
- **Why it matters:** “Control” suggests a transfer of sovereign authority and a settled change to alliance command arrangements. That would be far more consequential than an agreement expanding access, consultation, investment screening, or military presence within Danish and Greenlandic sovereignty.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Trump said the United States would have permanent control over security and other needs, and AP reports an expected larger U.S. military role. The United States already has extensive defense rights and operates Pituffik Space Base under the longstanding treaty framework.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The Danish-Greenlandic joint release says an agreement is expected to be signed the following week and will require national parliamentary procedures. It expressly says the framework recognizes sovereignty, territorial integrity, and Greenlanders' right to self-determination. No signed text was public by the cutoff. The 2004 treaty supplement requires consultation and identifies existing procedures rather than conveying sovereignty.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial — all parties confirm negotiations and an expected agreement, but the broad “control” formulation is not corroborated by a public text or the counterpart governments' release.
- **Current assessment:** unclear.
- **Confidence:** high that the strong claim is unestablished; low on the final agreement's undisclosed terms.
- **Sources:** [Danish-Greenlandic joint release](https://stm.dk/en/press/press-releases/expected-agreement-on-strengthened-security-in-the-arctic-and-the-north-atlantic-area/) provides the counterpart governments' language and procedural status; [2004 treaty supplement](https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rls/or/35269.htm) provides the legal baseline; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-greenland-military-denmark-4cf77cb4608b685a70410bd679479193) records Trump's statement and reported U.S. description.
- **Questions for auditor:** Do not translate a political statement into legal effect. Reassess only after obtaining the signed text, annexes, and ratification terms.

## Claim: The Iran war has definitively cost the United States $43.6 billion in total

- **Origin / principal promoters:** A nonpublic CENTCOM estimate inspected by AP, likely to circulate as an all-in price tag without its date and exclusions.
- **Why it matters:** A total-cost claim shapes congressional funding, public support, and comparisons with other wars. It can also conceal the more strategically relevant finding that the reported estimate assigns $28.1 billion to munitions.
- **Evidence offered for it:** AP reports that CENTCOM estimated $43.6 billion in Defense Department costs through September 3. A separate public CBO estimate puts Defense Department operational, logistical and sustainment costs at about $38 billion through August 1, making the order of magnitude plausible.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** AP reports that the CENTCOM number excludes damage to U.S. bases. It is a Defense Department estimate through a specified date, not a full whole-of-government, allied, economic, reconstruction, or future-replacement cost. The underlying CENTCOM document is not public, and CBO warns that different cost categories are qualitatively different.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial — CBO corroborates a broadly similar order of magnitude with an earlier cutoff, but not CENTCOM's exact figure or accounting method.
- **Current assessment:** weak as stated; supported only when restated as “CENTCOM's reported Defense Department estimate through September 3, excluding base damage.”
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-costs-us-military-congress-interceptors-7541f88490d17747537ee3fdbc119af8) reports the document and its exclusions; [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62756) provides a public comparator, scope definitions, and tempo scenarios.
- **Questions for auditor:** Keep the two estimates separate. Obtain the exact CENTCOM categories if the document becomes public; do not infer that their difference equals August spending.

## Claim: China deliberately rammed the Philippine fisheries vessel off Palawan

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The Philippine Coast Guard, Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, Department of Foreign Affairs and National Task Force for the West Philippine Sea. China says the Philippine vessel ignored warnings, crossed the Chinese vessel's bow, and bears responsibility.
- **Why it matters:** Deliberate use of a coast-guard ship against a civilian government vessel inside the Philippines' claimed exclusive economic zone would mark a coercive escalation and raise alliance-management questions. A navigational collision in a dangerous encounter would remain serious but imply a different intent threshold.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Philippine authorities released video showing a China Coast Guard cutter closing from astern/alongside before contact. The physical collision and visible damage are corroborated by multiple reports; the Philippines says its vessel maintained course on a fuel-support mission about 54 nautical miles from Palawan. Reuters Connect and USNI carry video descriptions and the Philippine timeline.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** China assigns responsibility to the Philippine vessel. Public clips do not provide a complete bridge-to-bridge audio record, both ships' full tracks, rudder/engine orders, or all minutes before contact. Those missing records limit a definitive finding about subjective intent, even though the closing maneuver is observable.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial — the collision, participants, location and damage are independently observable; deliberate intent is inferred from the maneuver and remains contested.
- **Current assessment:** plausible.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Sources:** [Philippine News Agency/DFA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/index.php/articles/1284397) gives the official Philippine location and condemnation; [USNI News with video](https://news.usni.org/2026/09/18/video-china-coast-guard-rams-philippine-fisheries-vessel-in-south-china-sea) describes the sequence and publishes the imagery; [Reuters Connect](https://www.reutersconnect.com/item/philippines-china-coast-guard-rams-philippine-vessel-carrying-fuel-to-fishermen-off-palawan/dGFnOnJldXRlcnMuY29tLDIwMjY6bmV3c21sX09XU0hCQzExODY2MDU) supplies licensable incident footage and metadata; [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/chinese-coast-guard-vessel-hits-philippine-supply-boat-in-spratly-islands) records China's competing account.
- **Questions for auditor:** Seek automatic-identification, radar or full track data and the unedited video. In final prose, state the collision as fact and attribute the allegation of deliberateness unless stronger evidence emerges.

## Claim: Iran's Revolutionary Guard struck the tanker Trend while it illegally attempted to transit the Strait of Hormuz

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Iranian state broadcasting citing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.
- **Why it matters:** A verified Iranian attack on a named commercial tanker would directly affect shipping risk, insurance, naval protection decisions and enforcement of Iran's restrictions in the strait.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Iranian state media identified the Togo-flagged Trend and said the IRGC struck it during an “illegal” attempted passage. The claim is temporally plausible in an environment with other projectile and maritime incidents.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** AP could not independently confirm the report. The inspected sources do not provide geolocated imagery, an owner/operator statement, crew testimony, damage photographs, a matched UKMTO incident notice, or verified ship-track evidence tying Trend to the alleged attack. “Illegal” is Iran's characterization, not an independently established legal conclusion.
- **Independent corroboration:** no.
- **Current assessment:** unclear.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Sources:** [AP regional roundup](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-roundup-news-september-18-2026-024350a0820dfc5dd433b4e13445fab5) attributes the claim and states that it could not confirm it.
- **Questions for auditor:** Check the ship manager, flag registry, UKMTO incident log, AIS history and imagery. Do not merge a separate unknown-projectile report with the Trend claim merely because both occurred in the same theater.

## Claim: China is building a dedicated mothership that will carry four extra-large unmanned submarines

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Naval analysts interpreting commercial satellite imagery, including H. I. Sutton; ISW and South China Morning Post summarize the interpretation.
- **Why it matters:** A dedicated recovery and support ship could extend Chinese uncrewed undersea operations into distant waters and complicate anti-submarine, seabed and blockade planning.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Imagery shows a roughly 200-meter unusual vessel at Hudong-Zhonghua with a large stern opening or apparent launch-and-recovery feature. Analysts say the geometry is consistent with carrying several extra-large UUVs.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** External hull geometry does not establish the mission. No official designation, builder description, internal arrangement, ownership, payload, crew concept or sea trial confirms that it is a military unmanned-submarine mothership, much less that it will carry exactly four vehicles.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial — multiple analysts can inspect the same physical vessel, but their reports may share the same Vantor imagery and inference chain rather than constitute independent confirmation of its purpose.
- **Current assessment:** plausible.
- **Confidence:** low.
- **Sources:** [ISW September 18 China–Taiwan update](https://understandingwar.org/research/china-taiwan/china-taiwan-update-september-18-2026/) identifies the imagery and analytic chain; [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3368012/could-mystery-shanghai-vessel-be-mothership-chinas-underwater-drones) reports additional specialist interpretation.
- **Questions for auditor:** Carry only as a watch item unless original imagery and an independent source materially strengthen the mission assessment. Avoid converting an analyst's capacity estimate into a known loadout.

## Claims to carry into verification

1. **Automatic 500/100 percent tariffs under H.R. 5334 — false.** This is the most important correction because statutory discretion changes the immediate economic forecast.
2. **Permanent U.S. control over Greenland security — unestablished.** The counterpart release confirms a prospective agreement while preserving sovereignty language; the text is not public.
3. **$43.6 billion as the complete Iran-war cost — misleading without scope.** Preserve the date, Defense Department scope and reported exclusion of base damage.
4. **Deliberate Chinese ramming off Palawan — plausible but intent remains contested.** State the collision and damage as facts; attribute the intent claim.
5. **IRGC strike on Trend — unconfirmed.** Do not merge it with other maritime alerts.
6. **Chinese unmanned-submarine mothership with a four-vehicle loadout — plausible, low confidence.** Observable vessel, inferred mission.
