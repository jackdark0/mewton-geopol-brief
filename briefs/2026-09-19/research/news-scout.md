## Candidate: Trump signs the Graham Russia-and-Iran sanctions law

- **Event time/date:** 2026-09-18 evening, Washington.
- **Region / actors:** United States, Russia, Iran, major buyers of Russian energy.
- **What changed:** President Donald Trump signed H.R. 5334. The enrolled text mandates a broad review and imposition process against Russian officials, state-linked banks, energy and defense support networks, and shadow-fleet vessels; it also directs tariffs of up to 100% on goods from countries in specified top-five Russian oil/gas buyer or sanctions-evasion categories. Most operative steps begin within 30 days, and the president retains a national-interest waiver after reporting to Congress. The act also extends the Iran Sanctions Act to 2031.
- **Why this may matter:**
  - moves a large part of Russia pressure from executive discretion into statute;
  - creates potential trade leverage against major Russian-energy buyers, but the actual tariff rates and waivers remain executive choices;
  - could complicate U.S. trade ties with China, India, Turkey, or other large buyers.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Confidence in basic event:** high.
- **Source chain:** White House notice and enrolled GPO text are primary and independent of AP's reporting; AP explains political context.
- **Sources:** [White House signing notice](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/congressional-bill-h-r-5334-signed-into-law/) confirms enactment; [enrolled bill text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr5334enr/html/BILLS-119hr5334enr.htm) establishes duties, sanctions, exceptions, waivers, termination, and the Iran extension; [AP](https://apnews.com/article/donald-trump-russia-sanctions-ukraine-lindsey-graham-d1715ad5e8feccba76bfae51c5f28c45) reports the signing and vote history.
- **Questions for auditor:** Which provisions are mandatory versus discretionary? Who are the likely top-five buyers under the statutory methodology? What can be waived or adjusted without a veto-proof congressional response?
