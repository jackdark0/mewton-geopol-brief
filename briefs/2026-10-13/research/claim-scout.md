## Method and labels

- Each heading is an atomic proposition, not a paraphrase of a broader narrative.
- **Primary — claimant** means the actor's own statement; it establishes the claim and wording, not necessarily its truth.
- **Primary — observable record** means an official record competent to establish an action, measurement, or procedural status.
- **Independent secondary** means reporting based on its own reporting or inspection. Independence is claim-specific.
- **Derivative / shared lineage** means the item repeats another actor's claim or assessment and is not counted as corroboration of the underlying fact.
- Verdict and confidence are separate. “Unclear” or “weak” does not mean false. “Plausible” does not mean verified.
- No aggregate truth rate is calculated. These six packets are a purposive set of consequential disputed claims, not a complete denominator of all claims made during the window.

## C-01 — Claim: As of the cutoff, Russia and Ukraine had both agreed to an immediately effective reciprocal halt on strikes against each other's energy infrastructure

- **Atomic proposition:** Before 2026-10-13 06:00 UTC, both belligerents assented to the same reciprocal prohibition on energy-infrastructure strikes, with a common scope and start time already in effect.
- **Claim type:** Empirical status / diplomatic agreement.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. President Donald Trump announced the ceasefire on 2026-10-11 and reaffirmed it on 2026-10-12. In a White House press exchange he said, “They agreed because I said they agreed.” The White House and sympathetic commentary promoted the agreement as operational.
- **Why it matters:** A real agreement would constrain a major Russian pressure tool and Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining capacity. A ceasefire announced without mutual assent or defined terms instead creates a ready-made violation narrative and could become leverage against only one belligerent.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Trump's public declaration and insistence are direct evidence that the U.S. president said an agreement existed. Ukraine said it supported an energy ceasefire in principle and refrained from refinery strikes for at least one night. This supports willingness, not assent to a shared text.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Ukraine's president described Kyiv's position as conditional — “we are ready to respect an energy ceasefire if Russia is ready to do the same” — and said Ukraine was waiting for details and was ready to sign guarantees. The UK prime minister said Ukraine would implement when a start date was confirmed. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov would not confirm Russian assent and said contacts were ongoing. No common text, target definition, monitoring method, Russian acceptance, or mutually acknowledged start time was public by the cutoff. Ukrainian officials also reported subsequent Russian strikes on energy sites; those strike claims do not by themselves prove that no secret understanding existed, but they undercut the claim of an operating mutual halt.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for a completed mutual agreement. **Partial** for the narrower facts that Trump made the announcement, Ukraine expressed conditional willingness, Russia did not publicly confirm it, and energy strikes continued.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Trump's press remarks are the originating U.S. claim. Ukrainian presidential material is a participant response, not independent verification. AP separately reported the White House exchange, Peskov's non-confirmation, and Ukrainian strike allegations. The UK statement reflects a direct call with Zelenskyy but corroborates only that a start date was unconfirmed.
- **Current assessment:** **Unsupported.** A U.S. proposal and Ukraine's conditional willingness are established; a mutually assented, operational ceasefire is not.
- **Confidence:** **High** that the public record did not establish a completed agreement by the cutoff; **low** on whether undisclosed U.S.–Russian contacts contained narrower oral commitments.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “Trump announced an immediate energy ceasefire, but Ukraine described conditional readiness, Russia did not publicly confirm assent, and no common scope or start time was available by the cutoff.”

## C-02 — Claim: Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps directed the suspected 2026-09-27 plot against RAF Fairford

- **Atomic proposition:** The IRGC, rather than merely Iran-linked individuals acting independently, directed the suspected attack planning around RAF Fairford discovered on 2026-09-27.
- **Claim type:** Intelligence / attribution / command responsibility.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham stated in Parliament that the incident was “highly likely to have been coordinated in Iran” and “almost certainly directed” by the IRGC. The UK government, the United States, and 30 other governments publicly recognized the UK attribution.
- **Why it matters:** The claim attributes covert attack planning on a U.S.-used British base to a state military organization during an active Iran war. If correct, it materially raises the case for sanctions, retaliation, and heightened NATO-base security; if overstated, it could intensify an interstate conflict on undisclosed evidence.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The UK presented a formal intelligence assessment. Reuters reported that U.S. officials separately described a suspected Iranian drone plot and that the U.S. removed bombers from Fairford because of the threat. UK Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said a known Iran-based individual was believed to be behind the incident. Police found gasoline in the suspects' vans, and Iran had previously described bases used to attack its territory as legitimate targets.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The underlying intelligence, communications, tasking chain, drone equipment, and alleged Iran-based coordinator's identity were not made public. No viable explosive was found. Seven arrested men had been released on bail and none had been charged by the cutoff. Iran denied involvement and demanded evidence. Capability, motive, an Iran-based contact, or prior threats would not alone establish IRGC command.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** U.S. officials' reported Iranian-plot assessment appears to be a second government intelligence stream, but the public record does not show whether it is independent of the UK intelligence. The multinational joint statement expressly “recognises” the UK's attribution and is therefore derivative, not independent confirmation.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Burnham's statement is the originating public UK assessment. The multinational statement repeats that assessment. Reuters adds named procedural facts and anonymously sourced U.S. official reporting. AP independently confirms arrests, bail status, gasoline/no-explosives findings, and Iran's denial, but does not independently prove IRGC direction.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible.** The convergent UK and reported U.S. assessments are significant, but the command-attribution chain is not publicly auditable.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate.** Confidence is limited by undisclosed intelligence, unclear U.S.–UK source independence, no charges, and missing public forensic or communications evidence.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “The UK assesses that the IRGC almost certainly directed the suspected Fairford plot; U.S. officials have also described an Iran-linked drone threat, but the public evidence and command chain remain undisclosed.”
- **Sources and source entailment:**  
  - [UK prime minister's Commons statement, 2026-10-12](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/pm-statement-to-the-house-of-commons-12-october-2026) — **Primary — claimant government.** Entails the formal confidence language and attribution; it does not disclose the evidence.

## C-03 — Claim: Russian actors attempted to influence the 2016 Brexit referendum

- **Atomic proposition:** Russian state or state-directed actors undertook an influence operation aimed at affecting the UK's 2016 EU referendum. This proposition does **not** include the distinct claim that the operation changed the result.
- **Claim type:** Foreign interference / attribution / historical intelligence assessment.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on a podcast that advisers gave him a clear affirmative answer after he entered office. In Parliament he narrowed his public case: Russian interference in the 2019 election is on record, and the government thinks it is “inconceivable” that this was the first attempt. The government separately clarified that it considers the 2016 result legitimate and does not claim interference changed it.
- **Why it matters:** The claim shapes the legitimacy debate around Brexit and the rationale for a new UK information-defense institution. Collapsing “attempted influence” into “changed the outcome” would materially overstate the public record.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Burnham says he received an affirmative official answer. The 2020 Intelligence and Security Committee report documented credible open-source studies of RT, Sputnik, and social-media activity and criticized the government for failing to seek a proper post-referendum assessment. The report also concluded that the state had underestimated Russia's intent and capability before 2016.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Burnham disclosed no operation, actor, platform, tasking record, scale, audience, or intelligence basis. The 2020 ISC report explicitly said it had not been given a post-referendum assessment and therefore did not resolve the question. A 2020 government answer said it had seen no evidence of **successful** interference, but that is a different and narrower proposition than whether an attempt occurred. Russia denies meddling.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial / unknown.** The older open-source studies and ISC findings support risk and possible activity, but the current affirmative assessment is classified and cannot be checked for independence from those same sources.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Burnham's podcast description and Commons answers are the present claim origin. AP and Reuters reports reproduce those remarks. The ISC report is an earlier independent parliamentary review, but it mainly establishes the absence of a proper assessment, not the new claim's truth. The 2020 Cabinet Office answer addresses success, not attempt.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible.** An attempted influence effort is consistent with the official assessment and earlier public evidence, but the evidence supporting the new certainty was not disclosed.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** in the “attempted” proposition; **low** for any claim about scale, operational mechanism, or effect on the result.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “Burnham says the UK now assesses that Russian actors tried to influence the Brexit vote. He has not released the evidence, and the government does not say the effort changed the result.”
- **Sources and source entailment:**  
  - [Hansard, International Update, 2026-10-12](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2026-10-12/debates/54FB2EDB-83AF-40FE-8A98-A914AD221CD7/InternationalUpdate) — **Primary — claimant / parliamentary record.** Entails Burnham's narrowed public reasoning and explicit denial that he claimed the result was altered.  
  - [Associated Press, 2026-10-12](https://apnews.com/article/uk-eu-brexit-russia-interference-burnham-3804ec3d1fe161cce6b9870853df5ab4) — **Independent secondary.** Entails the podcast account, government clarification that attempts were unsuccessful, and Russia's denial.  
  - [UK Intelligence and Security Committee Russia report, 2020](https://isc.independent.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/CCS207_CCS0221966010-001_Russia-Report-v02-Web_Accessible.pdf) — **Primary — parliamentary inquiry record.** Entails that government had not sought or supplied an adequate post-referendum assessment and that open-source allegations existed; it does not establish the present affirmative claim.

- **Questions for auditor:** Has a post-2020 intelligence assessment now been completed? Can an unclassified summary identify the actors, channels, objectives, and evidence standard? ## C-04 — Claim: The suspended PTI provincial government in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa knowingly facilitated the Pakistani Taliban

He said Intelligence Bureau and law-enforcement reports showed the provincial government empowered militants, facilitated attacks, and “sublet police stations” to the Taliban. The allegation was used to justify emergency federal rule.
- **Why it matters:** The allegation is the strongest factual justification offered for suspending an elected provincial government and deploying the army in Pakistan's most security-sensitive province. If substantiated, it indicates state penetration by militants; if not, it may be a pretext for removing the federal government's main political rival.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Federal ministers invoked undisclosed Intelligence Bureau and law-enforcement reports, alleged militant safe passage for PTI supporters near the Afghan border, and pointed to the province's deteriorating security. The underlying increase in militant violence is real context, but violence alone does not establish provincial-government collusion. The government also claimed a 196% rise in incidents over two and a half years; it did not publish the numerator, denominator, incident definition, or baseline in the inspected statement.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No intelligence report, police-station record, named militant liaison, payment, order, communication, or prosecutable act was released. PTI denied the claim and challenged the emergency. The timing — during PTI's march and immediately after failed political talks — supplies a plausible partisan alternative explanation for the accusation, though it does not disprove it. No court finding or charge established collusion by the cutoff.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for knowing collusion. AP independently confirms that the allegation was made, PTI denied it, and the suspension occurred; it does not corroborate the underlying militant relationship.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Radio Pakistan directly reports the federal news conference and is the originating public official lineage. AP quotes the same ministers and PTI response; its procedural reporting is independent, but the “solid evidence” claim still traces to the federal government. Court-challenge reporting establishes the legal contest only.
- **Current assessment:** **Unclear.** The public record is insufficient to move a serious collusion allegation to supported or even clearly plausible status; absence of disclosed proof is not proof that the intelligence is false.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** that the claim was not publicly substantiated by the cutoff; **low** on the underlying truth because the claimed intelligence remains secret.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “Pakistan's federal government alleges that the suspended KP administration facilitated the TTP, citing undisclosed intelligence reports. PTI denies it, and no public evidence or judicial finding established the allegation by the cutoff.”

## C-05 — Claim: Houthi forces carried out the 2026-10-10 mass-casualty strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport

- **Atomic proposition:** Weapons launched or directed by Yemen's Houthi movement caused the 2026-10-10 explosion and casualties at King Khalid International Airport.
- **Claim type:** Battlefield attribution / responsibility.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree claimed the attack. Saudi-aligned regional officials later told AP that an initial investigation attributed the barrage to a Houthi sequence of drones followed by cruise and ballistic missiles. Saudi partners and much reporting adopted Houthi attribution.
- **Why it matters:** Attribution may determine Saudi and possible U.S. retaliation, air-defense assistance, and whether the airport strike is treated as a deliberate Houthi escalation rather than an unidentified projectile, interception failure, or third-party action.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Houthis claimed responsibility; the attack fits their openly declared campaign and demonstrated missile/drone capability. Two regional officials told AP that the initial probe reconstructed a Houthi saturation sequence over roughly 1,000 kilometers. Subsequent Houthi claims and Saudi interception reports described additional Houthi launches toward Riyadh. Airport video and casualty reporting independently establish that a strike occurred, not who launched it.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The inspected public record contained no debris analysis, serial numbers, launch-track data, radar plot, imagery of launch sites, or public Saudi forensic attribution for the 2026-10-10 strike. The two officials were anonymous and may share the same Saudi-led investigation. A self-claim can be truthful but is not independent confirmation. The precise causal chain — direct impact versus interception debris or defense failure — was also not public.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** Houthi self-claim plus an initial regional-official investigation are two accounts but not fully independent or publicly auditable evidence streams. Reuters independently verified attack effects and reported the claim; it did not verify launch origin.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Saree's statement is the claimant origin. Reuters relays the self-claim and separately reports Saudi authority and eyewitness evidence of airport effects. AP's two regional officials provide a distinct investigative account, but their institutional relationship and independence from one another are unclear.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible.** Houthi responsibility is the leading explanation, but the public forensic chain was not closed by the cutoff.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate.** The self-claim, capability, pattern, and regional-official account converge; missing public launch/debris evidence and anonymous-source dependence prevent high confidence.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “The Houthis claimed the deadly Riyadh airport strike, and regional officials told AP an initial probe attributed it to a Houthi drone-and-missile barrage; public forensic evidence was not released by the cutoff.”
- **Sources and source entailment:**

## C-06 — Claim: The longer-range missile in North Korea's 2026-10-12 salvo was a hypersonic weapon

- **Atomic proposition:** At least one missile launched from the Pyongyang area on 2026-10-12 was equipped with and demonstrated a hypersonic glide or maneuvering capability, rather than merely following an unusual ballistic trajectory.
- **Claim type:** Technical military classification.
- **Origin / principal promoters:** The interpretation emerged from Japan's statement that the roughly 910-kilometer missile may have followed an irregular trajectory, the launch's proximity to South Korea's hypersonic test, and specialist/media speculation. South Korea did not confirm the classification.
- **Why it matters:** A demonstrated hypersonic or maneuvering weapon changes assessments of regional missile-defense stress and North Korean force development. Premature classification inflates the technical meaning of a launch before trajectory data are available.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Japan measured one missile at more than 910 kilometers, about 70 kilometers maximum altitude, and said an irregular trajectory was possible. South Korea detected multiple missile types and one flight beyond 900 kilometers. The characteristics are compatible with a maneuvering system, but compatibility is not identification.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Japan said analysis was continuing. South Korea repeatedly declined to classify the missile as hypersonic and said precise analysis was required. Before the cutoff, KCNA described at least one test in the salvo as a “tactical ballistic missile” testing an unspecified upgraded component, not as a hypersonic system. KCNA's description is a participant claim and may cover only part of the multi-type salvo, so it narrows but does not conclusively refute the proposition. No speed profile, glide-phase data, maneuver plot, separation imagery, or confirmed system name was public.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for a hypersonic classification. Japan and South Korea independently corroborate launches and some flight measurements, but neither confirmed hypersonic performance.
- **Source lineage / provenance:** Japan's Ministry of Defense and South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff are separate sensor/official streams. AP synthesizes both. Yonhap and Reuters relay KCNA's own later description; those reports share KCNA as the source for the “tactical ballistic missile” label.
- **Current assessment:** **Weak.** “Ballistic missile with a possibly irregular trajectory” is supported; “hypersonic weapon” was not established by the cutoff.
- **Confidence:** **Moderate.** The classification remains sensitive to unreleased trajectory data and to whether KCNA described every missile type in the salvo.
- **Publication-safe correction:** “North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles; Japan said one may have followed an irregular trajectory, but neither Japan nor South Korea confirmed a hypersonic weapon, and KCNA called at least one test a tactical ballistic missile.”
- **Sources and source entailment:**  
  - [Japan Ministry of Defense, 2026-10-12](https://www.mod.go.jp/j/press/news/2026/10/12e.html) — **Primary — observable official sensor assessment.** Entails at least two launches, approximate range/altitude, and only a possibility of irregular flight; it does not entail hypersonic classification.  
  - [South Korea Ministry of National Defense briefing, 2026-10-12](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=156784863) — **Primary — observable official sensor assessment.** Entails multiple missile types and one beyond 900 kilometers; repeatedly says hypersonic classification remained under analysis.  
  - [Associated Press, 2026-10-12](https://apnews.com/article/north-korea-kim-missiles-south-5b2d3a2d9a31118648b9f82022b9c4e3) — **Independent secondary synthesis.** Entails the two governments' measurements and the source of the hypersonic interpretation; it adds no independent technical classification.  
  - [Yonhap relay of KCNA, published 2026-10-13 06:27 KST / 2026-10-12 21:27 UTC](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261013000700315) — **Derivative from DPRK state media.** Entails KCNA's “tactical ballistic missile” description and unspecified component-upgrade claim; it may not cover all missiles detected by Japan and South Korea.  
  - [Reuters relay of KCNA, updated 2026-10-13 02:04 UTC](https://www.aol.com/articles/north-korea-test-fires-tactical-214449000.html) — **Derivative from DPRK state media.** Corroborates what KCNA reported, not the technical truth of the label.
- **Questions for auditor:** Did allied sensors observe sustained glide, cross-range maneuver, or a pull-up/corkscrew profile? Which detected missile does KCNA's singular “tactical ballistic missile” describe? Did the two official sensor streams track the same set of launches? Is “irregular trajectory” a literal measured feature or a provisional category used before classification?
