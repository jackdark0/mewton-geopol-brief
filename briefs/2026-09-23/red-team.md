## High-risk framing errors

### 1. Iran talks could be oversold as direct negotiations or a near-deal

- **Problem:** The strongest named account says mediators shuttled between the sides. The seven-day Hormuz terms come from one unnamed Iranian official.
- **Required correction:** Use “indirect talks.” Attribute the offer. State that Washington has not accepted it and no ceasefire exists.
- **Residual risk:** Medium. The political importance is real, but the probability of implementation cannot be estimated from the evidence.

### 2. Greenland political language could outrun the treaty

- **Problem:** “Control,” “ownership” and “permanent bases” can imply more than the text grants.
- **Required correction:** Lead with the signed access rights, identify mutual-agreement clauses and say the agreement is not yet in force. Explicitly reject sovereignty transfer.
- **Residual risk:** Low after correction.

### 3. Ukraine item risks turning a trial balloon into a ceasefire

- **Problem:** The official Ukrainian readout is generic. The energy concept is a proposal associated with Macron, and Russia has not agreed.
- **Required correction:** Put “no ceasefire exists” in the first paragraph and make Russian assent the first watch item.
- **Residual risk:** Medium because further details may emerge after the cutoff.

### 4. EU sanctions and French deterrence could be presented as a proven trade

- **Problem:** The timing and Latvian statement support linkage in the same bargaining period, not a documented quid pro quo.
- **Required correction:** Describe parallel outcomes and say no formal exchange is public. Explain that the French initiative is not a strict nuclear guarantee.
- **Residual risk:** Medium.

### 5. China’s rule could be mislabeled a ban or a proven fentanyl breakthrough

- **Problem:** Licenses may still be granted, and effects are not measured.
- **Required correction:** Use “permit requirement,” preserve the 16-to-18 denominator and ask for enforcement data.
- **Residual risk:** Low.
