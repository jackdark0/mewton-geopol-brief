### 1. U.S. and Iran used mediators for a day of talks at the UN

- **What changed:** U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said mediators shuttled between U.S. and Iranian teams on September 22. A senior Iranian official separately told Reuters that Tehran had offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eased military pressure and lifted its blockade of Iranian ports.

### 2. U.S., Denmark and Greenland signed an open-ended Arctic defense agreement

- **What changed:** The three governments signed an amendment to the 1951 Greenland defense agreement. It authorizes modernization at Pituffik, provides for new defense areas at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig subject to mutually agreed details, expands U.S. access and overflight rights, and screens some investment in sensitive sectors.
- **Why it may matter:** The agreement hardens the U.S. military position in the Arctic and ties future Greenlandic independence to assuming the agreement and remaining in or applying to NATO.
- **Caveat:** It is not in force until Danish and Greenlandic parliamentary procedures are complete. New defense areas require consultation and mutual agreement; the text reaffirms Danish sovereignty and Greenlandic self-determination.
- **Sources:** [full agreement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/agreement-between-the-government-of-the-united-states-of-america-and-the-government-of-the-kingdom-of-denmark-together-with-the-government-of-greenland-to-amend-and-supplement-the-agreement-of-27-apri/); [Danish prime minister’s office](https://stm.dk/en/press/press-releases/agreement-between-greenland-denmark-and-the-united-states/); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/084ac2a82952c2dff403c48bd3564113)

### 5. Latvia accepted the sanctions renewal as France opened nuclear-deterrence talks

- **What changed:** Latvia, the final holdout, said it would abstain rather than block the EU decision and pursue national sanctions against Usmanov and Fridman. The Latvian government also said Emmanuel Macron agreed to begin talks on Latvia joining France’s Forward Nuclear Deterrence Initiative and to strengthen French military presence, especially air defense.

### 6. China added two drug precursors to North America-specific export controls

- **What changed:** Five Chinese agencies added two chemicals to a permit list for exports to the United States, Mexico and Canada, taking the list from 16 to 18 and making the change effective immediately.
- **Why it may matter:** The move supplies a concrete counternarcotics deliverable immediately before Xi Jinping’s U.S. state visit and shows Beijing can tighten or loosen administrative controls that Washington treats as a bilateral security issue.
- **Caveat:** A permit rule is not an export ban, and no enforcement or shipment data yet show its effect. Timing alone does not prove the move was a summit concession.
- **Sources:** [Chinese government notice](https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/zfxxgkpt/fdzdgknr/202609/t20260922_717309.shtml); [State Council Information Office](https://english.scio.gov.cn/m/pressroom/2026-09/22/content_118708376.html); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/6e3012905f3263b796325c03e94d1e40)
