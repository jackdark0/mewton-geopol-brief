# Red-team review - 2026-10-27

## Finding: The two North African decisions are not deployments yet

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Algeria and Egypt “Strategic significance” sections.
- **Problem:** The analysis correctly states the implementation gap, but phrases such as “raises the cost” and “strengthens deterrent signaling” can still sound like delivered capability.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Algeria disclosed no unit, timing or rules of engagement. Egypt disclosed no deployment order or force package. Authorization and executive intent can affect expectations, but cannot be assumed to change interception capacity or ground security.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Both governments may be maximizing political reassurance while retaining the option not to deploy substantial forces.
- **Required editorial action:** qualify. Use “could” for capability effects; make the absence of observed movement the lead limitation in each story.

## Finding: Do not manufacture one regional package from separate Gulf tracks

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Egypt and Tehran stories; show-discussion candidate on deterrence and diplomacy.
- **Problem:** Juxtaposition may imply that the Egypt authorization and Tehran meeting were coordinated parts of one strategy.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The records establish that both occurred in the same window, not that the four governments designed them together.

## Finding: Serbia's 140-seat figure is preliminary

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Serbia “What changed” and editorial ranking.
- **Problem:** “Wins” can imply a certified result and obscure pending complaints or final allocation.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** RIK explicitly called the result preliminary. OSCE documented process problems but did not find that they changed the winner.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The result is highly likely to produce a majority, but the legal and political transfer remains incomplete.

## Finding: OSCE criticism is not proof of a stolen Serbian election

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Serbia strategic significance and any contested-claims wording.
- **Problem:** A compressed brief could put serious campaign failures next to the result in a way that implies outcome causation.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Observers positively assessed most observed voting stages while documenting intimidation, resource misuse, media bias and tabulation shortcomings. They did not conclude those defects reversed the result.
- **Best competing interpretation:** An uneven campaign can coexist with a decisive and correctly counted outcome.
- **Required editorial action:** keep both findings and explicitly separate process legitimacy from result causation.

# Analysis that survived challenge

- The Algeria and Egypt stories are major because their legal and executive thresholds changed, provided the brief does not convert them into operational deployments.
- The Tehran meeting is a verified new channel but not a bargain; the analysis's process-versus-outcome distinction holds.
- The Serbia result and OSCE findings can both be reported without implying that observed shortcomings changed the winner.
- The Brazil late-vote denominator is a strong rebuttal to a specific result-changing theory, while the broader rigging allegation remains unsupported because no evidence was supplied.
- Both UN reports warrant major-story treatment if their conclusions remain attributed and distinct from court judgments.
- Oleshky is a real implementation test whose success should be measured by aid, evacuation and within-zone compliance, not by fighting elsewhere.

# Highest-risk unresolved issue

The largest publication risk is collapsing legal or political stages: authorization into deployment, a commission finding into a court judgment, or a procedural defect into proof that an election outcome was caused by fraud. Every headline and BLUF line must preserve those boundaries.
