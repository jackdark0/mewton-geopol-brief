## Bottom line

The proposed six-story brief is supportable, but only if the editor preserves four bright lines:

1. The Makkah defense statement has **three parties**, not five, and announces implementation measures rather than a completed deployment.
2. Entry into Yemeni locations is not durable control of the Bab el-Mandeb coast.
3. The Black Sea sinking is confirmed; the Russian-drone cause is an attributed Ukrainian claim pending Romanian evidence.
4. Ukraine's “51%” refers to capacity it says it struck, not demonstrably 51% of Russia's national installed capacity.

Any headline or top-line summary that blurs those distinctions would fail the evidence standard.

## Story-by-story stress test

### 1. Yemen and the Makkah arrangement

**Strongest evidence:** AP's direct reporting and witness descriptions support a major offensive and entry into Dhubab. The Saudi Press Agency text directly supports the three-party implementation commitment.

**Weakest link:** The military control picture relies heavily on interested officials and wartime witnesses. U.S. support is reported through two anonymous officials. The pact supplies no public force table.

**Plausible alternative interpretation:** The offensive may be a limited Saudi-backed push amplified by political messaging, while the alliance statement may be designed chiefly to deter further attacks on Saudi territory rather than to create a standing expeditionary force.

**Required editorial correction:** Do not say “secured,” “liberated,” or “deployed.” Say forces “entered” or “advanced,” and say the three parties committed to implementation measures for rapid deployment in Saudi Arabia.

**Confidence after challenge:** Medium-high on the core story; medium on its durability.

### 2. Ethiopia and the four-state declaration

**Strongest evidence:** The declaration itself clearly links Red Sea governance, Nile conduct, Somalia's territorial integrity, and recurring coordination.

**Weakest link:** The analysis calls the grouping a “front,” which can imply military alignment beyond the text. The states may share objections to Ethiopia while differing sharply on Sudan, internal security, and the proper policy response.

**Plausible alternative interpretation:** The declaration could be low-cost diplomatic signaling intended to improve each participant's bilateral leverage, not the beginning of a durable bloc.

**Required editorial correction:** Prefer “coordinated line” or “alignment” to “alliance.” Make clear that Abiy spoke in bargaining terms and that no imminent Ethiopian military move is established.

**Confidence after challenge:** High on the declaration; medium on institutional durability.

### 3. Spain's election call

**Strongest evidence:** Sánchez's own institutional statement fixes the intended election date and legal sequence.

**Weakest link:** The dissolution decree was to be published after the cutoff. Campaign forecasts can become stale quickly and depend on the electoral system, not just national vote share.

**Plausible alternative interpretation:** An early election may be an attempt to regain initiative rather than an admission that defeat is inevitable.

**Required editorial correction:** Say “announced” or “called” an election. Do not state that parliament had already been dissolved at the cutoff. Frame PP–Vox as one possible governing route, not the expected result.

**Confidence after challenge:** High on the election call; low on the outcome.

### 4. Bosnia's preliminary returns

**Strongest evidence:** The election commission portal supplies a direct station denominator; AP supplies candidate and political context.

**Weakest link:** The 95.52% figure applies to represented ordinary polling stations at the snapshot, while several other ballot categories remained absent. It is easy to misread this as 95.52% of all votes.

**Plausible alternative interpretation:** The preliminary outcome may confirm existing structures more than it “strengthens nationalism.” A stable lead does not prove a new mandate for secession.

**Required editorial correction:** Use “preliminary” in the heading or opening sentence. Never call results final, and avoid converting an electoral result into an immediate institutional crisis.

**Confidence after challenge:** High on the preliminary direction; medium on consequences.

### 5. Black Sea vessel

**Strongest evidence:** Romania's Interior Ministry directly establishes location, rescue count, deaths, fire, and sinking.

**Weakest link:** Zelensky's attribution is not independent evidence. Route data do not establish cargo, ownership, targeting rationale, or intent.

**Plausible alternative interpretations:** The vessel may have been struck accidentally, damaged by debris, hit by a misdirected weapon, or suffered an unrelated fire. The inspected evidence cannot choose among these.

**Required editorial correction:** Put “cause unconfirmed” in the heading or first sentence. Avoid “attack” unless directly attributed. Do not imply NATO territory or an automatic Article 5 question from an incident in an exclusive economic zone.

**Confidence after challenge:** High on the casualty event; low-to-medium on cause.

### 6. Russian refinery disruption

**Strongest evidence:** The IEA provides a national installed-capacity denominator and observed/estimated throughput decline. AP accurately preserves Ukraine's claim and its verification limit.

**Weakest link:** The Ukrainian percentage has no public facility ledger, common date, repair assumption, or stable definition of “disabled.” The IEA's June figures are older context, not a measurement taken on October 5.

**Plausible alternative interpretation:** Ukraine may be counting nameplate capacity at every facility that sustained relevant damage while Russia continues to run unaffected units, reroute crude, and repair equipment. A high “struck capacity” percentage can coexist with much smaller national output loss.

**Required editorial correction:** Never shorten the claim to “Ukraine disabled 51% of Russian refining.” State the quoted denominator in paraphrase: Ukraine says 51% of the capacity it has struck is disabled. Immediately contrast that with the IEA's separate national throughput estimate and date.

**Confidence after challenge:** High that disruption is substantial; low-to-medium on the 51% measure.

## Cross-cutting failure modes

### Source independence

Multiple reports can repeat one official's claim. The Yemen control claims, Ukrainian Black Sea attribution, Ukrainian refinery figure, South Korean mine attribution, and DOJ allegations each have a common originating authority. The final must not imply “multiple confirmations” merely because several outlets carried the same statement.

### Temporal leakage

Spain's decree publication and any official investigation update after 02:00 America/New_York fall outside the edition. Calendar entries may describe future events, but later news cannot be backfilled into the story text.

### Causal overreach

- A new alliance statement does not prove battlefield coordination.
- The El Alamein declaration does not prove a planned anti-Ethiopian war.
- A nationalist election lead does not cause immediate Bosnian secession.
- Refinery throughput loss does not by itself prove a decisive constraint on Russian military operations.

### Loaded language

Avoid “war bloc,” “NATO waters,” “crushing defeat,” “crippled half of Russia's refineries,” “Chinese spy,” or “North Korean attack” without attribution and qualification. These formulations exceed the evidence or collapse allegation into fact.
