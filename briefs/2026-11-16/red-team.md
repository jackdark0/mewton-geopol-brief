## Finding: Do not let land control become control of the strait

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Yemen story; “Houthis emerged with the stronger land position around the strait” and all maritime-leverage analysis.
- **Problem:** The strongest source establishes a government withdrawal and remaining contested coastal positions. It does not provide a complete independent control map or measure the change in Houthi targeting capability. A punchy headline can easily outrun the careful body and become “Houthis control Bab el-Mandeb.”
- **Evidence or reasoning:** JMIC says the strait remained open, about 22 cargo-vessel transits per day continued on November 12-14, and no Houthi shipping attack was confirmed in its reporting window. Reuters says government-linked units remained south of Dhubab.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The government abandoned exposed positions to consolidate its defense while retaining enough nearby presence to contest the coast; the immediate maritime threat did not materially change.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Headline and BLUF must say “retreat near/overlooking Bab el-Mandeb,” not “loss/control of the strait.” State that added leverage is an assessment and use continued traffic as the limiting fact.

## Finding: The Bahrain event has three separate propositions

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Bahrain story and contested-claim treatment.
- **Problem:** The attack, attempted target, and successful impact can be silently merged. The first is supported by opposing official accounts and public alerts; the second is an Iranian claim; the third is unsupported.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Bahrain said attacks were intercepted before reaching targets. Iran said six drones targeted and struck a U.S. radar. Reuters found no U.S. or Bahraini confirmation of target or damage.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Iran launched drones but exaggerated or misstated the target effect after interception; alternatively, one or more vehicles could have reached an unacknowledged target. The record cannot choose beyond saying impact is unestablished.
- **Required editorial action:** **correct.** Use separate sentences and explicit attribution. Do not write “Iran attacked Al-Rifaa” as an established target, and do not write “all drones were destroyed” unless attributed to Bahrain.

## Finding: Do not turn temporal proximity into a proved policy test

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Bahrain strategic significance; claim that the event tests Trump's reported restraint condition.
- **Problem:** The event is relevant to the conditional signal, but “test” may imply the administration has accepted the event as falling under that condition. No White House response existed by cutoff.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The same Reuters report places the attack next to Trump's reported threshold, but the threshold is undefined and the administration had not classified the incident.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Washington may treat an intercepted attack as routine defense activity below the threshold, or may privately respond without changing public posture.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** Say the event “raises the first immediate question for” or “could clarify” the threshold; do not say it has activated or tested policy as a fact.

## Finding: Estonia's attribution is specific but not independently reproducible

- **Severity:** critical
- **Targets:** Estonia story, headline, BLUF, and any use of “GRU attack.”
- **Problem:** The Estonian security service may have strong evidence, but the public sources do not expose the evidentiary chain. The suspects are detainees, not convicted agents.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The foreign-ministry record reports KAPO's conclusion and eight detentions, while withholding communications, money trails, handler identities, or charging evidence.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The suspects attempted the attack for another reason, were intermediaries without knowledge of a state sponsor, or the public attribution is accurate but based on intelligence that readers cannot inspect.
- **Required editorial action:** **qualify.** “Estonia says/attributes” must appear in the headline or first sentence. Call the eight people suspects or detainees. State that underlying evidence is not public.

- **Targets:** Estonia “what changed” and watch indicators.
- **Problem:** Official phrasing varies between a government plan, an order to take necessary steps, and a categorical ministerial announcement. The closure date is December 1, so the editor must not write that Estonia has already closed the border or imply the legal instrument was inspected.
