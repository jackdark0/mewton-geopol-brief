# Claim Scout - 2026-11-23

**Cutoff:** 2026-11-23 02:00:00 America/New_York (07:00 UTC)
**Research window:** approximately 2026-11-21 20:00 America/New_York through the cutoff

## Claim: Ukraine launched the Benghazi attack from a base in western Libya

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Two unnamed military officials affiliated with eastern Libya's Libyan National Army, citing an initial investigation.
- **Why it matters:** The claim could implicate western Libyan territory in the Russia-Ukraine war and intensify Libya's internal conflict.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Officials told AP that several Ukrainian sea drones were launched from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya; four were intercepted and two struck the Russian corvette. Satellite imagery supports serious vessel damage but not attacker or origin.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No public launch imagery, wreckage analysis, command record, Ukrainian acknowledgment, or independent origin assessment was available by the cutoff. Eastern Libyan authorities have political and security interests in blaming western rivals.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for the strike and damage; no for Ukrainian responsibility or western-Libya launch origin.
- **Current assessment:** unclear.
- **Confidence:** high that the strike damaged the vessel; low on attribution and origin.
- **Sources:** [AP report and imagery](https://apnews.com/article/libya-russia-vessel-drone-benghazi-59f6f304f3053c69c8cc890138a94d1b); [UNSMIL statement](https://unsmil.unmissions.org/en/press-releases/unsmil-expresses-serious-concern-over-the-attacks-carried-out-at-benghazi).
- **Questions for auditor:** Can satellite or maritime tracking identify launch direction; did Russia publish damage or attribution evidence; was the ship operational?

## Claim: Two tankers struck Iranian naval mines east of Hormuz on November 22

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Iranian naval authorities and state-linked reporting.
- **Why it matters:** Confirmed Iranian mining or controlled minefields would materially raise energy-shipping risk and could trigger U.S. military action.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Iranian authorities said two tankers entered a restricted area and struck mines. UKMTO separately recorded a tanker incident involving military forces off Chabahar at 14:25 UTC.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** UKMTO did not identify a mine, attacker, vessel, damage, or link to two tankers. No named vessels, AIS tracks, crew accounts, imagery, or independent maritime authority confirmation were inspected.
- **Independent corroboration:** no for the two-mine account; partial only for a separate tanker-military incident.
- **Current assessment:** unclear.
- **Confidence:** high that UKMTO received one report; low on the Iranian two-tanker mine claim.
- **Sources:** [UKMTO Advisory 217-26](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/20261122_ukmto_-advisory_217-26.pdf); [Iran International report of the broader confrontation](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202611224547).
- **Questions for auditor:** Identify vessels and military force; locate JMIC update; distinguish mine, projectile, interdiction and fire accounts.

## Claim: Russia's intelligence services directed both assassination plots against the same Ukrainian-American

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Denmark's PET for the September Danish plot; Polish authorities for an August Polish plot; European Commission political statements accept the Danish attribution.
- **Why it matters:** A repeated cross-border operation would support a pattern of Russian proxy violence inside NATO and EU states.
- **Evidence offered for it:** PET says the Danish plot was planned and initiated by Russian intelligence; three people were stopped near a concert with firearms and knives, two were charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service, and Denmark expelled Russia's defense attaché. Poland says it arrested a Russian citizen recruited by Russian services in an earlier attempt against the same person.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The public PET statement does not disclose the intelligence behind command attribution. The defendants have not been convicted, and PET expressly separates its intelligence assessment from the criminal court's future findings. Russia denies the allegations.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial. Danish and Polish investigations are distinct, but public evidence for shared Russian command remains limited.
- **Current assessment:** supported as an official two-country attribution; unproven as a public evidentiary claim.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Sources:** [Danish PET](https://pet.dk/pet/nyhedsliste/rusland-udnytter-danske-kriminelle-miljoeer-til-likvideringsforsoeg-paa-dansk-grund/2026/11/20); [AP on Poland's disclosure and Russia's denial](https://apnews.com/article/poland-denmark-russia-ukraine-rapper-plots-291d2e6c33441c06963c4807c32eb7cd); [European Commission statement](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/statement_26_2556/STATEMENT_26_2556_EN.pdf).
- **Questions for auditor:** What public evidence connects recruiters or handlers; what precise offenses are charged; what did Poland disclose on November 22 versus in August?

## Claim: COP31 produced no agreement or useful outcome

- **Origin / principal promoters:** “Climate talks collapse” headlines and commentary.
- **Why it matters:** The framing changes how audiences judge both the material loss and the remaining multilateral machinery.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Parties did fail to agree the two presidency-led decisions, including the overarching cover package.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The UN climate chief said key negotiating decisions were made, including on just transition, and cited action-agenda and electrification-package progress. The failure was serious but not equivalent to zero decisions or zero implementation activity.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes on failure of the cover decisions; yes that other outputs survived.
- **Current assessment:** false if stated as “no outcomes”; supported if narrowed to “no consensus on the presidency-led cover decisions.”
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:** [UNFCCC closing remarks](https://unfccc.int/news/un-climate-change-executive-secretary-simon-stiell-remarks-at-the-closing-plenary-of-cop31); [AP on the failed agreement](https://apnews.com/article/cop31-climate-talks-united-nations-global-warming-baed7f1491d16a00ee9b60a35596e1f6); [official draft report](https://docs.unfccc.int/documents/10000401/files/en/CP31_draft_report.pdf).
- **Questions for auditor:** Produce the adopted-decision list and clarify the status of the electrification package.

## Claim: Trump decided to resume attacks on Iran after January 1

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Strong headline readings of Trump's Fox News comments.
- **Why it matters:** A fixed decision would imply imminent military preparation and a hard diplomatic deadline.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Trump reportedly said he was “inclined” to resume strikes after the New Year if no deal was reached and threatened blockade-running ships.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** “Inclined” and “if” are conditional. No attack order, target list, formal ultimatum, or announced negotiating deadline was inspected.
- **Independent corroboration:** multiple reports, but they share the Fox interview as one origin.
- **Current assessment:** false if phrased as a decided attack; supported as a conditional public threat.
- **Confidence:** high on the scope correction; medium-high on exact wording pending the full clip.
- **Sources:** [Iran International summary](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202611224547); [Daily Beast report](https://www.thedailybeast.com/trump-drops-ominous-hint-about-his-new-years-plans-for-iran).
- **Questions for auditor:** Retrieve the complete Fox video; check whether the condition was “no deal by year-end” or a broader post-New-Year decision point.

## Claim: The 258-seat Democratic House result is a mandate to end the Iran war

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Partisan interpretations of the midterm result and reporting that Iran-war anger contributed to Republican losses.
- **Why it matters:** The narrative may shape war-powers, funding and oversight strategy.
- **Evidence offered for it:** AP reported that voters were angry about prices and the Iran war, and Democrats will control both chambers with large margins.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The election covered many issues; no single-issue national vote or common foreign-policy program establishes an Iran-war mandate. The new Congress does not take office until January.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial on the electoral result; insufficient for a single-issue mandate.
- **Current assessment:** weak as stated; supported only that the result increases Democrats' capacity to act.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:** [AP on the 258-seat result and reported voter concerns](https://apnews.com/article/democrats-house-majority-election-2026-congress-721096a4d9ba2cd68683692361798cdb).
- **Questions for auditor:** Incoming leadership commitments, district-level evidence, and actual war-powers or appropriations proposals.
