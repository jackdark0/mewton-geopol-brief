## Claim: U.S. forces struck and rendered five named Iranian crude-oil tankers inoperable on September 8 after IRGC missile attacks on a U.S. warship

- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. Central Command. Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly framed tanker strikes as the direct cost Iran would pay for each attempted strike on U.S. naval vessels.
- **Why it matters:** The action expands the U.S.-Iran contest from escort and interdiction to a repeated campaign against Iran's tanker fleet, increasing escalation and global supply risk.
- **Evidence offered for it:** CENTCOM named M/T Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1, Riesco, and Derya; said crews were directed to abandon ship; and published strike video. It said the same U.S. warship had evaded Iranian ballistic missiles twice in two days. Iranian state television independently acknowledged U.S. attacks on tankers near Jask and Kharg Island and said crews were evacuated, with reports covering at least three tankers/areas.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Iranian reporting corroborates multiple attacks and crew evacuation but not all five named hulls, their IRGC ownership, or “rendered inoperable” status. CENTCOM is the sole public source for the exact five-ship inventory, the full damage assessment, the prior two attempted missile attacks, and the claim that the tankers funded the IRGC network. AP reproduced CENTCOM's list as “Kivik” rather than the command's “Kaviz,” a discrepancy worth resolving. No independent after-imagery or crew manifest was available in the inspected pre-cutoff record.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial.
- **Current assessment:** supported for U.S. strikes on multiple Iranian tankers, with the exact five-hull/damage account still principally CENTCOM-sourced.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.
- **Sources:** [CENTCOM, Sept. 8](https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/PUBLIC-RELEASES/Article/4593050/us-destroys-5-irgc-tankers-after-iran-targets-another-american-warship/) — primary U.S. claim, five names, trigger, crew warning, and damage characterization; [AP, published Sept. 8 at 20:45 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/iran-tanker-navy-warship-dfc9a1d9c9a5b8bc7b47fcc004025e65) — independent reporting of Iranian state-TV acknowledgment and the three reported strike areas, plus the name discrepancy.
- **Questions for auditor:** Match IMO numbers to all five names; resolve Kaviz/Kivik; inspect original CENTCOM video frame by frame; seek SAR/optical after-imagery, AIS histories, port/crew records, and Iranian loss notices; verify whether “destroyed” and “rendered inoperable” refer to the same damage threshold.

## Claim: Iran captured a functioning, cutting-edge U.S. underwater drone in a complex operation at the entrance to Hormuz

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The IRGC Navy and Iranian state media described the Dive-LD as one of America's most modern intelligent unmanned submarines and the capture as a complex intelligence and operational success.
- **Why it matters:** A coerced capture of a functioning sensitive platform could expose U.S. surveillance methods and demonstrate Iranian control at the strait's entrance; recovery of a failed commercial-configuration vehicle has much smaller intelligence and operational meaning.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Iranian media published imagery/video of an object matching the appearance of an Anduril Dive-LD. The U.S. military and Anduril both acknowledged losing possession of a Dive-LD in the region.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** CENTCOM said the older, defective drone had malfunctioned more than a day earlier, carried no classified sonar or radar, and collected no sensitive data; Anduril called loss an expected design possibility for an attritable system. Reuters matched the visible vehicle to archive imagery but could not verify when or where the Iranian footage was recorded. No public evidence shows Iran disabled, intercepted, or compelled a functioning vehicle, rather than recovered one already dead in the water.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for Iranian possession of a Dive-LD; no for the claimed complex capture of a functioning advanced-intelligence asset.
- **Current assessment:** weak as promoted; supported only in the narrow sense that Iran obtained a U.S. Dive-LD the United States had lost.
- **Confidence:** high on possession; medium on the malfunction sequence because that remains a U.S. account.
- **Sources:** [AP, Sept. 8](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-iran-us-hormuz-yemen-houthi-lebanon-israel-saudi-3a2a047784c920cadf6e3911bb8969ac) — carries the Iranian claim and CENTCOM's detailed malfunction/non-sensitive-configuration response; [Reuters via MarketScreener, Sept. 8](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/iran-says-it-captured-us-submarine-drone-in-strait-of-hormuz-ce785bd9d889f72d) — adds Anduril's acknowledgment and attritable-system explanation; [Reuters visual verification, released Sept. 8](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/us-iran-tensions-rise-after-tanker-strikes-ce785bd9db8bf724) — matches the object to Dive-LD archive imagery while explicitly leaving date and location unverified.
- **Questions for auditor:** Authenticate original file metadata and geolocate the video; establish the last U.S. telemetry time/position; determine whether propulsion, mission payload, and data storage were present; distinguish recovery, capture, and electronic/kinetic interception.

## Claim: Iran fired 20 ballistic missiles toward U.S. targets in Jordan; Jordan intercepted 18 and two fell in unpopulated areas without casualties

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Iranian state media claimed launches toward U.S. targets. A Jordan Armed Forces spokesperson supplied the 20-engaged, 18-destroyed, two-unpopulated-area, zero-casualty account.
- **Why it matters:** A large direct Iranian salvo against a U.S.-hosting ally broadens the conflict and tests regional air defense; claimed damage or intercept performance affects deterrence narratives on both sides.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Iran acknowledged launching missiles toward U.S. targets. Jordan officially reported engaging 20 ballistic missiles, destroying 18, and finding the other two in unpopulated areas; it reported no casualties and deployed debris teams.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The two parties corroborate a launch and Jordan as the target area, but each promotes a different performance narrative. Iran did not provide independently verified impact/damage evidence in the inspected record. Jordan provided no radar track, interceptor count, debris inventory, impact coordinates, or explanation of whether “destroyed” includes missiles that broke up or were deflected. CENTCOM told AP it had no information to offer at that point, so U.S. target damage and personnel status were not independently established by cutoff.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for the attack; no for the exact intercept rate or claimed target effects.
- **Current assessment:** supported for a substantial Iranian missile attack toward Jordan; plausible for Jordan's 18-of-20 account; unclear on intended-target damage.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Sources:** [Jordan News Agency/Petra, Sept. 9 at 02:57 local time](https://www.petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-armed-forces-intercept-18-iranian-ballistic-missiles-targeting-kingdom) — direct Jordan Armed Forces account, denominator, locations, and casualty status; [AP, Sept. 8–9](https://apnews.com/article/iran-tanker-navy-warship-dfc9a1d9c9a5b8bc7b47fcc004025e65) — records the Iranian launch claim, Jordan's response, and CENTCOM's lack of an immediate account.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain geolocated debris/impact imagery and hospital/emergency logs; identify the intended base and whether projectiles crossed only Jordanian airspace or were aimed at it; request U.S. base-damage and personnel reports; keep launch count, engaged count, destroyed count, and impacts as separate denominators.

## Claim: Houthi emissaries helped Iraqi militias plan and execute the July two-day drone swarm against Saudi oil facilities from a joint operations room

- **Origin / principal promoters:** An AP investigation citing two Saudi officials familiar with intelligence and a senior Iraqi security official; all were anonymous. The broader coordination narrative is also promoted by Saudi and U.S. officials concerned about Iran-backed groups.
- **Why it matters:** Operational integration would indicate a more networked Iranian-aligned threat able to attack Saudi oil infrastructure from multiple directions while preserving ambiguous attribution.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Three regional officials told AP that Houthi emissaries worked in an Iraqi-militia operations room for the July attack. A Houthi official and an Iraqi militia member separately confirmed that at least one Houthi official died in subsequent U.S.-Saudi strikes in Iraq. A U.S. military official said Houthis had been killed in earlier Iraq strikes and that Washington was concerned about their presence. Houthi leader Abdel-Malek al-Houthi had publicly referred to a joint operations room during the Gaza war.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Two Popular Mobilization Forces officials denied PMF involvement in the Saudi attack and denied that Houthis operated under its auspices; the Houthis did not answer AP's request. Houthi presence in Iraq and a Houthi death after the strikes do not prove participation in this particular attack. The three central confirmations are unnamed, may not be independent of one intelligence stream, and are not backed publicly by communications, travel records, launch-site evidence, or named participants.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for Houthi presence/coordination infrastructure in Iraq; no public independent proof tying the emissaries to the July swarm.
- **Current assessment:** plausible.
- **Confidence:** medium-low.
- **Sources:** [AP, published Sept. 9 at 03:59 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-houthis-yemen-iraq-blockade-41b104b908daeb58ad1d714224f8f570) — lays out the three-official intelligence claim, two-source confirmation of a Houthi death in Iraq, U.S. concern, PMF denials, and source anonymity/lineage.
- **Questions for auditor:** Determine whether the two Saudi officials and Iraqi official relied on distinct intelligence; seek travel/communications intercepts, launch-site forensics, Houthi casualty identity, and operations-room location; separate “Houthis present in Iraq” from “Houthis planned this attack.”

## Claim: The September 8 Houthi attack caused fires at Saudi oil facilities, temporarily halted some operations, and wounded 73 civilians

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Saudi coalition and government ministries for casualty and damage claims; Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree for responsibility and intended targets.
- **Why it matters:** Hits on Red Sea-side Saudi infrastructure could impair the main export bypass for disrupted Hormuz traffic and push Saudi Arabia back into full-scale war in Yemen.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Saudi officials reported 73 civilians wounded across Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan, and Najran, plus fires and temporary suspensions at oil facilities/utilities. The Houthis claimed dozens of missiles and drones aimed at oil, industrial, and military targets. A Planet Labs image published by AP shows a black smoke plume at an Aramco facility north of Abha on September 8, independently supporting at least one substantial fire at the claimed target class.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The satellite image does not establish the weapon, responsible launcher, casualties, number of facilities hit, or volume/duration of lost output. The exact 73 count remains Saudi official reporting; there was no public patient-level list or hospital cross-check. The Houthi projectile count and implied precision are not independently enumerated. Temporary suspension is not the same as a national production or export loss.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes for an attack and at least one Aramco-area fire; partial for temporary disruption; no independent confirmation of the exact injury count or projectile total.
- **Current assessment:** supported, with magnitude qualifiers.
- **Confidence:** high for attack/fire; medium for the exact injuries and operational effect.
- **Sources:** [AP, published Sept. 8 at 04:02 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/saudi-arabia-yemen-houthis-4ad9446f0bb8c096750c84b6e1ba86b8) — Saudi and Houthi accounts, 73 injuries, fires, temporary suspensions, and named targets; [Saudi official statement, Sept. 8](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/news/official-statements/4001791) — primary Saudi count and four-city denominator; [AP investigation, published Sept. 9 at 03:59 UTC](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-houthis-yemen-iraq-blockade-41b104b908daeb58ad1d714224f8f570) — Planet Labs image caption showing smoke from an Aramco facility north of Abha.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect the original Planet scene and FIRMS detections; obtain facility outage/throughput data and timestamps; reconcile the 73 injuries with hospital and civil-defense records; map confirmed impacts separately from all claimed targets.
