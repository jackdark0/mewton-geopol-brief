## Story: Houthi attacks damaged Saudi facilities and injured civilians

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- [SUPPORTED] [FACT; confidence: high] Saudi authorities reported attacks on civilian and economic sites in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan, and Najran, while the Houthis claimed the attacks. Associated Press reported the wave during the window. The occurrence has adversarial-side acknowledgment; each side's detailed operational account remains its own claim.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: moderate] Saudi authorities reported 73 injured civilians, including women and children. No hospital ledger or independently compiled victim list was inspected.
- [SUPPORTED] [FACT; confidence: moderate] Saudi officials reported fires and temporary suspensions at oil and utility facilities. The reporting supports localized disruption but does not establish lost production or a national supply effect.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: moderate] The Houthis said they used missiles and drones and named targets. Weapon counts, interception rates, and exact impact points were not independently established.

### Material claims not established

- A complete weapon/interception count; which named facility each weapon hit; the duration and quantity of lost output; a verified injury roster; or a link to Iraqi groups.

### Source provenance

- [Saudi Ministry of Foreign Affairs statement](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/news/official-statements/4001791) — primary Saudi record for the government's locations and 73-injury claim; an interested-party source, not independent verification.
- [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/4ad9446f0bb8c096750c84b6e1ba86b8), updated September 8 before cutoff — independent reporting that also records Houthi responsibility claims and Saudi damage claims. It does not create an independent origin for the official injury number.

### Open questions

- What do facility operators report about damage, throughput, and restoration times?
- Can imagery or debris analysis identify impact points and weapon types?

### Recommendation

- Include as a major development. Attribute the 73 figure and facility effects to Saudi authorities, and do not infer an oil-market shock from temporary suspensions.

## Story: United States struck five tankers it linked to the IRGC

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- [SUPPORTED] [FACT; confidence: high] The United States said it struck five tankers near Jask and Kharg Island, and Iranian state television separately acknowledged U.S. strikes near those locations and said crews evacuated. These are adversarial lineages supporting that strikes occurred on multiple tankers.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: moderate] CENTCOM named the five vessels as M/T Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1, Riesco, and Derya and said all were rendered inoperable after crews were directed to abandon ship. Associated Press recorded a spelling discrepancy for one vessel (“Kivik”), so the first name is not fully reconciled.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: low] CENTCOM described all five as part of an IRGC financial/shadow-fleet network. No registry, ownership chain, sanctions record, or cargo documentation was inspected to verify that predicate for each ship.
- [CONFIRMED] [FACT; confidence: high] No inspected source established crew deaths from these five strikes; evacuation reports should not be rewritten as proof that every crew member was safe.

### Material claims not established

- Exact damage to each vessel, cargoes, beneficial ownership, IRGC financial control, environmental release, or legal basis beyond the U.S. government's stated rationale.

### Source provenance

- [U.S. Central Command release](https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/PUBLIC-RELEASES/Article/4593050/us-destroys-5-irgc-tankers-after-iran-targets-another-american-warship/) — primary belligerent account for targets, names, and battle-damage assessment.
- [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-tanker-navy-warship-dfc9a1d9c9a5b8bc7b47fcc004025e65), updated September 8 before cutoff — independent reporting and retrieval of Iran's acknowledgment. Its strike information still depends on U.S. and Iranian actor statements; the lineages were not merged.

### Open questions

- Can vessel registries and commercial imagery confirm identities, locations, cargoes, and damage?
- What evidence connects each tanker to IRGC financing?

### Recommendation

- Include as a major escalation. State that the United States struck five tankers and that Iran acknowledged strikes in the two areas; attribute “IRGC-linked” and “inoperable” to CENTCOM.

## Story: Iran launched ballistic missiles toward Jordan; Jordan claimed 18 of 20 intercepted

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- [SUPPORTED] [FACT; confidence: high] Jordan's armed forces said missiles were launched from Iranian territory at the kingdom, while Iranian state media separately said Iran launched missiles toward U.S. targets in Jordan. The sources support the launch and destination but disagree by implication about whether Jordan itself or U.S. facilities there were the intended target.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: moderate] Jordan said its forces engaged 20 ballistic missiles, destroyed 18, and that two fell in unpopulated areas with no casualties. All four numbers come from one official Jordanian denominator.
- [CONFIRMED] [ARITHMETIC; confidence: high] Eighteen divided by twenty is 90%, but “90% interception rate” would inherit the uncertainty of Jordan's self-reported numerator and denominator. No independent launch count was retrieved.
- [UNSUPPORTED] [CLAIM; confidence: high] The available evidence does not establish which U.S. facility was targeted or that all missiles were aimed at the same target set.

### Material claims not established

- Independent launch and intercept counts, intended aim points, missile variants, impact locations, and battle-damage assessment.

### Source provenance

- [Jordan News Agency/Petra](https://www.petra.gov.jo/en/news/jordan-armed-forces-intercept-18-iranian-ballistic-missiles-targeting-kingdom) — primary Jordanian record, posted at 02:57 local time September 9, for the 20/18/two/no-casualty account.
- [Associated Press regional report](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-iran-us-hormuz-yemen-houthi-lebanon-israel-saudi-3a2a047784c920cadf6e3911bb8969ac), updated September 8 before cutoff — independent reporting that retrieved Iran's state-media claim. It does not independently count launches or interceptions.

### Open questions

- Will debris, radar data, or U.S. records identify missile types and intended targets?
- Did any missile or interceptor debris cause unreported damage?

### Recommendation

- Include as a major development. Present “18 of 20” as Jordan's claim and preserve the targeting ambiguity between Jordan and U.S. facilities in Jordan.

## Story: United States and Canada escalated their trade conflict

**Priority:** high

### Verified propositions

- [CONFIRMED] [FACT; confidence: high] Canada imposed counter-tariffs effective 00:01 EDT September 8 on 629 U.S. tariff lines at rates of 15%, 25%, or 50%, covering what Canada described as C$27.6 billion in imports.
- [CONFIRMED] [FACT; confidence: high] The United States issued five Section 338 proclamations in response and announced import bans on specified Canadian product categories, including certain dairy and alcohol products, scheduled for September 29, plus other tariff-schedule changes dated September 15.
- [CONFIRMED] [FACT; confidence: high] Canada's C$27.6 billion measure and U.S. descriptions using U.S.-dollar values are different currency denominators. They must not be presented as contradictory totals or added together without conversion and a common scope date.
- [CLAIMED] [CLAIM; confidence: moderate] The White House said Canada's measures were discriminatory and justified the U.S. response. That is the U.S. government's legal and political position, not an independently established motive or adjudicated finding.

### Material claims not established

- Realized trade losses, pass-through to prices, retaliation beyond the published measures, the outcome of any legal challenge, or a converted like-for-like bilateral total.

### Source provenance

- [Canada Department of Finance product list](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/08/list-of-products-from-the-united-states-subject-to-counter-tariffs-effective-september-8-2026.html) — primary Canadian record for effective time, tariff rates, product count, C$ scope, and origin/in-transit rules.
- [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-responds-to-canadas-retaliation/) — primary U.S. summary for the five proclamations, implementation dates, categories, and government rationale.
- [White House dairy proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/excluding-certain-canadian-products-from-importation-into-the-united-states-in-response-to-continued-discrimination-against-the-commerce-of-the-united-states-with-respect-to-dairy/) — primary legal text for one import-ban category.
- An AP page updated after the cutoff was inspected but excluded from the evidence chain; none of its later framing is used here.

### Open questions

- How do the product lists compare on a common currency, base period, and customs-value denominator?
- Will either government exempt in-transit goods, inputs, or politically sensitive products beyond the stated rules?

### Recommendation

- Include as a major strategic-trade development. Preserve C$27.6 billion as Canada's denominator and distinguish measures already effective from U.S. measures scheduled for later dates.

# Rejected or deferred candidates

- **IRGC attacks on two U.S. ships and eight tankers — rejected as a combined count.** The scouts' count aggregated incidents and actor statements with unclear vessel identities and time boundaries. CENTCOM substantiated a narrower U.S. tanker-strike account, not the entire Iranian claim set.
- **Iran's recovery of a U.S. Dive-LD underwater drone — deferred to Rapid Fire.** U.S. and Iranian accounts support that Iran obtained a Dive-LD after the United States lost possession, but disagree on capture versus malfunction and on sensitivity. The event was lower priority than the eight retained items; if used, say the mechanism and intelligence value are disputed.
- **South Korea-France Hormuz statement — rejected for the main brief.** The inspected evidence set did not include a joint primary text, and a statement that troops were not being deployed did not itself establish a material change.
- **Trump non-opposition to Israeli military plans — deferred.** The proposition depended on an Axios account about private deliberations. No transcript, order, or second independent origin was inspected.
- **Claim that the UAE warned Netanyahu before October 7 — deferred.** The alleged communication, recipients, timing, and content remained disputed and were not grounded in a primary record available in this audit.
- **UN commission findings on Syria war crimes — deferred.** Potentially significant legal findings require the report itself and paragraph-level findings, including attribution and evidentiary standard. Those were not successfully inspected here.
- **Hungary's expulsion of ten Russian diplomats — deferred.** The candidate relied on reporting not retrieved in this audit; identities, legal status, and the government's stated basis were not verified.
- **Romanian sabotage-operation allegation — deferred.** An alleged operation and attribution were not established with a primary judicial or security record plus independent corroboration.
- **Bosnia's expulsion of Serbian diplomats — deferred.** The legal authority, number/status of personnel, and implementation were not verified from an inspected primary record.
- **Trump-Putin call — deferred.** A call's occurrence alone is insufficient for a major item, and no transcript or independently verified policy outcome was inspected.
- **Houthi-Iraqi militia coordination — rejected as unestablished.** The claim depended on anonymous sourcing and inference; no attributable operational evidence established coordination.
- **China export surge — deferred.** The scouts mixed monthly, year-on-year, and destination-specific comparisons. No customs table was inspected to establish a common product, period, currency, and base denominator.
- **German transfer of nationally held air-defense interceptors — deferred as a separate claim.** The broader UK/EU package is retained, but the German quantity, system, delivery timing, and national-stock status were not established in an inspected primary source.
