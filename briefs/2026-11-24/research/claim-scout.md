## Claim: Russia can launch a nuclear-armed satellite within 60 days

- **Origin / principal promoters:** A joint WDR/NDR/Sueddeutsche Zeitung investigation citing intelligence warnings shared among NATO states and assessments by the Bundeswehr and BND.
- **Why it matters:** If accurate, the warning compresses allied decision time and raises immediate space, treaty, and escalation questions.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The investigation reports that a satellite called Sputnik-S has been completed and stored near Moscow and that early-November warnings described a possible launch within 60 days. DIA separately assesses that Russia is developing a satellite designed to carry a nuclear weapon.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** No named official or public technical evidence confirms Sputnik-S, warhead integration, launch preparation, or a Russian decision to deploy it. Russia denies the account. NATO publicly said it continually assesses deterrence but did not validate the timeline.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial for the development program; no for the 60-day readiness claim.
- **Current assessment:** plausible that Russia is developing the capability; unclear that a launch can or will occur within 60 days.
- **Confidence:** medium on development; low on readiness and intent.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.tagesschau.de/investigativ/ndr-wdr/satellit-russland-100.html - originating investigation and Russian response.
  - https://www.dia.mil/Portals/110/Images/News/Military_Powers_Publications/Challenges_Security_Space_2026.pdf - independent public development assessment.
  - https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2026/11/23/joint-press-conference-by-the-secretary-general-with-the-federal-chancellor-of-germany - NATO/German public response.
- **Questions for auditor:** What observable launch indicators exist? Is the 60-day window an intelligence confidence judgment, a worst-case planning assumption, or a technical estimate?

## Claim: Ukraine sank the Russian corvette Merkury and disabled a weapons-carrying cargo ship

- **Origin / principal promoters:** President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Ukrainian officials.
- **Why it matters:** Damage level determines the operation's military effect; cargo attribution affects whether the second vessel was a legitimate military target and whether Russian African logistics were disrupted.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Zelenskyy claimed responsibility, said the corvette was on the seabed, and described the second vessel as carrying weapons. Imagery and AP reporting show the corvette severely damaged or capsized.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Eastern Libyan authorities described heavy damage rather than confirming a sinking. Public evidence for the second ship's cargo, damage, and operational role is limited. Launch location and local support remain unverified.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial on damage; no on all operational details.
- **Current assessment:** supported that Ukraine conducted the operation and severely damaged the corvette; unclear on sinking status, cargo, and launch origin.
- **Confidence:** high on responsibility; medium on corvette condition; low on cargo and launch claims.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/raketi-dlya-petriotiv-potribni-tut-v-ukrayini-mi-duzhe-ochik-107457 - direct claim.
  - https://apnews.com/article/cbd82b965a111dcf4605b8b2d0493780 - independent reporting, imagery description, and Libyan statements.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate current satellite imagery and vessel tracking; verify which ship is the Angara and whether cargo manifests or imagery support the weapons claim.

## Claim: FinCEN identified $14.7 billion in Iranian illicit finance

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Treasury messaging about a FinCEN Financial Trend Analysis.
- **Why it matters:** Treating suspicious activity as proven illicit finance would overstate both the evidentiary finding and sanctions-enforcement success.
- **Evidence offered for it:** U.S. financial institutions reported about $14.7 billion in activity potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking from January 2025 through August 2026.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Treasury's own wording is "potentially linked." Suspicious-activity reporting does not by itself establish Iranian control, illegality, or unique proceeds; the summary does not disclose overlap or a proved-illicit denominator.
- **Independent corroboration:** no; this is an official data analysis.
- **Current assessment:** false if phrased as $14.7 billion proven illicit finance; supported if phrased as activity flagged as potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0698/ - exact scope and qualifying language.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect the full FinCEN report for report counts, thresholds, duplicates, methodology, and cautionary language.

## Claim: Russia violated South Korean territorial airspace near Ulleung Island

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Initial South Korean government alerts and some early public reporting.
- **Why it matters:** Territorial airspace violation is legally and strategically more serious than entry into an air-defense identification zone.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Safety Ministry initially issued an airspace-violation warning and shelter instruction.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** South Korea's Joint Chiefs corrected the account, saying the aircraft entered KADIZ and flew near territorial airspace. The alert was lifted after three minutes. Aircraft tracks were not published.
- **Independent corroboration:** no public independent track evidence.
- **Current assessment:** weak; the authoritative military account supports a KADIZ entry, not a confirmed territorial-airspace violation.
- **Confidence:** high that the stronger claim is not established.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/south-north-korea-tensions-airspace-ulleung-island-a0347d1dda863d8189bbdf21c320ee3b - initial warning and Joint Chiefs correction.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain track maps and the original warning text; seek a Russian account.

## Claim: Canada's defense plan is not credible and a mixed fighter fleet would undermine NORAD

- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. Defense Department fact sheet and policy officials.
- **Why it matters:** The claim can shape a major fighter procurement, bilateral defense relations, and continental air defense.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Pentagon figures on serviceability and readiness, Canada's staged spending plan, and personnel burdens associated with a mixed fleet. Canada's budget watchdog has also questioned the spending path.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The public report does not show a neutral readiness audit or quantify the marginal cost of a mixed fleet. Canada cites its NATO Latvia deployment, Ukraine support, recent spending increase, and need for domestic industrial benefits. Joint operations and intelligence sharing continue.
- **Independent corroboration:** partial on spending-path concerns; unclear on the causal NORAD claim.
- **Current assessment:** supported that Canada faces readiness and funding problems; unclear that the fighter review itself materially undermines NORAD.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/canada-us-defense-spending-hegseth-pentagon-2ce983323fc56d21db88db5401422fde - Pentagon claims, Canadian figures, and countercontext.
  - https://globalnews.ca/news/12163344/pentagon-canada-defence/ - Canadian reporting and response context.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate primary Canadian budget-watchdog analysis and comparable readiness data. Identify which claims are descriptive versus advocacy for the F-35 purchase.

## Claim: The EU's 22nd sanctions package has taken effect

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Loose headline phrasing and present-tense descriptions of the Commission announcement.
- **Why it matters:** Treating a proposal as implemented overstates the immediate legal and economic effect.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Commission publicly announced specific restrictions on November 23 and filed formal proposal documents.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The Commission has proposal authority; the package requires unanimous Council adoption. The full legal annexes are not publicly accessible in the inspected register.
- **Independent corroboration:** yes that it is a proposal, not an adopted package.
- **Current assessment:** false if described as in force at cutoff; supported that the Commission proposed it.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/statement_26_2563/STATEMENT_26_2563_EN.pdf - proposal content.
  - https://apnews.com/article/ecad86868390f6602b346c70d1fa5757 - unanimity requirement.
  - https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/documents/public-register/latest/?page=3 - proposal records.
- **Questions for auditor:** Has any Council adoption occurred before cutoff? Which provisions are in the formal legal draft?
