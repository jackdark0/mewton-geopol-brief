## Candidate: A reported launch window turns Russia's orbital nuclear program into a near-term decision problem

- **Underlying development:** German public broadcasters and Sueddeutsche Zeitung report that intelligence shared among NATO states assesses a Russian nuclear-armed satellite could be launched within 60 days. The DIA's October public report had already assessed development of a satellite designed to carry a nuclear weapon, but did not publish this launch timeline.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The dramatic headline can obscure the important distinction between a public development assessment and an anonymously sourced readiness warning.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A credible near-term launch window forces choices on intelligence disclosure, launch detection, space resilience, alliance consultation, and deterrent signaling before the weapon is observable in orbit. Once launched, physical interception could create debris or escalation risks even if the device is not detonated.
- **What evidence is observable now:** DIA's public development assessment; the joint German investigation; NATO and German leaders taking the reporting seriously without publicly validating the intelligence; Russian denial.
- **What remains inference:** Sputnik-S's existence, warhead integration, storage location, launch readiness, political authorization, and intended employment.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.tagesschau.de/investigativ/ndr-wdr/satellit-russland-100.html - joint investigation and intelligence timeline.
  - https://www.dia.mil/Portals/110/Images/News/Military_Powers_Publications/Challenges_Security_Space_2026.pdf - public U.S. development assessment.
  - https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2026/11/23/joint-press-conference-by-the-secretary-general-with-the-federal-chancellor-of-germany - allied public response.
- **Questions for auditor:** Does any named official confirm the 60-day estimate? What indicators would distinguish launch preparation from standing capability?

## Candidate: The EU's 22nd package targets service networks and governance, not only assets

- **Underlying development:** The Commission's proposal would extend transaction bans and SWIFT removal to about 60 Russian banks, create a crypto-sanctions task force, reach third-country payment networks, prohibit European energy services including electricity-related services, add more than 70 shadow-fleet ships, and restrict European board service in Russia.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Sports-symbol provisions are visually salient, while the financing, service, and corporate-governance restrictions carry more strategic weight.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Service bans and payment-network rules can raise the transaction and repair costs of Russian energy and financial activity. Third-country measures could improve enforcement but also make unanimity harder and increase evasion incentives.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The Commission statement and formal Council-register proposals dated November 23.
- **What remains inference:** Final legal text, exemptions, member-state support, adoption timing, and actual enforcement effect.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/statement_26_2563/STATEMENT_26_2563_EN.pdf - primary package description.
  - https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/documents/public-register/latest/?page=3 - formal proposal records.
  - https://apnews.com/article/ecad86868390f6602b346c70d1fa5757 - unanimity requirement and independent summary.
- **Questions for auditor:** Which measures could survive a narrower unanimous compromise? Are the 60 banks all newly targeted or partly upgraded from prior restrictions?

## Candidate: Canada's F-35 review becomes a NORAD interoperability dispute

- **Underlying development:** The Pentagon linked Canada's fighter-procurement review and readiness shortfalls to its credibility as a North American defense partner, while a separate directive restricted some bilateral military engagement.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The dispute can be misread as another NATO spending quarrel; the NORAD and mixed-fleet interoperability implications are more specific.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A split fighter fleet can increase training, maintenance, weapons, and personnel burdens. Open U.S. pressure may also make Ottawa less willing to buy American systems, turning a readiness concern into an industrial and sovereignty contest.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Pentagon fact-sheet claims quoted by AP; Canada's budget path; the continued F-35 review; reassurance that joint operations and intelligence sharing are not expected to stop.
- **What remains inference:** Whether procurement changes would materially impair NORAD; accuracy and comparability of serviceability figures; whether pressure changes Ottawa's decision.
- **Initial priority:** high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/canada-us-defense-spending-hegseth-pentagon-2ce983323fc56d21db88db5401422fde - fact-sheet claims and Canadian context.
  - https://globalnews.ca/news/12163344/pentagon-canada-defence/ - Canadian reporting on policy consequences.
- **Questions for auditor:** Locate the public fact sheet. What exactly is restricted under the classified directive, and what remains operationally unchanged?

## Candidate: Ukraine's Benghazi operation pressures Russia's African logistics and Libya's internal balance

- **Underlying development:** Ukraine openly claimed a naval-drone operation against a Russian corvette and a cargo vessel off Benghazi, moving beyond the prior anonymous attribution.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The immediate ship damage is obvious; the use of divided Libya as a logistics and strike theater is the deeper change.
- **Strategic mechanism:** If the vessels supported Russian African operations, repeated maritime strikes could raise resupply and force-protection costs. Ukraine's use of or access to western Libya could also expose rival Libyan authorities to retaliation and bargaining pressure.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Zelenskyy's admission; imagery and reporting of severe corvette damage; Libyan and UN concern.
- **What remains inference:** Launch location, local sponsorship, cargo, operational effect on Russian African deployments, and whether the corvette is sunk.
- **Initial priority:** high.

## Candidate: FinCEN's Iran dataset exposes network geography but not an illicit-activity total

- **Underlying development:** FinCEN identified about $14.7 billion in activity potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking between January 2025 and August 2026, highlighting flows from China, Singapore, and the United Kingdom to UAE banks.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The headline number invites a false equivalence between suspicious activity and proven illicit proceeds.
- **Strategic mechanism:** The analysis can redirect compliance scrutiny toward financial centers and corporate registries, increasing friction for Iranian trade and sanctions evasion even without new designations.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Treasury's published time window, aggregate, route amounts, and industry categories.
- **What remains inference:** Proven Iranian control, illegality, unique-transaction count, overlap among reports, and relationship to actual oil or weapons revenue.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0698/ - primary trend-analysis summary.
- **Questions for auditor:** Inspect the underlying Financial Trend Analysis and its methodology; define the report count, duplication handling, and limits of SAR data.

## Candidate: Authorized U.S. drone collection inside Mexico tests a new security boundary

- **Underlying development:** Mexican officials acknowledged authorizing a U.S. surveillance drone over Mexican territory to help locate a wanted cartel leader before Mexican forces conducted the capture.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The arrest story can overshadow the precedent of an acknowledged foreign intelligence flight during an active domestic security operation.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Repeat use could deepen intelligence integration while preserving Mexico's ban on U.S. ground operations. It could also create pressure for more direct U.S. roles if violence persists.
- **What evidence is observable now:** Mexican authorization, three months of intelligence sharing, Mexican execution of the arrest, an outstanding U.S. extradition request, and subsequent lethal violence.
- **What remains inference:** Platform, collection authorities, command relationships, targeting role, and whether the model will be repeated.
- **Initial priority:** medium-high.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/mexico-sinaloa-drone-cartels-meza-flores-us-912af4eaae5e476a102adc2eca11abee - official disclosures and limitations.
- **Questions for auditor:** Did U.S. authorities or Mexico publish a legal instrument or operational protocol? Was intelligence limited to geolocation?

## Candidate: South Korea's false airspace alert is an escalation-control failure separate from the KADIZ entry

- **Underlying development:** Russian aircraft entered KADIZ and South Korea scrambled fighters, but civilian agencies briefly announced a territorial-airspace violation and shelter order before correction.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Coverage may collapse the military flight and warning-system error into one event.
- **Strategic mechanism:** A mistaken public description of territorial violation can increase political pressure and complicate military signaling during a fast-moving intercept. Reliable civil-military warnings are part of deterrence stability.
- **What evidence is observable now:** The three-minute shelter alert, Joint Chiefs correction, fighter scramble, and planned protest.
- **What remains inference:** Aircraft type, route, intent, and whether the error came from a training message or incomplete coordination.
- **Initial priority:** medium.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apnews.com/article/south-north-korea-tensions-airspace-ulleung-island-a0347d1dda863d8189bbdf21c320ee3b - official chronology and unresolved warning failure.
- **Questions for auditor:** Obtain South Korean warning texts and military track data; seek a Russian Defense Ministry account.
