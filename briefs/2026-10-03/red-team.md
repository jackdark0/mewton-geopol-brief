# Red-team review - 2026-10-03

## Finding: Yemen planning is not a launch decision

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “Saudi and Yemeni forces prepare a Bab el-Mandeb counteroffensive”; all language implying the campaign will begin on a known schedule.
- **Problem:** Reuters and Axios both rely on unnamed officials, possible US-source overlap is unresolved, and Reuters' timing range runs from within a week to after November 3. “Saudi leadership approved plans” in Axios is stronger than Reuters' description of options, but the underlying order is not public.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** No Saudi, Yemeni, Houthi, or US principal confirmed an operation order, participating force, or launch date. More than 100,000 is a contingent mobilization ceiling, not deployed strength.
- **Best competing interpretation:** Officials may be describing serious contingency planning while also shaping Houthi calculations, allied support, or negotiations; multiple plans may be at different readiness levels.
- **Required editorial action:** Use “reportedly preparing” or “active planning,” attribute all operational detail, state that the operation had not begun by cutoff, and separate planning confidence from execution confidence.

## Finding: Do not grant verified Houthi control of the strait

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Yemen story's references to “Houthi leverage at Bab el-Mandeb,” “control of the coast,” and any final headline saying the Houthis control the strait.
- **Problem:** Reuters and Axios describe Houthi gains and areas around the chokepoint, but the public evidence packet does not independently establish effective control of all traffic, islands, shores, or the navigational channel.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Control of adjacent coast, high ground, islands, and commercial navigation are separate atoms. The reports' strategic framing may exceed observable control evidence.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The Houthis possess enough nearby territory and strike capability to threaten shipping without exercising durable territorial or administrative control over the strait itself.
- **Required editorial action:** Write “areas around” or “leverage over” Bab el-Mandeb. Avoid unqualified “seized/controls the strait.”

## Finding: Camp David report adds salience, not confirmation of a decision

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Yemen analysis's use of the pre-cutoff Axios Camp David report.
- **Problem:** A second Axios story from three anonymous US officials says senior officials met, but the White House declined comment and no decision was disclosed. It may share the same administration source network as Axios' offensive report.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** A meeting is observable only through anonymous reporting; “things were decided or at least deeply discussed” is an unnamed official's ambiguous characterization.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The meeting was a broad contingency review prompted by multiple simultaneous crises and does not show movement toward either direct Yemen strikes or a renewed Iran campaign.
- **Required editorial action:** If retained, use it only as evidence that high-level review was reported, not as confirmation of an operational decision. One link is enough; do not count it as independent corroboration of the Saudi plan.

## Finding: The G7 headline volume has unresolved accounting

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “G7 directs a 100-million-barrel release” and any description of 100 million as entirely new supply.
- **Problem:** The G7 says the action takes account of commitments already fulfilled, while the IEA says 325 of March's 400 million barrels have been released. The statement does not reconcile 100 million with the 75-million unreleased balance.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** The final statement's exact wording prevents a clean “new 100 million” claim. A subtraction is not an allocation schedule.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The action may combine the remaining March commitment with an additional amount or may restate implementation in a new four-month program.
- **Required editorial action:** Say the G7 announced or set a 100-million-barrel implementation program, not necessarily 100 million wholly additional barrels. Make the ambiguity visible near the first number.

## Finding: Korean launch timing does not establish motive

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** “North Korea adds a ballistic launch to the DMZ mine crisis” and any causal link between the mine dispute and the launch.
- **Problem:** The launch occurred during the dispute, but pre-cutoff sources did not establish why it was ordered or what system was tested.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** North Korea conducts recurring missile tests; temporal proximity and rhetoric support context, not causation.
- **Best competing interpretation:** The launch belonged to an existing weapons-development schedule and was only secondarily useful as political signaling.
- **Required editorial action:** Use “amid” or “against the backdrop of,” not “in response to.” State that the exact missile type remained under analysis at cutoff.

## Finding: Japan's legal mechanism is a permission regime

- **Severity:** major
- **Targets:** Japan sanctions story.
- **Problem:** “Sanctions on 35 vessels” is acceptable shorthand only if the exact mechanism follows. “Asset freeze on 35 ships” would be inaccurate.
- **Evidence or reasoning:** Japan's Foreign Ministry applies permission requirements to specified services and related capital transactions, with a legacy-contract exception through October 31.
