## Method and provenance labels

Claims were kept atomic enough to separate an event from its alleged motive, an actor's affiliation from state direction, preparation from execution, and control of a node from control of a region. Each evidence packet contains the strongest support found, the strongest contrary or narrowing evidence found, and the source chain.

- **Primary-origin claim:** the page records what the named actor or institution asserted, but does not by itself prove the assertion.
- **Independent reporting:** a reporting organization obtained observations or sources beyond republishing the originating statement.
- **Derivative lineage:** multiple pages repeat the same statement, witness, document, wire report, or anonymous source pool; they count as one evidentiary line unless a separate origin is visible.
- **Observable evidence:** imagery, physical findings, contemporaneous witnesses, or other material that can test the proposition. Public descriptions of unreleased evidence are not the same as releasing it.

## Claim C-01: Iran directed or materially assisted the FlyDubai FZ1073 cockpit attack

- **Origin / principal promoters:** U.S. President Donald Trump publicly suggested an Iranian link; Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said investigators were testing whether the co-pilot had been sent by someone. The claim is distinct from the narrower proposition that the co-pilot may have adopted extremist views.
- **Why it matters:** State direction would turn a grave aviation-security incident into Iranian covert action against a civilian flight, creating a potential trigger for renewed U.S. strikes. Treating radicalization, Omani nationality, or an employment-vetting failure as proof of Iranian command would close a large inferential gap without evidence.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Trump said early information suggested a Tehran connection but provided no underlying intelligence. A person familiar with the investigation told AP that Oman had previously barred the co-pilot from flying over concerns about extremist views; this supports a possible prior security concern, not Iranian tasking. The UAE calls the incident a dangerous terrorist act and is investigating planning, motive, and direction.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** AP reported during the window that an official familiar with the investigation said there was **currently no evidence** linking Iran to the attack. No inspected source disclosed communications, payment, handler contact, travel, organizational membership, tasking, or a command chain. Emirati prosecutors remained in the evidence-gathering stage. Iran's capability and history of asymmetric action are context, not event-specific evidence.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for Iranian direction. **Partial** for a possible extremist-radicalization background: AP's anonymous source said Oman had barred the co-pilot, while the Wall Street Journal was identified as the first outlet to report that detail; those may share one official lineage.
- **Current assessment:** **Unsupported / speculative in the public record.** The cockpit attack is established; Iranian direction is not. The narrower radicalization proposition is **plausible but not publicly proven**.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that the public record did not support state attribution at cutoff; **low** on the ultimate attribution because the investigation is early and relevant intelligence may be non-public.
- **Provenance / source chain:** Trump and Netanyahu statements -> political attribution line. UAE investigation -> primary institutional process line. AP anonymous official -> separate investigative-status line. Oman-bar detail -> one anonymous-source line, with possible AP/WSJ overlap.
- **Sources:**
  - [Associated Press, October 2 — Iran asymmetric-war explainer](https://apnews.com/article/iran-asymmetric-warfare-sabotage-allegations-aaa62ff83ed516b04bbaa290e6a49a02) — reports that no confirmed Iran connection had emerged and that an official said there was then no evidence of an Iran link.
  - [Associated Press, October 2 — co-pilot had reportedly been barred in Oman](https://apnews.com/article/israel-saudi-flight-copilot-flydubai-repatriation-93d54fd230acb35d7112f47bba203459) — supports the alleged prior security concern, identifies the anonymous-source limitation, and again reports no current Iran evidence.
  - [UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, October 1 — investigation statement (older background)](https://www.mofa.gov.ae/en/MediaHub/News/2026/10/1/UAE-fly-dubai) — establishes that motive, terrorism, prior planning, and direction were questions under investigation, not findings.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Can the UAE Public Prosecution, Omani aviation/security authorities, or FlyDubai confirm the alleged Oman ban on the record?
  - Did Trump or Netanyahu rely on event-specific intelligence, or only an early hypothesis?
  - Is there any public evidence of an Iranian handler, financing, communications, or direction before cutoff?

## Claim C-02: Saudi Arabia approved and is preparing a Yemeni-ground-force offensive to retake the Bab el-Mandeb coast, with U.S. intelligence but no present U.S. combat role

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Reuters cited six Gulf, Yemeni, and Western sources familiar with preparations. Axios separately cited two U.S. officials. Neither Saudi Arabia, Yemen's internationally recognized government, the Houthis, nor U.S. Central Command confirmed the plan publicly by cutoff.
- **Why it matters:** A large coastal or multi-front offensive could reopen a major phase of Yemen's war, threaten shipping at Bab el-Mandeb, draw retaliation against Saudi energy infrastructure, and deepen U.S. operational involvement even without U.S. strikes.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Reuters reported that options included a coastal operation or synchronized fronts, that more than 100,000 Yemeni troops could be mobilized depending on scope, and that U.S. support already included advisers and targeting intelligence. Axios reported that Saudi leadership had approved plans, that Yemeni ground forces would receive Saudi air cover, and that a U.S. official said more than 200 Houthi targets had been provided in the previous two weeks. Existing Saudi/Yemeni air operations and earlier reported U.S. advisory support make the reported preparations operationally coherent.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The exact plan, force package, start date, rules of engagement, and approval document are not public. Reuters' six sources gave timing estimates ranging from within a week to after the November 3 U.S. election, which is evidence of unresolved timing or multiple contingencies. No inspected primary source announced mobilization or an operation order. Officials may be signaling or shaping negotiations as well as describing executable plans.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial-to-yes.** Reuters' pool spans Gulf and Yemeni officials and Western diplomats; Axios' pool consists of U.S. officials. Some Western/U.S. overlap is possible, but the distinct regional source categories and independently published operational details make this stronger than repetition of one leak.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported as preparation and active contingency planning; not yet supported as an irrevocable decision to launch on a fixed date.** U.S. intelligence/targeting support is supported by both current reporting and earlier independent reporting, while “no U.S. combat role” is a current policy description, not a durable guarantee.
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** Multiple source lines converge, but every load-bearing planning source is anonymous and the principals declined public confirmation.
- **Provenance / source chain:** Reuters -> six-source regional/Western pool. Axios -> two-U.S.-official pool. The outlets may share some U.S. sources, but Reuters also cites Gulf and Yemeni origins. Publicly observable current strikes corroborate escalation, not the future ground plan.
- **Sources:**
  - [Reuters via Business Recorder, October 2 — planned Saudi offensive](https://www.brecorder.com/live/iran-israel-war) — the inspected “Saudis plan assault” section reports six sources, two possible operational concepts, a contingent force of more than 100,000, U.S. intelligence support, and the absence of official comment.
  - [Axios, October 2 — Saudi/Yemeni counteroffensive and U.S. support](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/02/saudis-yemen-houthis-bab-al-mandeb-strait) — reports plan approval, Saudi air cover, a current U.S. refusal of direct strikes, and alleged provision of more than 200 targets.
  - [U.N. Security Council meeting transcript, September 2026 (older background)](https://transcripts.un.org/en/sc/10222) — establishes that a Yemeni-government counteroffensive and multi-front clashes already existed before this reported larger operation.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Can a Saudi or Yemeni mobilization order, logistics movement, unit concentration, or air-tasking change independently confirm execution rather than planning?
  - Are Reuters' Western sources and Axios' U.S. officials independent, or partly the same briefing chain?
  - Does “more than 100,000” describe available personnel, a maximum contingency, or forces actually assigned?

## Claim C-03: The Houthis carried out the September 29 attack on a Medina power station serving the Prophet's Mosque

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The Saudi-led coalition said an investigation and examination of drone wreckage established Houthi responsibility. A Houthi military source quoted by Houthi-run Saba denied the allegation and said it was cover for an earlier unsubstantiated Saudi allegation about Mecca.
- **Why it matters:** An intentional Houthi strike connected to one of Islam's holiest sites could mobilize religious and coalition support for Saudi escalation. The exact target also matters: damage to a distribution station that supplies the mosque is not automatically proof the mosque itself was the intended target.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The coalition reported a disabled transformer, no wider network disruption, and drone-wreckage findings pointing to Houthi planning and execution. Recent verified Houthi attacks on Saudi territory provide capability and operational context.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The inspected public record did not include wreckage photographs, serials, flight-path data, radar tracks, munition identification, an impact-site report, or a Houthi claim of responsibility. The coalition is an interested belligerent. The Houthi denial does not disprove responsibility, but it leaves the attribution contested. Even if the Houthis launched the drone, the evidence presented publicly does not establish that the Prophet's Mosque rather than a power node was the intended object.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No** for perpetrator or intent. Reuters independently reported the opposing statements but relied on the coalition for the forensic conclusion and on Saba for the denial.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible but unverified publicly** for Houthi responsibility. **Unsupported** for the stronger claim that the Prophet's Mosque itself was deliberately targeted.
- **Confidence:** **Low-to-medium** on responsibility; **medium** that target intent remained unestablished publicly.
- **Provenance / source chain:** Saudi coalition -> damage and wreckage-attribution line. Reuters and derivative republications -> reporting line, not new forensic evidence. Houthi-run Saba -> denial line.
- **Sources:**
  - [Reuters via Business Recorder, October 2 — Medina power-station attribution dispute](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40442267) — contains the coalition's wreckage claim, the reported damage, the Houthi denial, and the distinction between the station and mosque.
  - [Saudi Gazette, October 1 — coalition account (older primary-origin background)](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/665037/saudi-arabia/coalition-says-houthi-drone-targeted-power-station-supplying-prophets-mosque-in-madinah) — relays the coalition spokesperson's direct account and intended deterrent framing.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Will Saudi authorities publish the wreckage, radar track, munition type, or investigation method?
  - Is there independent imagery confirming the transformer damage and impact direction?
  - What evidence distinguishes an attack on a distribution facility from intent to attack the mosque it supplies?

## Claim C-04: Pro-government Tigray Peace Forces captured Mekelle's airport and had nearly eliminated TPLF territorial control in Tigray

- **Origin / principal promoters:** The pro-federal Tigray Peace Forces (TPF) claimed on social media that it had taken full control of Alula Aba Nega Airport near Mekelle. Its commander separately said the TPLF had effectively been wiped out and that pro-government forces were close to full control of Tigray.
- **Why it matters:** Control of Mekelle's airport and approaches would affect resupply, evacuation, air operations, and the federal government's ability to pressure the regional capital. “Near full control,” however, is a much broader proposition than holding an airport or one approach road.
- **Evidence offered for it:** Residents and a diplomat told Reuters that pro-government forces were approaching Mekelle; Reuters reported a TPF claim to have captured Milazat about 20 kilometers southeast of the capital. AFP reported a humanitarian source saying the TPLF had been pushed out of Afar, while ACLED said pro-government forces retook Abala, Erebti, and Megale. The Critical Threats Project assessed that the federal counteroffensive was advancing on three axes and had largely repelled the rebels' earlier offensive. TPLF leader Debretsion Gebremichael acknowledged that federal forces occupied parts of Tigray.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** AFP and Al Jazeera could not independently verify airport control because access and communications were restricted. No inspected geolocated imagery, airport operational notice, or neutral on-site report established who held the runway and terminal at cutoff. Al Jazeera's Ethiopia bureau chief said the TPLF still held several towns. Reuters reported a diplomat's assessment that senior TPLF leaders had left Mekelle but that conflict would likely continue even if the city fell. That directly narrows the maximal “wiped out” and “near full control” narrative.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial** for a rapid pro-government advance and reversal of TPLF gains; **no** for airport control or near-total regional control. Reuters, AFP, ACLED, and CTP supply distinct observation/analysis lines, but all face the same access blackout.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported core, materially overstated parent claim.** A substantial federal/TPF advance is supported. Airport control is **plausible but unverified**. Near-full control or destruction of the TPLF is **weak / unsupported**.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** on the advance; **low** on precise node control; **medium** that the maximal regional-control claim exceeded available evidence.
- **Provenance / source chain:** TPF social-media posts -> origin claim. Reuters residents/diplomat -> independent but anonymous local/diplomatic line. AFP humanitarian source -> separate anonymous line. ACLED/CTP -> specialist conflict-monitoring line. Al Jazeera reporter -> separate narrowing assessment.
- **Sources:**
  - [AFP, October 2 — airport claim and counteroffensive indicators](https://origin-zh9p7m-www.afp.com/en/ethiopian-pro-government-militia-claims-tigray-airport-regional-tensions-spike) — reports the airport claim, explains why it could not be verified, and gives ACLED/CTP and humanitarian-source evidence for narrower gains.
  - [Reuters via MarketScreener, October 2 — forces approaching Mekelle](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/ethiopian-forces-near-tigray-capital-fears-grow-of-wider-conflict-ce785ddad18bf524) — reports the Milazat claim, residents' observations, the TPLF leader's partial admission, and the diplomatic assessment that the conflict could persist after a city capture.
  - [Al Jazeera/Reuters, October 2 — near-full-control claim](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/ethiopian-govt-aligned-group-claims-close-to-full-control-of-tigray-region) — preserves the broad TPF claim and the contrary report that the TPLF still held several towns.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Can satellite imagery, geolocated video, flight notices, or independent witnesses confirm control of Alula Aba Nega Airport at the exact cutoff?
  - Which towns and transport nodes remained under TPLF control, and what denominator would make “near full control” meaningful?
  - Are ACLED and CTP using independent observations or the same TPF/government reports?

## Claim C-05: Seoul and Kyiv had a comprehensive nondisclosure agreement covering the North Korean POW transfer, and Kyiv breached it

- **Origin / principal promoters:** South Korean President Lee Jae Myung and senior Foreign Ministry officials say Ukraine requested secrecy, a bilateral agreement covered the entire matter, and written diplomatic records prove it. Ukrainian presidential communications adviser Dmytro Lytvyn denied that such an agreement existed.
- **Why it matters:** The dispute could affect South Korean assistance and defense-industrial cooperation with Ukraine, the safety of the two transferred soldiers and their families, and Seoul's attempted diplomacy with Pyongyang. It also illustrates why the existence, level, parties, and scope of an “agreement” must be treated as separate atoms.
- **Evidence offered for it:** On October 2, a senior South Korean Foreign Ministry official gave a detailed chronology: nondisclosure was a prerequisite, working-level consultations culminated in a foreign-minister meeting, Seoul warned by text before Zelenskyy's U.N. address not to breach confidentiality, and written records exist. Seoul and Kyiv were also discussing the text and tone of a possible Ukrainian announcement, including an apology. That negotiation is indirect evidence that a substantive diplomatic misunderstanding exists, though not proof of Seoul's full characterization.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** The alleged written records were not released. Ukraine's adviser gave a direct denial. Inspected reporting also showed a possible scope mismatch: one Ukrainian account denied an agreement, while another described no agreement at the presidential level. The public record does not establish whether there was a binding state-to-state commitment, a working-level understanding, a request, or different expectations about what details—not necessarily the transfer's existence—could be disclosed. No joint text or authenticated message was public by cutoff.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial** for the existence of extensive confidentiality discussions; **no** for the exact comprehensive agreement or its alleged breach. South Korean officials and media reports share one government-document lineage. The Ukrainian denial is an opposing-party line, not independent adjudication.
- **Current assessment:** **Plausible** that a confidentiality understanding existed; **unclear** whether it had the comprehensive scope Seoul asserts and whether Zelenskyy's disclosure breached an agreed term.
- **Confidence:** **Medium-low.** Seoul's detailed account and claimed records are stronger than a bare assertion, but the decisive documents remain withheld and the parties define the alleged agreement differently.
- **Provenance / source chain:** South Korean president/Foreign Ministry/diplomat -> one official documentary claim line. Korea Times and SBS -> reporting of that same government line. Ukrainian presidential adviser -> separate opposing primary-origin line. Ongoing minister-to-minister talks -> observable diplomatic process but not the underlying agreement.
- **Sources:**
  - [Korea Times, October 2 — Seoul claims written proof](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/foreignaffairs/20261002/seoul-says-written-proof-of-nk-pow-secrecy-deal-exists) — records the alleged written evidence, pre-speech text warning, asserted scope, and refusal to publish diplomatic documents.
  - [SBS, October 2 — Foreign Ministry chronology](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008780616) — reports that officials described confidentiality as comprehensive and says the two foreign ministers were negotiating an announcement and possible apology.
  - [Ukrainska Pravda, September 25 (older background)](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/09/25/8055035/) — provides the direct Ukrainian presidential adviser's denial that an agreement existed.
  - [Associated Press, October 2 — diplomatic dispute](https://apnews.com/article/south-korea-ukraine-north-pow-russia-war-330e1b3f1e2c40c274964c6939c03a10) — independently reports the live dispute and its implications but does not authenticate either party's documents.

  - Can either side publish a redacted text, authenticated message, agreed minute, or matching contemporaneous record?

## Claim C-06: North Korean anti-personnel mines caused the September 21 DMZ blasts and their placement south of the military line violated the armistice

- **Origin / principal promoters:** South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff announced the forensic assessment after joint site inspections with the U.S.-led United Nations Command (UNC). UNC then determined that an armistice violation occurred. Kim Yo Jong reiterated North Korea's denial during the research window and described Seoul's conclusion as fabricated.
- **Why it matters:** The finding bears on responsibility for injuries to three South Korean officers, possible intent behind North Korea's fortification work, and the risk that South Korean warning fire or countermeasures trigger armed retaliation.
- **Evidence offered for it:** South Korea reported TNT and brown residue on equipment, an absence of metal fragments associated with South Korean/allied mines, no record of South Korean or allied mine placement in the area, and a third suspected North Korean mine at the site. A follow-up joint inspection reportedly found an active DPRK anti-personnel mine on the ROK side of the Military Demarcation Line. UNC publicly classified the physical finding as an armistice violation.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** North Korea denies responsibility. The complete forensic report, photographs of identifying features, chain of custody, age of the mine, and mechanism by which it crossed or was placed south of the line were not in the inspected public record. A DPRK-manufactured mine south of the line is strong evidence of origin and violation, but it does not by itself establish when it was placed or whether the September 21 emplacement was a deliberate contemporary attack rather than an older or displaced mine.
- **Independent corroboration:** **Partial.** ROK and UNC are two institutions but conducted a joint investigation and therefore share one physical-evidence lineage. AP and NK News report that investigation; they did not conduct a separate forensic examination.
- **Current assessment:** **Supported** that DPRK-type mines caused the blasts and that an active DPRK mine was found south of the line. **Plausible but not established publicly** that North Korean personnel deliberately and recently placed them to injure patrols. UNC's armistice-violation determination is established as an institutional legal conclusion; its underlying legal/factual record is not fully public.
- **Confidence:** **Medium-high** on mine origin and location; **low-to-medium** on deliberate recent emplacement or intent.
- **Provenance / source chain:** ROK JCS + UNC joint physical investigation -> one main evidence lineage. UNC legal determination -> institutional conclusion from that same evidence. North Korean statement -> denial line. AP/NK News -> secondary reporting, not independent forensics.
- **Sources:**
  - [Associated Press, September 28 (older forensic background)](https://apnews.com/article/south-korea-border-mines-injured-3eb069b3d252d4451958de25893a74ea) — details residue, fragment, mine-record, scene, and location evidence from the preliminary joint inspection.
  - [Associated Press, September 30 (older investigation update)](https://apnews.com/article/south-korea-land-mines-kim-yo-jong-27210b8bafe9e11b6b456892b80ca881) — reports discovery of another North Korean mine and UNC's armistice finding, alongside North Korea's denial.
  - [NK News, September 30 (older specialist report)](https://www.nknews.org/2026/09/un-command-says-dmz-blast-involving-north-korean-mine-violated-armistice/) — records UNC's statement that an active DPRK anti-personnel mine was found on the ROK side of the line.
  - [Yonhap, October 2 — renewed North Korean denial](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261002008851315) — establishes that Kim Yo Jong reiterated the denial during the live window.
  - [Associated Press, October 2 — missile launch and current escalation](https://apnews.com/article/south-north-korea-projectile-fired-0b5b69e919f9c9a2358f3cd3346649e7) — links the continuing mine dispute to the subsequent missile launch and repeats the joint-inspection/armistice findings.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Can UNC or the ROK release imagery, model-identification features, emplacement age, and chain-of-custody details?
  - Does the armistice determination depend on deliberate emplacement, simple presence south of the line, or both?
  - Could flooding, soil movement, or legacy minefields plausibly explain the location, and did investigators test those alternatives?

## Claim C-07: Kandahar's governor hosted senior BLA, AQIS, and Hafiz Gul Bahadur figures on August 26 to plan increased attacks inside Pakistan

- **Origin / principal promoters:** Pakistani army spokesperson Lt. Gen. Ahmad Sharif made the allegation at a televised news conference on October 1. Kandahar Governor Mohammad Shirin Akhund's office denied it on October 2 and called it propaganda intended to justify Pakistani airstrikes.
- **Why it matters:** A governor knowingly hosting multiple militant organizations would be direct evidence of Taliban-state facilitation, strengthen Islamabad's claimed self-defense rationale for cross-border strikes, and weaken mediation. It is much stronger than the broader claim that militants find sanctuary somewhere in Afghanistan.
- **Evidence offered for it:** The Pakistani army spokesperson named the alleged date, place, and three militant organizations. Pakistan has documented a serious militant threat and attributes many attacks to groups it says operate from Afghan territory, but that background does not prove this specific meeting.
- **Evidence against it / missing evidence:** Sharif did not explain the intelligence basis or provide names of participants, communications, imagery, travel records, minutes, intercepted planning, or subsequent attacks tied to the alleged meeting. The governor's denial is interested and does not disprove it. No independent public source inspected during the run corroborated the gathering.
- **Independent corroboration:** **No.** AP independently reported both parties' words but found no evidence packet or separate confirmation.
- **Current assessment:** **Unsupported / speculative in the public record; not disproven.** The specificity raises a testable lead but does not meet the threshold for asserting state-hosted operational coordination.
- **Confidence:** **Medium** that the public record was insufficient; **low** on the ultimate truth because the alleged evidence is intelligence-sensitive.
- **Provenance / source chain:** Pakistani military spokesperson -> sole support line. Kandahar governor -> opposing denial line. AP -> independent reporting of the dispute, not an independent witness to the meeting.
- **Sources:**
  - [Associated Press, October 2 — Kandahar governor denial and Pakistani allegation](https://apnews.com/article/afghanistan-pakistan-airstrikes-fighting-militants-0477a65dd16745f5fc766a7b884d7f30) — gives the precise groups and date, notes that Pakistan offered no evidence, and records the governor's denial.
  - [Associated Press, October 1 (older background) — airstrikes and competing casualty claims](https://apnews.com/article/e212cf566f4cbac4f05f07289740d40e) — establishes the strike context, UNAMA's civilian minimum, and lack of independent verification for Pakistan's militant-casualty claim.
- **Questions for auditor:**
  - Can Pakistan provide a declassified intelligence summary, participant identities, intercept chronology, or links to specific attacks?
  - Did any named militant group acknowledge presence in Kandahar or issue material traceable to the alleged meeting?
  - Was the allegation part of the target justification for the October 1 strikes, or a separate strategic narrative?

## Claims most worth carrying into verification

1. **Saudi Arabia is preparing a Yemeni-ground-force offensive to retake Bab el-Mandeb with U.S. intelligence support.** Two reporting organizations and multiple source categories converge, but timing, force size, and final launch authority require observable confirmation.

## Auditor-wide cautions

- Treat the Reuters and Axios Yemen reports as partially independent until their anonymous-source overlap is tested; do not count eight quoted sources mechanically.
- For Ethiopia, demand node-by-node control evidence. Control of an approach town, airport, capital, and region are four different propositions.
- For FlyDubai, do not let the reported Omani security concern launder an Iran-attribution claim. Ideological radicalization, organizational membership, foreign encouragement, and state tasking are separate atoms.
- For Medina, separate launch attribution, object struck, intended target, and religious-site narrative.
- For the Korean mine case, distinguish physical origin/location from contemporary intent; treat the UNC armistice ruling as a legal/institutional finding, not independent forensic replication.
- No denominator-based percentages were calculated because this is a selected consequential-claim set, not a census of all public claims in the research window.
