## Major findings

### 1. “U.S. withdrawal from Iraq” overstates the verified event

**Challenge:** CENTCOM verified the end of the OIR mission in Iraq and the departure from Erbil, while also describing a continuing bilateral relationship and a Jordan-based coalition headquarters. A headline implying all U.S. troops left Iraq would exceed the source.

**Required action:** Use “ends OIR mission in Iraq” or “leaves Erbil.” State plainly that this is not verified as the end of every U.S. military presence or activity in Iraq.

### 2. The Russian letter is unpublished

**Challenge:** NATO confirms a letter and reply, but Rutte declined to disclose the exact text. The reported Kaliningrad trigger comes from secondary reporting. Treating the wording as an official quotation or a new Russian doctrine would collapse provenance.

**Required action:** Attribute the conditional warning to reporting, then pair it with NATO’s direct statement that there was no observed posture change or imminent threat. Do not place unverified language in quotation marks.

### 3. One Ukrainian energy strike does not prove a winter campaign

**Challenge:** A large attack focused on energy infrastructure is verified. Campaign duration, cumulative grid loss, and Russian strategic success are not. “Russia begins winter campaign” would turn an assessment into a fact.

**Required action:** Say the attack “raises” or “renews” the risk of a winter pressure campaign. Attribute casualty totals to Ukrainian officials and label them preliminary where useful.

### 4. An exchanged Iran response is not a deal

**Challenge:** Iran publicly says it received a U.S. response. The text is unavailable, Washington did not publicly confirm it by the cutoff, and the “sequencing is the main obstacle” account rests on one unnamed person briefed on the talks.

**Required action:** Keep “Iran says” in the headline or first sentence. Do not say the parties agreed on a framework, accepted the seven points, or are down to one remaining issue.

### 5. The DMZ evidence supports a narrower conclusion than intentional attack

**Challenge:** UNC’s violation finding rests on an active North Korean mine found south of the line. The public record does not establish that the mines which exploded were recently planted, how the intact mine arrived, or whether it targeted the patrol.

**Required action:** Separate three propositions: North Korean manufacture/provenance, location south of the line, and deliberate emplacement. Only the first two are supported strongly enough for declarative wording.

### 6. The Milwaukee Framework is not a common tariff regime

**Challenge:** Consensus on a framework is real, but its operative language leaves action to national law and future decisions. The 745-million-ton figure is a 2028 forecast, not a present measurement.

**Required action:** Use future-oriented verbs for remedies. Label the capacity figure as a forecast and identify implementation as the test.

### 7. Contested state-attribution claims need public-evidence labels

**Challenge:** The British prime minister’s Fairford attribution and political descriptions of the Flydubai incident are newsworthy, but neither public record establishes state direction or ideological motive. Repetition by senior officials is not independent confirmation.

**Required action:** Put both in the contested-claims or Rapid Fire sections with explicit “no public evidence yet” / “investigation open” language. Do not promote either to a major development on the current record.

## Minor findings

### A. Denominators in the Ukraine story

Site-level damage is not the same as system-wide capacity loss. Ask how much generation or transmission was lost, for how long, and against what national total.

### B. Iraq causality

Recent Iranian pressure may have affected political presentation or timing at the margin, but the transition was previously planned. Avoid a monocausal “Iran forced the exit” explanation.

### C. Sanctions mechanics

Ukraine’s planned list under the Graham Act is a request into a statutory process, not a self-executing sanctions package. Keep proposal, review, determination, and designation separate.

### D. Alaska LNG numbers

The $54 billion figure is a presidential claim disputed by the prospective investor’s government. It should never appear as a finalized capital commitment.
