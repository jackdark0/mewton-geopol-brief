## Candidate: DS-01 — Kyiv's warning failure exposes an intelligence dependency

- **Underlying development:** In his 13 October evening address, Zelenskyy said partners supplied no advance warning for the previous day's Kyiv ballistic attack; he said Ukrainian forces detected five missiles only seconds before impact. Separately, Sybiha said U.S. intelligence cooperation continued and attributed the late alert to a particular weapon type. These are distinct statements, not mutually exclusive measurements.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** The argument about an energy ceasefire can obscure the operational difference between continued intelligence cooperation in general and a useful launch warning for one attack.
- **Strategic mechanism:** If warning channels fail, defenders have less time to alert civilians and prepare interception. An unreliable dependency could alter Kyiv's bargaining position. This mechanism does not establish why this warning failed.
- **What evidence is observable now:** DS-01a: the presidential statement exists, dated 13 October 19:18 Kyiv (12:18 EDT). **Supported / high confidence** on what he said. DS-01b: the actual missing warning is a participant account, **supported as an attributed claim / moderate confidence**, without access to sensor or communications logs. DS-01c: Sybiha's continuation statement is reported by Ukrinform from the Warsaw forum. DS-01d: an inspected Reuters relay says three sources, including one meeting participant, denied that U.S. envoys threatened to cut support at the 9 October Miami meeting.
- **What remains inference:** A deliberate U.S. suspension, a political instruction to withhold this alert, a permanent sensor failure, and responsibility for particular casualties. The Reuters account narrows the alleged threat; it does not independently prove the operational status of every intelligence channel.
- **Initial priority:** high. Sorting dimensions S/E/U/N/D = 5/3/4/3/3; score 35. Uncertainty 3, recorded separately rather than subtracted.

## Candidate: DS-02 — OPEC's new report shows recovered crude output alongside transport strain

- **Underlying development:** October's OPEC report puts September DoC crude output at about 39.07 million barrels/day, up about 0.90 million from August. Its highlights simultaneously describe sharply higher tanker freight rates and tight Atlantic middle-distillate markets. Newly published backward-looking data challenge the simple assumption that more crude automatically resolves the current fuel and shipping bottleneck.
- **Why it may be under-covered:** Discussion of sanctions relief and additional diesel cargoes can conflate production, market supply, crude exports, refinery throughput, and delivered diesel.
- **Strategic mechanism:** Extra crude helps only if ports, tankers, routes, refineries, and product distribution can move it. A transport bottleneck can preserve energy coercion even as upstream production recovers. This is an assessment, not a measured attribution to one actor.
- **What evidence is observable now:** DS-02a: primary Table 5-7 reports September DoC crude at 39,068 thousand barrels/day versus August 38,165: +903 thousand. DS-02b: Saudi direct-communication footnotes give September production 8,372 versus August 6,238 thousand barrels/day; supply to market is a different series, 7,988 versus 7,122. DS-02c: the highlights report rising tanker rates and stronger U.S. Gulf Coast middle-distillate margins. **Supported / moderate confidence** as OPEC's reported measurements, with methodological and timing limits.
- **What remains inference:** Global supply normalization, a fall in delivered diesel prices, spare capacity actually available, and the immediate effectiveness of U.S.–Russian arrangements. OPEC's 2027 rebound is a forecast contingent on this year's softening and inventory replenishment, not an observed recovery.
- **Initial priority:** high, subject to version timing check. S/E/U/N/D = 5/3/4/3/3; score 35. Uncertainty 2.
